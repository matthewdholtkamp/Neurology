    Brain Death (Death by Neurologic Criteria)¶

    Last reviewed: October 2026

    One-liner: how to declare death by neurologic criteria (BD/DNC) in adults at the bedside — the prerequisites, examination, and apnea test that make the diagnosis, per the 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM consensus guideline. BD/DNC is a clinical diagnosis: permanent coma, brainstem areflexia, and apnea despite adequate hypercarbic/acidemic stimulation.

    🚩 Do not start — or do not continue — the evaluation if

    Pattern Why it stops you Do this instead
    Any arousal/awareness, any preserved brainstem reflex, any brain-mediated motor response, or any spontaneous breathing The exam has already failed — BD/DNC is excluded Stop; keep treating; re-evaluate later if the patient deteriorates
    No identified catastrophic mechanism, or neuroimaging inconsistent with the injury's severity Permanence cannot be established Do not proceed until the cause and its permanence are clear
    Core temp <36°C, SBP <100, MAP <75, uncorrected metabolic derangement, or residual sedation/blockade Confounders mimic BD/DNC Correct every confounder first (Step 1)
    Primary posterior-fossa injury without catastrophic supratentorial injury on imaging Brainstem findings may not reflect whole-brain loss Get imaging proving supratentorial catastrophe before evaluating

    Rapid approach¶

    1. Confirm the substrate: identified catastrophic, permanent brain injury, neuroimaging consistent with its mechanism and severity, sufficient observation time elapsed.
    2. Correct every confounder — temperature, blood pressure, metabolic, drugs, blockade.
    3. Examine once, fully (adults): coma + complete brainstem areflexia. A second independent exam is optional under the guideline; your hospital policy or state law may require more.
    4. Apnea test once after the final examination.
    5. Ancillary testing only if the exam or apnea test cannot be completed safely or fully.
    6. Document everything; the time of death is when the final qualifying component is complete.

    The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)¶

    Step 1 — prerequisites (all must be true before you touch the patient)¶

    Step 1 — prerequisites checklist

    • Identified catastrophic injury known to cause BD/DNC (e.g., massive ICH/SAH, large hemispheric infarct with herniation, severe TBI, post-anoxic injury) with neuroimaging consistent with the mechanism and severity.
    • Permanence established: sufficient observation for the injury's pathophysiology — ≥24 hours after acute hypoxic-ischemic brain injury; and if core temp was ever ≤35.5°C, wait ≥24 hours after rewarming to ≥36°C before evaluating (ancillary testing cannot bypass this wait).
    • Primary posterior-fossa injury: imaging must also show catastrophic supratentorial injury before you evaluate.
    • Do not proceed if there is any consciousness, any preserved brainstem reflex, brain-mediated movement, or spontaneous breathing — the evaluation is already over.

    Step 2 — correct confounders (the exam is invalid until these hold)¶

    Step 2 — physiologic and pharmacologic prerequisites

    • Temperature ≥36°C (see the rewarming wait above).
    • SBP ≥100 mm Hg and MAP ≥75 mm Hg, maintained.

    • Metabolic: correct severe metabolic, endocrine, acid-base, and electrolyte disturbance. The guideline supplement's consensus warning thresholds (not validated diagnostic cutoffs — treat values outside these as "stop and correct"):

      Laboratory value Potentially confounding range
      Sodium <130 or >160 mmol/L
      Potassium <3 or >6 mmol/L
      Glucose <70 or >300 mg/dL
      BUN >75 mg/dL
      Calcium <7 or >11 mg/dL
      Ionized calcium <1 or >1.3 mmol/L
      Magnesium <1.5 or >4 mg/dL
      Ammonia >75 μmol/L
      pH <7.3 or >7.5
      Free T4 ≤0.4 or >5 ng/dL

    Ammonia/thyroid testing is guided by clinical indication, not routine. - Drugs: exclude intoxication and CNS depressants. Blood alcohol ≤80 mg/dL; pentobarbital <5 μg/mL or below the lab's detection limit. If drug levels are unavailable, allow ≥5 half-lives — longer with renal/hepatic dysfunction, obesity, or prior hypothermia. - Blockade: exclude paralysis with train-of-four stimulation or demonstrable deep-tendon reflexes.

    Step 3 — the clinical examination (adults: at least one full exam)¶

    Step 3 — examination components (every one must be absent)

    • Consciousness: no response to visual, auditory, or tactile stimulation.
    • Motor: no brain-mediated response to noxious stimulation of face/head, trunk, and limbs. Spinal reflexes (e.g., triple flexion, Lazarus-like movements) may persist and do not exclude BD/DNC.
    • Pupils: bilaterally absent response to bright light.
    • Corneas: bilaterally absent corneal reflexes.
    • Oculocephalic: absent — omit if cervical-spine or skull-base integrity is uncertain.
    • Oculovestibular (cold calorics): bilaterally absent — still required even when the oculocephalic reflex is untestable. An untestable oculocephalic reflex alone does not force ancillary testing if bilateral oculovestibular testing is valid and absent.
    • Gag and cough: both absent with pharyngeal stimulation and deep tracheal (suction) stimulation.

    Step 4 — the apnea test (at least one, after the final examination)¶

    Step 4 — apnea test

    Beforehand: no hypoxemia, hypotension, or hypovolemia; cardiopulmonary risk must be acceptable. Baseline PaCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg and pH 7.35–7.45, unless chronically hypercarbic.

    Preparation: - Preoxygenate with 100% oxygen for ≥10 minutes, targeting PaO₂ >200 mm Hg. - Obtain a baseline ABG. Use an arterial catheter when possible.

    Conduct: stop mandatory ventilation, maintain apneic oxygenation (e.g., O₂ via suction catheter/ETT), and observe continuously for respiratory effort. Draw ABG after 8–10 minutes — earlier if the patient becomes unstable.

    Endpoint (all required): no respirations AND pH <7.30 AND PaCO₂ ≥60 mm Hg AND PaCO₂ ≥20 mm Hg above baseline.

    Chronic hypercarbia: use the known premorbid PaCO₂ as baseline. If unknown, estimate it — the same endpoints apply relative to the pretest PaCO₂.

    Abort the test (reconnect, stabilize, ABG before reconnection if feasible) for: - SBP <100 or MAP <75 mm Hg despite support, - any spontaneous breath, - progressive desaturation below 85%, or - arrhythmia causing hemodynamic instability.

    If endpoints are unmet, the test is incomplete — extend/repeat when safe, or move to ancillary testing.

    Step 5 — ancillary testing (only when the clinical evaluation cannot be completed)¶

    Ancillary testing is required only when an exam component is inaccessible/uninterpretable, apnea testing is unsafe, or a metabolic confounder cannot be corrected. Complete every assessable component first; all findings must still be consistent with BD/DNC. Ancillary testing cannot bypass hypothermia, the post-rewarming waiting period, or high sedative levels.

    Accepted (Level A) NOT accepted under this guideline
    4-vessel catheter angiography (no intracranial filling) EEG
    Radionuclide cerebral blood flow scan (e.g., HMPAO — "hollow skull") Evoked potentials (auditory, somatosensory)
    Transcranial Doppler — adults only (reverberating flow / systolic spikes) CTA, MRA, MRI

    Special situations¶

    • ECMO: apnea testing on ECMO is addressed in the guideline's published correction. For VA-ECMO, qualifying pH/PaCO₂ values are required from both the patient's distal arterial line and the postoxygenator circuit; the sampling site that best reflects the cerebral circulation depends on cannulation. For VV-ECMO, patient arterial sampling only. When in doubt, add an accepted ancillary blood-flow study.
    • Targeted temperature management: the 24-hour post-rewarming wait above applies to TTM and any other cause of hypothermia ≤35.5°C.
    • Severe facial trauma, cervical-spine injury, or otologic barriers: the affected component is untestable — complete everything else and use an accepted ancillary test rather than guessing at the missing reflex.

    Documentation¶

    Record the mechanism of injury, imaging findings, every prerequisite value (temp, BP, relevant labs, drug levels/washout), each examination component with its result, apnea-test ABGs (baseline and final with times), and any ancillary study. Note the date and time each component was completed — the legal time of death is when the determination is complete (final qualifying component), per your institutional policy and state law. Communicate the result to the family and the primary team directly, and notify the organ-procurement organization per local protocol.

    Military medicine — brain death

    BD/DNC is death. The frame below is casualty and benefits, not readiness — verify against current service casualty-affairs and LOD policy before counseling a family.

    • Deployability: not applicable — the service member is deceased. In theater, death determination follows the same clinical standard; coordinate with the chain of command and mortuary affairs.
    • Profile (DA Form 3349): not applicable.
    • EPTS / LOD: a line-of-duty determination still matters — it drives survivor benefits (death gratuity, SGLI, VA benefits). Document the mechanism and circumstances of the injury precisely; casualty reporting (DD Form 1300) runs through casualty affairs, not the medical chain.
    • Retention (AR 40-501 / DoDI 6130.03): not applicable.
    • MEB / IDES: not applicable — death ends any disability process. The family's benefits path is casualty affairs + VA, not the DES.
    • Aeromedical / special duty: not applicable. Note for the chart: organ-donor suitability screening and OPO notification are required per hospital policy regardless of duty status.

    📚 Discover — evidence brief¶

    Written from the primary guideline and its correction; source articles are a reading list, not copied.

    What the 2023 consensus guideline changed and why it matters:

    • One guideline for adults and children replaces the separate 2010 (adult) and 2011 (pediatric) AAN statements — the first unified BD/DNC standard from AAN, AAP, CNS, and SCCM.
    • Adults need one examination and one apnea test. A second independent exam is optional under the guideline (institutional policy and state law may still require more) — a deliberate balance between the conservative imperative and the cardiopulmonary risk of prolonged testing.
    • EEG is out as an ancillary test, along with evoked potentials, CTA, MRA, and MRI. The only accepted studies are 4-vessel angiography, radionuclide perfusion scanning, and (in adults) transcranial Doppler — all tests of cerebral blood flow, not electrical activity.
    • The rewarming wait is now explicit: core temp ≤35.5°C → wait ≥24 hours after reaching ≥36°C, and no ancillary test can shortcut it.
    • New ground is covered: apnea testing on ECMO (with a published correction on sampling), evaluation after targeted temperature management, primary infratentorial injury, and the supplement's consensus lab warning thresholds — explicitly not validated diagnostic cutoffs, so treat them as "stop and correct" flags.
    • Language matters: the guideline uses "permanent" (will not resume spontaneously; will not be restored by intervention) rather than "irreversible," and interprets the UDDA as loss of function of the brain as a whole, including the brainstem.

    Key sources: the 2023 consensus guideline and its 2024 correction; the companion Neurology Clinical Practice implementation guideline.
