    Myopathy¶

    Last reviewed: October 2026

    One-liner: the prescriptive approach to inflammatory myopathy in 2026 — which antibody changes the plan, the IMNM induction regimens that actually get used (anti-HMGCR vs anti-SRP), the dermatomyositis/polymyositis treatment ladder with the August 2026 brepocitinib (Lisraya) approval, myositis-associated ILD from chronic to rapidly progressive, and inclusion body myositis under the new 2024 ENMC framework. For the neurologist or rheumatologist seeing progressive weakness, a hot CK, or a myositis antibody panel that just resulted.

    🚩 Red flags — don't miss these

    Pattern Worry Do now
    Rapidly progressive ILD, especially anti-MDA5 DM — rising O₂ needs over days to weeks Death from respiratory failure; this moves in days, not months Inpatient, multidisciplinary, upfront combination immunosuppression — do not wait for the complete antibody panel
    Dysphagia with aspiration risk (coughing on swallows, prolonged mealtimes, weight loss) in any myopathy Aspiration pneumonia — the killer in IBM and severe IMNM/DM Same-visit swallow screen → videofluoroscopy; dietitian; consider early IVIG in inflammatory disease
    CK >5,000 IU/L with myoglobinuria (tea-colored urine) Rhabdomyolysis → acute kidney injury Aggressive IV fluids, monitor CK/creatinine/electrolytes, admit — this is not "just myositis"
    Statin-exposed patient with severe proximal weakness and CK in the thousands Anti-HMGCR IMNM — stopping the statin alone does not treat it Anti-HMGCR antibody, muscle biopsy, start immunosuppression (early IVIG for moderate–severe)
    Inflammatory muscle biopsy labeled "polymyositis" in a patient over 45 with finger-flexor/quadriceps weakness IBM misdiagnosed as polymyositis — immunosuppression will not help and may harm Reconcile biopsy with the clinical pattern (2024 ENMC framework); only 43% of IBM biopsies show all three classic findings
    Systemic-sclerosis overlap about to get high-dose steroids for myositis Scleroderma renal crisis precipitated by steroids Rethink the steroid dose; involve rheumatology — the routine high-dose myositis approach does not apply

    Diagnosis / classification¶

    Weakness is a pattern-recognition problem. Proximal symmetric weakness + elevated CK + myopathic EMG + muscle edema on MRI + inflammatory biopsy is the inflammatory myopathy pattern — but the antibody result re-sorts the patient into diseases with different organs at risk and different first moves.

    The entities on this page:

    Entity Pattern clue Organ risks that change management
    Dermatomyositis (DM) Heliotrope rash, Gottron papules/sign, shawl/V-sign; proximal weakness ILD (esp. MDA5), malignancy (esp. TIF1-γ, NXP2), dysphagia
    Antisynthetase syndrome Myositis + ILD + arthritis + Raynaud + mechanic's hands + fever Chronic or rapidly progressive ILD
    Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM) Severe proximal weakness, CK often >10× ULN, pauci-inflammatory necrotizing biopsy Anti-HMGCR (statin-associated), anti-SRP (severe, bulbar/respiratory)
    Polymyositis (PM) A diagnosis of exclusion Before escalating "refractory PM," reconsider antisynthetase syndrome, IMNM, IBM, and noninflammatory mimics
    Inclusion body myositis (IBM) Age ≥45, ≥12 months of slowly progressive finger-flexor + quadriceps weakness, dysphagia No effective immunotherapy — exercise and supportive care are the treatment

    The myositis antibody panel — what changes the plan¶

    Order an expanded myositis-specific/myositis-associated antibody panel, but confirm anti-HMGCR is included or order it separately — some panels omit it. Include Ro52, PM/Scl, Ku, and U1-RNP for overlap disease.

    Antibody Phenotype Risk that changes management
    Anti-MDA5 Amyopathic/hypomyopathic DM Rapidly progressive ILD — highest-risk serology; pulmonary assessment in parallel, not after send-outs return
    Anti-TIF1-γ DM Malignancy — strongest cancer association; full 2023 IMACS clinical-serologic cancer screening
    Anti-NXP2 DM Malignancy — strong cancer association
    Antisynthetase (Jo-1, PL-7, PL-12, EJ, KS) Myositis ± ILD, arthritis, Raynaud High ILD burden, including lung-dominant non–Jo-1 phenotypes
    Anti-OJ Antisynthetase Notable line-blot blind spot — reference-lab immunoprecipitation may be needed
    Anti-HMGCR IMNM, often statin-exposed Early IVIG for moderate–severe disease
    Anti-SRP IMNM, often severe Early rituximab; IVIG bridging for bulbar/respiratory disease
    Anti-Mi-2 Classic DM More treatment-responsive phenotype
    Anti-SAE DM Prominent dysphagia/skin disease
    Anti-cN1A (NT5c1A) IBM Supportive only — ~30–76% sensitive, >90–95% specific vs muscle disease, but positive in Sjögren (~36%), SLE (~20%), DM, IMNM; negative does not exclude IBM

    Assay cautions: do not rely on a commercial line blot alone when the phenotype and the result disagree. For high-risk serologies (MDA5, TIF1-γ), pulmonary and cancer assessment proceed in parallel with send-out testing.

    Rapid approach¶

    1. Sort emergent from routine on the first contact — respiratory symptoms, dysphagia with aspiration, or myoglobinuria are admit-now problems; a slowly progressive proximal weakness with a CK of 800 is a workup-in-clinic problem. Rapidly progressive ILD gets combination immunosuppression in days (below), not a months-long ladder.
    2. Establish the inflammatory-myopathy pattern — CK, aldolase, myopathic EMG (fibrillations, small polyphasic units), muscle MRI (edema vs fatty replacement), and muscle biopsy with immunostaining. Biopsy distinguishes DM (perifascicular atrophy), IMNM (necrosis, sparse inflammation), and IBM (rimmed vacuoles, protein aggregates — but see the 2024 ENMC update below).
    3. Send the antibody panel and risk-stratify by result — MDA5 → lungs now; TIF1-γ/NXP2 → cancer screening now; HMGCR/SRP → IMNM induction now; cN1A → supports IBM but never decides it.
    4. Treat the phenotype, not the CK — CK improvement alone is not remission; persistent weakness may be damage, steroid myopathy, or deconditioning. Reassess the diagnosis (MRI, repeat biopsy review) before escalating immunosuppression for a CK that will not normalize.

    The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)¶

    1. Confirm pattern + exclude mimics — exam, CK, EMG/NCS, muscle MRI, biopsy; TSH, statin/drug review, and (once) the expanded antibody panel.
    2. Phenotype by antibody and organ involvement — lung (PFTs + HRCT for MDA5, antisynthetase, or any dyspnea), cancer screen (TIF1-γ/NXP2 per IMACS), swallow screen (dysphagia in any myopathy).
    3. Start the disease-specific induction below — IMNM gets early combination; DM/PM gets steroids + an early steroid-sparing agent; IBM gets exercise and supportive care, not immunosuppression.
    4. Reassess on a clock, not on hope — no convincing improvement by ~4–6 weeks on adequate therapy means reconsider the diagnosis and optimize combination treatment, not more months of the same.
    5. Run the monitoring and prophylaxis ordersets in parallel — PJP prophylaxis, HBV screening before rituximab, vaccine review, bone health, and rehab from day one.

    Workup¶

    Myopathy workup — order as a set

    • CK, aldolase, AST/ALT (muscle source — check GGT when assessing liver), creatinine, TSH, ESR/CRP.
    • Expanded myositis antibody panel — confirm anti-HMGCR included; add Ro52, PM/Scl, Ku, U1-RNP. If phenotype–result mismatch, send reference-lab immunoprecipitation (OJ and other line-blot blind spots).
    • EMG/NCS — myopathic pattern; in IBM expect mixed short-duration and large long-duration units (can mimic neurogenic disease — do not mislabel).
    • Muscle MRI — edema (active inflammation) vs fatty replacement (damage); in IBM, distal quadriceps "melted" appearance, anterior > posterior thigh; guides biopsy site.
    • Muscle biopsy with immunostaining — DM: perifascicular atrophy, MAC deposition; IMNM: necrosis with sparse inflammation, MAC on non-necrotic fibers; IBM: endomysial CD8⁺ invasion, rimmed vacuoles, p62/TDP-43 aggregates (but only 43% show all three classic findings — see 2024 ENMC).
    • Lung: PFTs + HRCT chest for anti-MDA5, antisynthetase antibodies, or any dyspnea/cough — do not wait for the antibody panel if RP-ILD is suspected.
    • Cancer screening for adult DM (TIF1-γ/NXP2 highest risk) per the 2023 IMACS clinical-serologic framework — age- and symptom-appropriate screening at minimum.
    • Swallow screen in every myopathy patient with bulbar symptoms → videofluoroscopy/FEES if abnormal.

    Avoid

    • Do not label an inflammatory biopsy "polymyositis" without reconciling it with the clinical pattern. The classic IBM trap — immunosuppression will not help and steroids may worsen weakness.
    • Do not chase the CK. Escalating immunosuppression for a persistently elevated CK without active weakness risks toxicity without benefit — distinguish active myonecrosis from fatty replacement, steroid myopathy, and deconditioning first.
    • Do not use methotrexate as an ILD treatment. With active ILD, mycophenolate, tacrolimus, or rituximab is the choice; methotrexate treats muscle/skin, not lung.

    Immune-mediated necrotizing myopathy (IMNM)¶

    IMNM is the myopathy where early combination therapy beats prolonged steroid monotherapy. Anti-HMGCR (often statin-exposed — stopping the statin does not treat the disease) and anti-SRP (often severe, bulbar/respiratory involvement) drive different first moves.

    Step 1 — IMNM induction (start here)

    • Prednisone 1 mg/kg/day PO (or methylprednisolone 500–1,000 mg IV daily ×3 days for severe/rapidly progressive weakness, significant dysphagia, or respiratory-muscle involvement, then ~1 mg/kg/day PO).
    • Do not linger on steroid monotherapy — the second agent starts with induction, not after steroids fail. Reassess every 2–4 weeks; no convincing improvement by ~4–6 weeks means reconsider the diagnosis and optimize combination treatment.

    Step 2a — anti-HMGCR IMNM (moderate–severe: IVIG early)

    • IVIG 2 g/kg per cycle, divided over 2–5 days (e.g. 1 g/kg/day ×2 days or 0.4 g/kg/day ×5 days), every 4 weeks — prioritize early for moderate–severe disease, alongside steroids.
    • Add a conventional agent for maintenance: methotrexate 15 mg PO/SC weekly (titrate to 20–25 mg weekly) + folic acid 1 mg PO daily, or mycophenolate mofetil 500 mg PO BID (titrate to 1,000–1,500 mg BID), or azathioprine 50 mg PO daily (titrate toward 2–3 mg/kg/day; check TPMT/NUDT15 before full dose).
    • After ~2–3 monthly IVIG cycles plus a conventional agent with still-active disease, consider rituximab 1,000 mg IV ×2, two weeks apart (or 375 mg/m² weekly ×4).

    Step 2b — anti-SRP IMNM (severe: rituximab early)

    • Rituximab 1,000 mg IV ×2, two weeks apart (or 375 mg/m² weekly ×4) — favored early in severe anti-SRP disease.
    • IVIG 2 g/kg per cycle (as above) for rapid bridging when bulbar or respiratory disease cannot wait for rituximab's 8–12-week onset.
    • Steroid backbone as in Step 1; assess emerging rituximab response around 8–12 weeks while bridging.

    IMNM monitoring and prophylaxis

    • PJP prophylaxis when prednisone ≥20 mg/day is expected for ≥4 weeks, especially with a second immunosuppressant: TMP-SMX 80/400 mg PO daily or 160/800 mg PO three times weekly (renal adjustment; avoid casually combining with methotrexate — marrow toxicity) or atovaquone 1,500 mg PO daily with food.
    • HBV screening (HBsAg, anti-HBc) before rituximab; prophylaxis/specialist management if indicated; baseline immunoglobulins, CBC.
    • Conventional-agent labs: CBC with differential, AST/ALT, creatinine at baseline and ~every 2–4 weeks during titration, then every 8–12 weeks when stable.
    • IVIG: renal function, volume tolerance, thrombotic and hemolysis risk each cycle.

    Dermatomyositis / polymyositis — the treatment ladder¶

    Glucocorticoids plus an early steroid-sparing agent, with IVIG introduced early for severe muscle or bulbar disease — not after multiple drug failures. Most sequencing recommendations remain low-certainty evidence and expert consensus. "Polymyositis" is a diagnosis of exclusion (see above).

    2026 update — brepocitinib (Lisraya) FDA-approved for adult DM

    Brepocitinib (Lisraya) received FDA approval on August 27, 2026, for adult dermatomyositis at 30 mg PO once daily — the first new oral DM therapy in this space. Phase 3 (n=241): mean week-52 Total Improvement Score 46.5 vs 31.2 placebo; skin, functional, and steroid-sparing outcomes favored 30 mg; serious infections ~10% vs 1%. Do not combine with another JAK/TYK2 inhibitor or a biologic DMARD such as rituximab (label recommends against). The 15-mg dose is not an approved maintenance dose. Boxed warning: serious infection, malignancy, major cardiovascular events, thrombosis, mortality. Do not initiate (or interrupt) with ANC <1,000/mm³, ALC <500/mm³, or hemoglobin <8 g/dL; not recommended with eGFR <30 or Child-Pugh C; include GGT (AST/ALT may be muscle); lipids ~12 weeks after initiation. Its place relative to methotrexate, mycophenolate, IVIG, and rituximab is not yet established by head-to-head trials — and the indication does not extend to polymyositis or rapidly progressive ILD. Verify at the TRICARE Formulary Search before prescribing — new approvals lag formulary updates.

    Step 1 — DM/PM induction (muscle-dominant disease)

    • Prednisone 0.5–1 mg/kg PO daily (usually 40–60 mg/day; higher end for substantial weakness or systemic disease). Maintain the initial dose until a clear response (~4–6 weeks), then taper. Severe/rapid disease: methylprednisolone 500–1,000 mg IV daily ×3 days → ~1 mg/kg/day PO.
    • Plus one steroid-sparing agent from day one:
      • Methotrexate 10–15 mg PO/SC once weekly → +2.5–5 mg every 2–4 weeks toward 20–25 mg weekly + folic acid 1 mg PO daily (good for muscle and skin; weekly — not daily; not an ILD treatment), or
      • Azathioprine 50 mg PO daily → toward 2–3 mg/kg/day (TPMT/NUDT15 first; slower onset), or
      • Mycophenolate mofetil 500 mg PO BID → 1,000–1,500 mg BID over 2–4 weeks (target 2–3 g/day; preferred with cutaneous disease or any ILD).
    • Measure: MMT-8, reproducible functional tasks, CDASI for skin, CK — CK alone is not remission, and persistent weakness may be damage.

    Step 2 — when to add IVIG (escalate on a clock)

    • Escalate at presentation with aspiration-risk dysphagia, respiratory-muscle weakness, rapidly declining ambulation, or organ-threatening disease: hospital-level assessment, IV glucocorticoids, and early IVIG alongside the longer-acting strategy — do not require several oral-agent failures first.
    • ~4–6 weeks: no objective improvement or continued deterioration despite adequate glucocorticoids → confirm active disease, adherence, and diagnosis; add IVIG rather than waiting months for a slow oral agent.
    • ~8–12 weeks of an optimized conventional agent with persistent activity → optimize/switch the conventional agent or add IVIG/rituximab; in DM, consider brepocitinib (above).
    • IVIG regimen: 2 g/kg per cycle over 2–5 days (1 g/kg/day ×2 days or 0.4 g/kg/day ×5 days), every 4 weeks — 2 g/kg total per cycle, not per day. Assess over ~3–4 cycles (12–16 weeks) in stable disease; do not wait that long if deteriorating. Octagam 10% carries the US adult-DM indication (labeled max rate 0.04 mL/kg/min — do not generalize to other products); PM use is off-label with weaker evidence. ProDERM: 79% IVIG vs 44% placebo achieved ≥minimal improvement at week 16.

    Step 3 — refractory disease (by phenotype, not a mandatory sequence)

    • Rituximab 1,000 mg IV on days 1 and 15 — guideline-supported refractory option; response more likely with anti-Jo-1, anti-Mi-2, anti-SRP or antisynthetase syndrome. Caveat: the RIM trial did not meet its randomized primary endpoint — do not present it as placebo-controlled proof of efficacy.
    • Tacrolimus ~0.075 mg/kg/day PO divided BID, trough 5–10 ng/mL (ACR/CHEST framework) — useful with ILD; monitor trough, creatinine, K⁺/Mg²⁺, BP (1–2× weekly the first month).
    • Cyclophosphamide 500–750 mg/m² IV every 4 weeks — for severe/refractory disease; CBC, creatinine, urinalysis before; CBC ~10–14 days post-dose; fertility, bladder toxicity, cumulative exposure counseling.
    • Tofacitinib 5 mg PO BID (off-label) — specialist option, particularly with ILD; TB/hepatitis screening, lipids, CV/VTE/malignancy assessment.
    • Abatacept 125 mg SC weekly — the 2025 phase 3 failed its primary (56.0% vs 42.5%, P=0.083); selective use only, not ahead of IVIG/rituximab.
    • Skin-predominant DM: photoprotection + topical corticosteroids/calcineurin inhibitors; hydroxychloroquine 200–400 mg/day (retinal screening) for cutaneous disease — it is not a treatment for muscle inflammation or ILD. Refractory skin disease: methotrexate/mycophenolate, IVIG, or brepocitinib.

    Steroid taper and maintenance (practice-based)

    • Begin tapering once strength/function and organ activity are clearly improving (~4–6 weeks): >20 mg/day: −5–10 mg every 1–2 weeks; 20→10 mg/day: −2.5 mg every 2–4 weeks; <10 mg/day: −1 mg every 2–4 weeks. Pause for documented relapse; distinguish relapse from withdrawal or adrenal insufficiency.
    • Continue the conventional agent dose that achieved control; consider cautious reduction only after ~12–24 months of sustained inactive disease, ideally off steroids. After control on IVIG: 1 g/kg every 4 weeks or lengthen toward 6–8 weeks — one change at a time. Rituximab: repeat ~every 24 weeks when needed, individualized. Post-cyclophosphamide: transition to mycophenolate or azathioprine.

    Myositis-associated interstitial lung disease¶

    ILD is a separate treatment pathway, titrated to lung severity — not a footnote to the muscle ladder. The two avoidable errors: treating irreversible weakness as persistent inflammation, and applying a months-long muscle schedule to rapidly worsening ILD. The 2023 ACR/CHEST SARD-ILD guideline and the 2026 ERS/EULAR CTD-ILD guideline both support immunosuppression and combination treatment including glucocorticoids for IIM-ILD (very-low-certainty evidence for much of it).

    Myositis-ILD, clinically active but not rapidly progressive

    • Prednisone ~0.5–1 mg/kg/day PO as a short-term bridge plus mycophenolate mofetil 1–1.5 g PO BID — generally the preferred first-line ILD immunosuppressant in ACR/CHEST guidance.
    • Alternatives by phenotype/tolerability: azathioprine 2–3 mg/kg/day, tacrolimus ~0.075 mg/kg/day divided BID (trough 5–10 ng/mL), rituximab 1,000 mg IV days 1 and 15, or cyclophosphamide 500–750 mg/m² IV q4w.
    • Systemic-sclerosis overlap: high-dose steroids may precipitate renal crisis — do not apply the routine high-dose myositis approach.
    • Escalate on confirmed progression at any time: new/rising O₂ requirement, worsening dyspnea or exertional desaturation, increasing ILD on HRCT, or reproducible PFT decline. First exclude infection, aspiration, PE, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, anemia, and respiratory-muscle weakness. Progressive pulmonary fibrosis criteria: 2 of 3 domains worsening within a year without another explanation.
    • If ILD progresses on adequately dosed mycophenolate: add or switch to rituximab, cyclophosphamide, or tacrolimus (more aggressive disease); tofacitinib 5 mg PO BID is a specialist option. IVIG 2 g/kg over 2–5 days may be added when severe muscle/bulbar disease coexists or infection concerns limit further immunosuppression. Prolonged high-dose glucocorticoids are not the strategy for ongoing progression.
    • RECITAL (severe/progressive CTD-ILD including myositis): rituximab 1 g at weeks 0 and 2 vs cyclophosphamide 600 mg/m² q4w ×6 — both improved FVC; rituximab not superior, but supported as an alternative.
    • Progressive fibrotic ILD despite management: nintedanib 150 mg PO BID with food (reduce to 100 mg BID for intolerance) — slows fibrotic progression; it does not replace immunosuppression for active disease and is not treatment for acute RP-ILD. LFTs monthly ×3, then ~every 3 months; watch diarrhea, weight loss, bleeding risk.
    • Monitor: PFTs every 3–6 months early, every 6–12 months when stable; repeat HRCT at ~3–6 months in IIM-ILD (2026 ERS/EULAR), earlier if progression is suspected.

    Rapidly progressive ILD — especially anti-MDA5 (inpatient emergency)

    • This is a multidisciplinary inpatient emergency. Progression from baseline O₂ to high-flow or ventilation over days to weeks triggers urgent treatment and transplant-center consideration — do not wait for the complete antibody panel when the phenotype suggests myositis-associated RP-ILD.
    • Methylprednisolone 500–1,000 mg IV daily ×3 days, then prednisone ~1 mg/kg/day PO, individualized to response and complications.
    • Add two additional agents for confirmed or suspected anti-MDA5 RP-ILD (one or two for other RP-ILD phenotypes) — "triple therapy" means glucocorticoid plus two agents:
      • Rituximab 1,000 mg IV days 1 and 15, and/or
      • Tacrolimus ~0.075 mg/kg/day divided BID (trough 5–10 ng/mL), and/or
      • Cyclophosphamide 500–750 mg/m² IV q4w (intensified anti-MDA5 schedules need specialist protocols), and/or
      • IVIG 2 g/kg over 2–5 days (severe muscle/bulbar disease or infection concerns).
    • Reasonable expert-center combinations: steroid + rituximab + tacrolimus, or classical steroid + tacrolimus + cyclophosphamide. ACR/CHEST conditionally favored rituximab over cyclophosphamide for anti-MDA5 RP-ILD — but no single combination is established as universally superior, and a 2025 multicenter cohort did not reproduce the historical Japanese triple-therapy survival benefit (higher mortality in the triple group). Treat true RP-ILD with early combination and individualized selection — not automatic cyclophosphamide triple for every MDA5-positive patient.
    • JAK inhibitors: tofacitinib 5 mg BID is a legitimate specialist option (a 515-patient 2025 cohort linked it to better 1-year transplant-free survival than CNIs, clearest in less severe/non-RP disease) — not established standalone rescue for fulminant RP-ILD. A 2026 matched JAKi-plus-CNI study showed no overall survival advantage; do not stack immunosuppressants indiscriminately.
    • Reassess same-day for rising O₂/ventilator needs or worsening imaging: disease activity vs infection, drug exposure, and whether to change the combination. Plasma exchange is salvage for refractory disease, not routine first-line — schedule it around rituximab/IVIG (it removes them). ECMO is an individualized bridge, usually tied to transplant planning.

    Inclusion body myositis (IBM)¶

    As of October 2026, IBM remains a clinical–pathological diagnosis without an approved, established disease-modifying drug. The major diagnostic update is the 2024 ENMC framework, which formally incorporates muscle imaging and anti-cN1A antibodies. Treatment is rehabilitation, independence, and swallowing/respiratory management — not the immunosuppressive ladder used above.

    IBM diagnosis — the 2024 ENMC framework

    • Common presentation: onset ≥45 years, progressive weakness for ≥12 months, CK ≤15× ULN, and deep finger-flexor and/or knee-extensor weakness. Uncommon presentations (younger, shorter duration, higher CK, atypical distribution) need stronger corroboration, not automatic exclusion.
    • Mandatory investigation: muscle biopsy showing necrotic fibers with endomysial lymphocytes surrounding non-necrotic fibers, with or without invasion. Rimmed vacuoles are not universally required — vacuoles and protein aggregates are one supportive category, not two.
    • Four supportive findings: (1) anti-cN1A; (2) rimmed vacuoles and/or cytoplasmic protein aggregates (p62/TDP-43); (3) mitochondrial abnormalities exceeding age expectations; (4) typical MRI and/or muscle-ultrasound pattern. Common presentation with both characteristic groups → biopsy finding alone; one group → biopsy + ≥1 supportive; uncommon → biopsy + ≥2 supportive. Imaging and antibodies do not replace biopsy.
    • Practical traps: a nondiagnostic biopsy does not settle it — sampling fatty-replaced muscle is uninformative; reconsider muscle selection, get expert review, repeat if it changes management or trial eligibility. EMG can mimic neurogenic disease. Atypical or rapid course → reconsider mimics (other IIM, muscular dystrophies, myofibrillar/multisystem proteinopathies, neurogenic disease).
    • Anti-cN1A use: supportive, not a verdict — negative does not exclude, positive does not establish. Most useful when an additional supportive finding resolves an incomplete picture. Confirm the panel actually includes cN1A; assay performance varies (ELISA ~30–50% sensitive). Do not use serial titers to monitor — follow function, swallowing, nutrition, and respiratory performance.

    IBM — what not to do

    • Do not treat IBM with chronic immunosuppression. Steroids, methotrexate (failed to preserve strength/function in an RCT despite lowering CK), azathioprine, and other conventional agents have no convincing sustained benefit — and steroids may worsen weakness. Do not escalate merely because CK stays up or the biopsy is inflammatory.
    • Do not promise IVIG for limb weakness. No established disease-modifying effect; small studies suggest possible short-term swallowing benefit in selected patients — insufficient for routine treatment or aspiration-prevention claims. A carefully defined, time-limited trial for substantial objectively documented dysphagia may be considered by an IBM specialist (specify the functional outcome that justifies continuing; subjective improvement alone is not a reason for indefinite IVIG).
    • Failed/investigational agents to know by name: bimagrumab (RESILIENT, 251 patients, failed 6-minute walk), arimoclomol (failed IBMFRS over 20 months), ulviprubart/ABC008 (272-patient MUSCLE phase 2/3 failed IBMFRS at week 76 — investigational), sirolimus (44-patient trial missed primary knee-extension endpoint; secondary signals only), pioglitazone (2026 single-arm, no clinical improvement). Refer to a neuromuscular center for trial eligibility — not for unproven off-label combinations.

    IBM — exercise and rehabilitation (the actual treatment)

    • Individualized exercise is generally safe and the only intervention consistently showing benefit — it improves conditioning and preserves selected muscle performance, but has not been proven to arrest IBM or change its natural history.
    • Starting framework (adapt with a neuromuscular physical therapist): aerobic ~3 days/week — start 10–15 minutes, build toward 20–30 minutes, recumbent cycling, conversational moderate effort; resistance ~2 nonconsecutive days/week — light-to-moderate, individualized repetitions, focus on residual strength, avoid failure, assisted/gravity-reduced for very weak muscles; range of motion + safe transfer practice, task adaptation, balance training. Increase one variable at a time; back off for prolonged pain, excessive fatigue, or worse next-day function. A transient post-exercise CK rise without strength loss does not require stopping.
    • Target what limits independence: knee buckling, unsafe chair/toilet transfers, foot drop, grip weakness, fatigue — walking aids/orthoses, raised seating, bathroom adaptations, assistive utensils, powered mobility. Hand weakness affects walker/wheelchair selection; assess functionally.
    • Blood-flow-restriction training is not routine care — limited evidence, trained supervision and vascular assessment only.

    IBM — swallowing, respiratory, and follow-up

    • Swallowing: screen actively every visit (food/pills sticking, repeated swallows, coughing during meals, prolonged mealtimes, texture avoidance, weight loss) — low threshold for speech-language pathology; videofluoroscopy for bolus transit/upper-esophageal-sphincter dysfunction, FEES complementary. Manage by physiology: compensatory strategies, texture/posture/bolus adjustments, dietitian; cricopharyngeal dilation or myotomy for selected obstructive components; enteral nutrition when oral intake is unsafe or insufficient — before an emergency (a tube does not eliminate aspiration of secretions/reflux, and no IBM-specific survival benefit is established).
    • Respiratory: underrecognized and does not track limb weakness. Ask orthopnea, morning headache, unrefreshing sleep, daytime somnolence, weak cough, recurrent infections. Seated vital capacity + supine VC, inspiratory/expiratory pressures or SNIP, peak cough flow ~every 6 months individualized; overnight testing when hypoventilation is suspected (normal daytime O₂ does not establish adequate ventilation); NIV for documented nocturnal hypoventilation or chronic failure plus an airway-clearance plan.
    • Follow-up: neuromuscular every 3–6 months — IBMFRS (0–40), bilateral finger-flexor/knee-extensor strength (quantitative when available), falls/mobility/aid suitability, weight and nutrition. Do not use serial CK or anti-cN1A titers as treatment targets; repeat EMG/MRI/biopsy only for a new diagnostic question, an unexpected course, or research.
    • Prognosis counseling: years-to-decades course; wheelchair commonly necessary ~10–15 years after onset (wide range — not an individual deadline); aspiration-related respiratory disease, respiratory-muscle weakness, malnutrition, and disability complications are the major preventable threats. No enhanced malignancy screening — no clear excess cancer risk; continue age-appropriate screening.
    • Red-flag return: new inability to maintain nutrition/hydration, recurrent aspiration, orthopnea or resting dyspnea, ineffective cough during infection, unexpectedly rapid weakness — sudden deterioration warrants a search for a superimposed problem, not automatic attribution to IBM.

    TRICARE — myopathy prescribing

    Prednisone, methotrexate, azathioprine, mycophenolate, and hydroxychloroquine are generic with minimal barriers — they should never be the drugs that wait on paperwork. IVIG (Octagam 10% carries the adult-DM indication), rituximab, tacrolimus, cyclophosphamide, nintedanib, and brepocitinib (Lisraya) are branded/specialty and PA-gated with specialty requirements; start the PA the same visit you decide, and expect step-therapy documentation for the newer agents. Verify current status at the TRICARE Formulary Search before counseling — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically, and new approvals lag formulary updates.

    Disposition¶

    • Inflammatory myopathy (DM/PM/IMNM/antisynthetase): rheumatology–neurology co-management; visits every 2–4 weeks during induction, spacing as disease stabilizes. PFTs every 3–6 months early when ILD is present. Admit for rapidly progressive ILD, aspiration-risk dysphagia, rhabdomyolysis with AKI risk, or respiratory-muscle weakness.
    • IBM: neuromuscular clinic every 3–6 months with PT/OT, speech-language pathology, dietitian, and pulmonary as indicated. Most care is outpatient and supportive — the interventions that matter are swallowing surveillance, NIV timing, falls prevention, and equipment.
    • Goals-of-care: in IBM, advance directives and feeding-tube preferences before a crisis; in severe inflammatory disease with ILD, transplant candidacy and ceilings of care discussed early.

    Military medicine — myopathy

    Framework — verify against current AR 40-501, DoDI 6130.03, DoDI 1332.18, service deployment/aeromedical policy (and any relevant JTS CPG) before counseling.

    • Deployability: active inflammatory myopathy with weakness, ILD, dysphagia, or immunosuppression is not deployable; IBM is progressive and not deployable. Duty limitations begin at the workup stage.
    • Profile (DA Form 3349): temporary P3 during induction/workup; permanent P3 typical once chronic — functional limits on lifting, carrying, marching, running, and duties requiring reliable swallow or respiratory reserve.
    • EPTS / LOD: document onset, exposures (including statin history for anti-HMGCR IMNM — the LOD determination differs from idiopathic disease), and duty status carefully; the clinical timeline is the evidence.
    • Retention (AR 40-501 / DoDI 6130.03): progressive or treatment-refractory myopathy with functional limitation fails retention standards → MEB/IDES referral; initiate promptly rather than watching decline.
    • MEB / IDES: refer at diagnosis for IBM or when inflammatory myopathy declares itself chronic/refractory; the documentation that carries the board is the longitudinal record — MMT-8/functional trends, PFT trends when ILD is present, antibody results, and the biopsy report.
    • Aeromedical / special duty: not waiverable for continued flight or special duty with active disease, ILD, respiratory-muscle weakness, or chronic immunosuppression; ground/remove from status.
    • VA note: unlike ALS there is no presumptive service connection for myopathy — document the in-service onset, exposures, and functional impact thoroughly so the veteran's claim file is complete at separation.

    📚 Discover — evidence brief¶

    Written from primary guidelines and trials; source articles are a reading list, not copied.

    • Brepocitinib is the first genuinely new thing in the DM cabinet in years. Approved August 27, 2026, for adult dermatomyositis on a 241-patient phase 3 (week-52 Total Improvement Score 46.5 vs 31.2 placebo), it is a TYK2/JAK1 inhibitor dosed 30 mg daily — but the serious-infection signal (~10% vs 1%) and the boxed warning mean it is a selected-refractory option, not a first-line swap, and its place versus IVIG and rituximab awaits head-to-head data.
    • The 2024 ENMC IBM framework ends the rimmed-vacuole absolutism. Vacuoles and protein aggregates are now one supportive category among four, imaging and anti-cN1A are formally incorporated, and the criteria acknowledge what clinicians knew: only 43% of IBM biopsies in a 335-patient cohort showed all three classic findings. Biopsy is still mandatory — antibodies and MRI support, they do not replace.
    • The IBM drug pipeline keeps failing, and that is the point of the page. Bimagrumab (RESILIENT, n=251), arimoclomol, and now ulviprubart/ABC008 (MUSCLE, n=272, failed IBMFRS at week 76) all missed their primaries. Exercise remains the only active therapy with consistent benefit — which is why the IBM section is a rehab prescription, not a drug ladder.
    • RP-ILD rewards speed and punishes ritual. The 2023 ACR/CHEST guideline conditionally favors rituximab over cyclophosphamide for anti-MDA5 RP-ILD, but a 2025 multicenter cohort failed to reproduce the historical Japanese triple-therapy survival benefit — the lesson is early individualized combination, not automatic cyclophosphamide triple for every MDA5-positive patient.
    • "Polymyositis" is now a diagnosis of exclusion on this page — before escalating apparently refractory PM, the framework forces reconsideration of antisynthetase syndrome, IMNM, IBM, and mimics. Several "refractory PM" cases in the literature were IBM all along.
