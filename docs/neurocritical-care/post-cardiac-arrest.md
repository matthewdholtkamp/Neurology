# Post–cardiac arrest care and neuroprognostication

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** After adult ROSC, prevent secondary injury, find the cause and treat seizures early. Put the formal prognosis on the calendar after rewarming and confounder clearance.

!!! danger "Act now on reversible problems"
    Recurrent arrest, shock, hypoxemia, ongoing convulsions, acute focal deficits and unexplained pupillary changes warrant immediate reassessment. Obtain critical care input. If a primary brain event is plausible, pursue the appropriate [stroke](../stroke/index.md), [hemorrhage](../stroke/intracerebral-hemorrhage.md) or [coma](coma-workup.md) pathway.

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — First hour after ROSC"
    - Record arrest circumstances, initial rhythm, resuscitation timeline, ROSC time, suspected cause, pre-arrest function and drugs received. Check ECG, glucose, relevant labs and blood gas. Use echo, CT or other tests according to the differential and stability.
    - Give **100% oxygen initially until oxygenation can be measured reliably**, then titrate to avoid hypoxemia and hyperoxemia. AHA gives **SpO₂ 90–98%** or **PaO₂ 60–105 mm Hg**. Confirm with blood gas when readings are unreliable. In ventilated patients who remain comatose, generally target **PaCO₂ 35–45 mm Hg**, checked by blood gas when available.
    - Maintain **MAP at least 65 mm Hg** and individualize for perfusion and cause of shock. Involve cardiology for persistent ST elevation or concern for ongoing ischemia, cardiogenic shock or recurrent ventricular arrhythmia.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Temperature, exam, and EEG"
    - For adults unresponsive to verbal commands after ROSC, start a protocolized temperature strategy. The 2025 AHA range is **32–37.5°C**, with **at least 36 hours** of temperature control reasonable. Choose the target with the ICU and document initiation, rewarming and the fever prevention plan.
    - Trend pupils, brainstem responses and best motor response. Write down temperature, sedatives, neuromuscular blockers, renal/hepatic dysfunction and metabolic confounders alongside the exam.
    - Obtain EEG promptly when the patient remains unresponsive. Use serial or continuous recording when appropriate and available. Treat clinical and electrographic seizures. Myoclonus needs EEG correlation. AHA advises against suppressing myoclonus without an EEG correlate solely to stop the movement. Routine seizure prophylaxis is not recommended.

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Prognostic synthesis with the ICU team"
    - **Wait at least 72 hours** after ROSC when hypothermic temperature control was not used, or **at least 72 hours after rewarming** when it was. The 2025 AHA guideline emphasizes synthesis at least 72 hours after normothermia and discontinuation of sedatives. Wait longer if sedative effect or other confounders remain.
    - Combine serial examination with EEG, imaging and, where appropriate, somatosensory evoked potentials and biomarkers. Apply each test at its validated time and check technical quality. Use imaging patterns of diffuse injury and laboratory trends in context. Avoid universal biomarker cutoffs across assays.
    - Review discordant results or indeterminate prognosis in a multidisciplinary discussion. Continue observation when consistent with the patient's goals. Early myoclonus, poor motor response or coma at 72 hours alone cannot establish an unfavorable outcome.

## Family conversation and disposition

Start family meetings early. Explain which treatments are underway, why the exam may be unreliable today, and when the team expects to reassess. Ask what recovery would matter to the patient. Example: “We’re treating the causes we can and watching for seizures. The medicines and temperature treatment affect the exam. We’ll put the examination, EEG, and scans together once those effects have cleared.”

Patients requiring ventilatory, hemodynamic, temperature or seizure management need ICU-level care. For survivors, assess cognition, mood, mobility, fatigue and caregiver needs before discharge. Arrange appropriate cardiac and neurologic follow-up and rehabilitation.

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Give the transport team the arrest timeline, exam trend, drug times and temperature phase. During recovery document cognitive, motor and endurance limitations, plus onset and duty context for EPTS/LOD. Use the [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guide](../military/deployability.md) and [deployment screen](../military/deployment-medications.md).

## Discover

Temperature targets are a range within an active protocol. Prognostication is a separate decision that depends on timing, confounders and the agreement of several findings. See [death by neurologic criteria](brain-death.md) for that distinct determination.
