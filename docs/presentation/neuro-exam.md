# Focused neuro exam and localization

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Find the deficit that changes what you do next, then write it down so the next clinician can tell whether it changed.

!!! danger "Move before you finish the exam"
    - Sudden focal deficit, including new aphasia, visual field loss, or inability to walk: activate the [stroke pathway](../stroke/index.md). Establish last known well and check bedside glucose in parallel.
    - Falling consciousness, new pupil asymmetry with deterioration, ongoing convulsions, or respiratory/bulbar weakness: get help and stabilize immediately. See [coma](../neurocritical-care/coma-workup.md), [status epilepticus](../seizure/index.md), and [acute weakness](acute-weakness.md).
    - New bilateral limb deficits with a sensory level or bladder symptoms: pursue [cord or cauda equina evaluation](../acute-myelopathy.md) urgently. Early cord injury can be flaccid.

## The bedside pass

!!! orderset "Examine, localize, repeat"
    1. **Set the clock.** Establish onset, last known normal, baseline deficits, and whether the change was abrupt, progressive, episodic, or fluctuating. Record sedatives, glucose, pain, and anything that limits the exam.
    2. **Attention and language.** Check alertness, orientation, and attention, then ask the patient to name an object, repeat a phrase, and follow a command. Describe language separately from articulation. Account for language barriers and hearing loss.
    3. **Eyes and face.** Compare pupils in light and dark, test fields in each eye, examine gaze and alignment, and check facial movement. Add acuity, color comparison and a swinging flashlight test when vision is the complaint. Assess speech, voice and secretion handling.
    4. **Motor and reflexes.** Compare arm drift, finger taps, and strength at proximal and distal joints. Grade and name the muscles if weak. Compare tone, tendon reflexes and plantar responses as indicated.
    5. **Sensation and coordination.** Compare sides for touch and pin, test vibration and joint position when needed, and look for a truncal level or extinction. Check finger–nose and heel–shin with weakness accounted for.
    6. **Stance and gait, when safe.** Watch the patient stand, start, turn and walk. Use assistance and document when testing is unsafe. If gait is the symptom, a normal supine exam leaves the key question unanswered.

## Where is the lesion?

| Pattern to look for | Likely level or next question |
|---|---|
| Aphasia, neglect, gaze preference, field cut, or face/arm/leg weakness on one side | Hemisphere. Acute onset goes to the stroke pathway, even with few findings. A pure motor deficit can be subcortical or brainstem. |
| Diplopia or dysarthria with long-tract findings; cranial nerve signs on one side and body findings on the other | Brainstem. Look for dysphagia, nystagmus, ataxia and other subtle findings. |
| Dysmetria, truncal instability or lateral veering out of proportion to weakness or sensory loss | Cerebellar or vestibular pathways. Sudden severe imbalance can be a stroke. Compare with [acute dizziness](dizziness.md). |
| Bilateral limb findings below a level, back pain or new bladder dysfunction | Cord or cauda equina. Compare upper and lower limb findings, and check for sacral symptoms. |
| Myotomal weakness with reflex change, or weakness crossing roots within a single named nerve | Root versus peripheral nerve. Map weak **and spared** muscles. See [radiculopathy](../radiculopathy-plexopathy.md) and [neuropathy](peripheral-neuropathy.md). |
| Fluctuating ocular or bulbar symptoms, or symmetric proximal weakness | Consider neuromuscular junction or muscle. Check sensation, reflexes, fatigability, respiratory function and time course. |

**Handoff example:** “Last known well 01:10. Awake, follows two-step commands. New right lower facial weakness, right arm drift and word-finding difficulty. Baseline walks independently. Glucose checked. Stroke team activated.” Use observed findings, laterality and times. Repeat the same exam after a clinical change or before transfer.

## Disposition

New or progressing focal deficits, declining alertness, unsafe swallowing or breathing, inability to walk safely, and suspected cord compression need emergency evaluation. For stable outpatient findings, document the working localization, the test or referral that would change it, and the escalation instructions.

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Record observable function such as gait, hand dexterity, visual fields and fatigability. Document onset and duty or injury context for EPTS/LOD. Use the [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guide](../military/deployability.md) and [deployment and medication screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the confirmed diagnosis, task limitations and applicable theater or special-duty standards.

## Discover

The AAN clinical neurology curriculum ties the exam to localization, time course and urgent management. This page adapts that framework for a short handoff. Expand the exam where the deficit points, and use the disease-specific pathway when a time-critical syndrome is suspected.
