# Spells, syncope, and functional neurologic disorder

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Reconstruct the minute before, during and after the spell. Get the witness and the ECG before the chart decides it was epilepsy.

!!! danger "First exclude a time-critical cause"
    - Ongoing convulsions for five minutes, or recurrent convulsions without return to baseline: stabilize and use the [convulsive status pathway](../seizure/index.md). Persistent impaired awareness after a spell needs reassessment for ongoing seizure, toxic/metabolic illness or other acute causes.
    - Collapse during exertion, chest pain or palpitations around the spell, abnormal ECG, known structural heart disease or a family history of early sudden death: urgent cardiac assessment.
    - New focal deficit, severe headache, significant injury, major bleeding, pregnancy/postpartum with concerning features, or unstable vitals: route to emergency evaluation for the suspected cause.

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Reconstruct the event"
    1. Check vitals and bedside glucose when indicated. Obtain a 12-lead ECG after transient loss of consciousness. Consider orthostatic vitals once safe when the story suggests orthostasis. Review medicines, substances and missed doses.
    2. Get a witness account or consented video. Ask about posture, trigger, prodrome, color, movement timing, eye position, injuries, duration and the recovery clock. Clarify what the person remembers.
    3. Determine whether loss of awareness or posture actually occurred. Ask whether recurrent event types differ. Time the event rather than relying on “it lasted forever.”

| Clues | Next route |
|---|---|
| Standing, pain or heat trigger; warmth, nausea or tunnel vision; rapid recovery | Reflex syncope is plausible after assessment and ECG. Brief jerks can occur with syncope. |
| Abrupt collapse with exertion, supine, or with palpitations; abnormal ECG or structural heart disease | Consider arrhythmia or structural cardiac disease. Arrange monitoring and urgent evaluation as appropriate. |
| Stereotyped onset, lateral tongue injury, sustained head turning, or prolonged confusion | Consider epileptic seizure. Use the [first-seizure workup](../seizure/index.md), including EEG and imaging when indicated. |
| Recurrent events with features suggestive of functional seizures | Obtain specialist confirmation. Review the full semiology, ideally with video-EEG of each typical event type where feasible. Functional seizures and epilepsy can coexist. |

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Order for the leading risk"
    - With unexplained or high-risk transient loss of consciousness, prioritize cardiac evaluation and select ambulatory ECG monitoring according to event frequency and suspicion. Routine EEG has little value when the presentation clearly fits uncomplicated syncope.
    - With suspected epilepsy, arrange timely specialist evaluation. A normal interictal EEG cannot rule out epilepsy. For persistent altered awareness, use the [altered mental status and EEG pathway](../neurocritical-care/ams-workup.md).
    - For other possible FND presentations, look for positive findings specific to the symptom. In functional leg weakness, Hoover’s sign is weak voluntary hip extension that improves during resisted flexion of the opposite hip. Interpret in context, including pain and comprehension. New objective findings still deserve evaluation. A history of trauma, psychiatric symptoms or normal imaging alone cannot establish FND.

## Give the diagnosis and the next step

When functional seizures are established, name them and explain the evidence: “These are real, involuntary episodes. The findings we saw during your usual spell support functional seizures. We can work on a plan to help you manage them.” Provide a clear event safety plan, review coexisting epilepsy and other conditions, and arrange neurology continuity and an appropriate psychological intervention. For motor FND, consider rehabilitation with clinicians familiar with functional symptoms.

Benzodiazepines and antiseizure medicines have no role for established functional seizures alone. If they were prescribed solely for functional seizures, arrange a supervised taper. Treat coexisting epilepsy on its own plan. During a typical confirmed event, protect from injury, observe breathing, and coach witnesses to stay calm and avoid restraint. If a future spell differs or emergency features arise, reassess.

## Disposition

Urgent evaluation or observation when a cardiac or neurologic emergency remains plausible, the patient has not returned to baseline, or safe follow-up cannot be secured. Confirm follow-up and discuss applicable driving rules and precautions around water, heights, machinery and other safety-sensitive activity.

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Document the episode, known triggers, recovery, recurrence and functional impact. Record onset and duty or injury context for EPTS/LOD. Specify temporary profile restrictions by task while the cause is clarified. Use [retention and MEB/IDES guidance](../military/deployability.md) and the [deployment and medication screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the established diagnosis. Coordinate flight or other special-duty decisions with the relevant authority.

## Discover

NICE emphasizes the witness history and ECG in blackouts. The AAN functional seizures guideline emphasizes respectful explanation, evaluation for coexisting epilepsy, and continuity of care. The treatment plan follows the confirmed diagnosis and the individual event types.
