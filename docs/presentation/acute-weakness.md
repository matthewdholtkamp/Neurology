# Acute weakness and Guillain–Barré syndrome

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Check breathing and swallowing while you localize. A patient can still be talking while their respiratory reserve is shrinking.

!!! danger "Get help now"
    Weak cough, difficulty handling secretions, bulbar weakness, respiratory distress, rapid progression, or marked blood pressure or heart-rate swings need early ICU and airway involvement. A reassuring oxygen saturation alone cannot exclude neuromuscular hypoventilation. Sudden asymmetric weakness goes to the [stroke pathway](../stroke/index.md). A sensory level, new retention or saddle symptoms warrants urgent [spinal evaluation](../acute-myelopathy.md).

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Stabilize and localize"
    1. Assess airway protection, voice, cough and breathing effort. Check vitals and bedside glucose. Monitor ECG and blood pressure if GBS is suspected. Keep oral intake on hold if swallowing is unsafe.
    2. Obtain baseline and serial forced vital capacity (FVC) and inspiratory/expiratory pressures where available. Record the trend, patient position, effort and quality of the mouth seal. If respiratory testing is unavailable, follow speech, cough, effort and secretion handling while arranging transfer as indicated.
    3. Measure limb and neck strength and reflexes. Map sensory loss and check for ocular symptoms, a sensory level or sphincter dysfunction. Review the time course, recent infection, medication changes and exposures. Use targeted electrolytes, including potassium, magnesium and phosphate, and CK when the pattern suggests metabolic weakness or myopathy.

| Pattern | Working route |
|---|---|
| Progressive, relatively symmetric limb weakness with reduced reflexes, paresthesias or neuropathic pain | GBS. Check facial, bulbar and autonomic findings. The classic pattern progresses over days and usually reaches nadir within four weeks. Variants can break the pattern. |
| Fluctuating ptosis, diplopia or bulbar fatigue with preserved sensation | [Myasthenia](../nmj/index.md). Look for impending crisis. |
| Bilateral weakness with a level, back pain, or new bowel/bladder dysfunction | [Cord/conus/cauda equina](../acute-myelopathy.md). Early cord syndromes can have depressed reflexes. |
| Prominent proximal weakness or myalgia, or weakness after a metabolic trigger | [Myopathy or electrolyte disorder](../myopathy/index.md). Treat a dangerous reversible cause in parallel. |

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Confirm the pattern while watching the airway"
    - In suspected GBS, obtain CSF and EMG/NCS with neurology when feasible. Elevated CSF protein with few cells supports the diagnosis. Early CSF protein and early electrodiagnostics can be normal. Significant CSF pleocytosis or strongly atypical features should prompt a search for alternatives.
    - Consider anti-GQ1b testing when ophthalmoplegia, ataxia and areflexia suggest Miller Fisher syndrome. Urgently image when the presentation could localize to the brainstem or spinal cord.
    - Discuss elective ventilation with ICU when FVC approaches **20 mL/kg**, falls quickly, or airway protection deteriorates. Use the trajectory and bedside exam together. ICU escalation can be appropriate with higher FVC if there is bulbar weakness, dysautonomia or rapid progression.

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Treat GBS and prevent complications"
    - **IVIG 0.4 g/kg IV daily for five days** for patients unable to walk unaided, preferably within two weeks of weakness onset. The guideline also supports considering IVIG at two to four weeks. Coordinate early treatment with neurology for ambulatory patients with rapid progression, bulbar or respiratory involvement, or other high-risk features.
    - **Plasma exchange** is an alternative, typically **four to five exchanges over one to two weeks** for patients unable to walk unaided presenting within four weeks. Choose with neurology and apheresis based on access, contraindications and clinical stability. See [infusion and exchange protocols](../immunotherapy-order-sets.md).
    - Use aspiration and thrombosis precautions as indicated, monitor autonomic instability, and start rehabilitation in hospital. Corticosteroids are ineffective for classic GBS. Avoid routine sequential IVIG and exchange or a second IVIG course for predicted poor outcome. Reassess new deterioration after stabilization with neurology. Worsening beyond eight weeks from onset or three or more treatment-related fluctuations should raise acute-onset CIDP.

## Disposition and handoff

Admit suspected GBS for serial neurologic and respiratory assessment. Escalate to ICU for respiratory, bulbar or autonomic risk. If the facility cannot provide monitoring or respiratory support, call early for transfer. Send onset and progression, last safe ambulation, cough and swallowing status, respiratory trends, and hemodynamic events.

!!! tip "TRICARE — IVIG and apheresis"
    Acute treatment and transfer take priority. For subsequent infusions, confirm the exact product, treatment site and medical or pharmacy benefit. Use the [TRICARE authorization guide](../military/tricare.md) and [Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst) for covered pharmacy products.

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Record premorbid function, onset, exposure or duty context for EPTS/LOD, and residual endurance, gait and respiratory limits. In a deployed facility with limited monitoring, coordinate early consultation and evacuation. For recovery, use the [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guide](../military/deployability.md) and [deployment screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the actual theater and duty.

## Discover

EAN/PNS guidance pairs early recognition and serial respiratory monitoring with immunotherapy according to severity and timing. Laboratory confirmation can lag the bedside picture. Treatment-related fluctuation requires fresh assessment and has different implications from slow recovery.
