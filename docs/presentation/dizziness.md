# Acute dizziness and vertigo

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Ask when it started, whether it stops, and what brings on a new episode. “Spinning” alone will send you down several corridors.

!!! danger "Think posterior circulation early"
    New focal deficits, severe gait or truncal instability, sudden head/neck pain with neurologic symptoms, or new unilateral hearing loss with continuous acute vertigo warrant urgent assessment for a central cause. Activate the [stroke pathway](../stroke/index.md) when indicated. A negative head CT cannot clear a suspected posterior circulation infarct.

## The bedside flow

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Classify the syndrome"
    1. Document last known well, episode duration, symptoms at rest, and whether turning the head **starts** a discrete attack or merely worsens ongoing dizziness. Check vitals, ocular movements, hearing, coordination and safe gait.
    2. **Continuous, still symptomatic** with nausea or gait instability: acute vestibular syndrome. Assess for stroke versus a peripheral vestibulopathy.
    3. **Brief episodes triggered by position** with relative baseline between them: test for BPPV. If standing is the trigger and the patient feels faint, assess orthostasis and use the [blackout pathway](spells.md) as appropriate.
    4. **Spontaneous episodes**: use the history and focal exam to assess TIA, vestibular migraine, Ménière disease or a nonvestibular cause. Examine between attacks, but remember a resolved episode can leave a normal exam.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Choose the bedside test and imaging"
    - **Continuous syndrome with spontaneous nystagmus:** HINTS is for trained clinicians in this setting. A normal head impulse, direction-changing gaze-evoked nystagmus, or skew is concerning for a central cause. Check for new unilateral hearing loss as well.
    - **No spontaneous nystagmus:** do not use HINTS to clear stroke. Assess gait and truncal stability, and use the focal exam and clinical risk to guide the stroke workup.
    - **Central or equivocal HINTS, or stroke concern without a trained examiner:** involve the stroke team and pursue appropriate imaging. GRACE-3 recommends MRI/MRA for central or equivocal HINTS. In a stroke activation, follow the local acute imaging pathway without waiting for elective MRI. With persistent concern after early negative MRI, reassess with neurology.
    - **Triggered positional spells:** use Dix–Hallpike for suspected posterior canal BPPV. Brief, characteristic upbeat-torsional nystagmus supports that diagnosis. If compatible, use the Epley maneuver on the affected side, adapting or deferring positional maneuvers when neck mobility or injury makes them unsafe. With a compatible history and horizontal or absent positional nystagmus on Dix–Hallpike, use the supine roll test or refer for lateral canal assessment.
    - **Spontaneous episodic symptoms with TIA concern:** use the [TIA pathway](../stroke/tia.md) and discuss CTA or MRA for large-vessel pathology. The scan should answer the vascular question.

## Treatment and disposition

Use repositioning for typical posterior canal BPPV and review fall precautions. Routine imaging and vestibular suppressants are unnecessary in a characteristic, confirmed case. Arrange reassessment within one month, sooner for ongoing disability or atypical findings. For probable vestibular neuritis after a central cause has been addressed, review hydration, symptom support and vestibular rehabilitation. GRACE-3 treats corticosteroids in the first three days as a shared decision with very low certainty of benefit. Use local prescribing guidance if considering them.

Observe, admit, or transfer when stroke remains plausible, walking is unsafe, oral intake is inadequate, or timely evaluation cannot be completed locally. For a secure peripheral diagnosis, document gait and reassessment, arrange follow-up, and give return instructions for new neurologic symptoms or worsening imbalance. For symptoms after head injury, use [post-TBI vestibular care](../tbi/vestibular.md).

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Document whether symptoms impair safe driving, climbing, weapons handling, or flight duties. Record onset and exposure or injury history for EPTS/LOD. Use [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guidance](../military/deployability.md) and the [deployment medication screen](../military/deployment-medications.md). Review sedating medications and involve flight medicine for covered duties.

## Discover

GRACE-3 organizes adult ED dizziness by timing and triggers. Its bedside eye-movement recommendations depend on examiner training and syndrome selection. The positional BPPV recommendations support a maneuver-first workflow for the characteristic presentation.
