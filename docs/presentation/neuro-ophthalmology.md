# Vision loss, double vision, and facial palsy

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Check acuity, fields, pupils and eye movements before a reassuring label gets attached to a dangerous exam.

!!! danger "Protect sight and act on vascular clues"
    | Presentation | Immediate route |
    |---|---|
    | Sudden painless monocular loss, including transient loss suspicious for retinal ischemia | Emergency stroke evaluation with ophthalmology. Record last known well. Coordinate acute options with the receiving team. |
    | New visual loss with jaw claudication, scalp tenderness or other features of giant cell arteritis in an adult over 50 | Send ESR, CRP and CBC with platelets when feasible. Call ophthalmology and the treating team for immediate glucocorticoid treatment when suspicion is high. Draw tests without delaying treatment for threatened vision. |
    | New third nerve palsy, especially painful or with an enlarged pupil | Urgent vascular imaging for aneurysm. Pupil sparing alone cannot clear an acute third nerve palsy. |
    | New ptosis and small pupil with ipsilateral head or neck pain or after trauma | Evaluate for carotid dissection with emergency vascular imaging. |
    | Painful red eye with reduced vision, halos or vomiting, or vision loss after eye trauma | Emergency ocular assessment. Consider acute angle closure or an injury requiring immediate ophthalmology. |
    | Suspected papilledema with declining vision, severe headache or altered alertness | Emergency imaging and ophthalmology/neurology assessment. See [raised ICP](../neurocritical-care/increased-icp.md) and [venous thrombosis](../stroke/cerebral-venous-thrombosis.md). |
    | Facial weakness plus dysarthria, diplopia, ataxia or limb deficits | Activate the [stroke pathway](../stroke/index.md). A pontine lesion can affect the entire hemiface. |

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Document the minimum eye exam"
    1. Test acuity in each eye with correction when possible, then pinhole if reduced. Compare confrontation fields, pupils in light/dark, relative afferent pupillary defect and color perception. Record eye pain, redness, trauma and onset. A painful red eye with reduced vision needs urgent ocular assessment.
    2. Check alignment, lids and motility in all directions. Ask whether double vision clears when **either** eye is covered. Examine the fundus if able. If the disc cannot be assessed reliably, document that and arrange an appropriate exam.
    3. For facial weakness, compare forehead, eye closure and smile. Check other cranial nerves, ear canal and pinna, and assess for limb findings, hearing change or vertigo.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Match workup to the pattern"
    - **Monocular visual loss:** localize to the eye or optic nerve. Flashes, floaters or a curtain suggest retinal tear or detachment and need urgent ophthalmology. Pain with eye movement, reduced color vision and an afferent defect suggest optic neuropathy. Use dedicated orbit MRI with and without contrast when optic neuritis is suspected, with brain imaging as indicated. Route severe or atypical cases promptly to the [demyelination workup](../ms/index.md).
    - **Binocular diplopia:** map the movement defect and accompanying signs. Ptosis with impaired adduction, elevation or depression suggests III. Vertical diplopia worse in downgaze can suggest IV. Abduction weakness suggests VI. Multiple nerve deficits, proptosis, or pain with fever require urgent orbital/cavernous sinus evaluation. Fluctuating ptosis or diplopia with normal pupils can suggest [myasthenia](../nmj/index.md).
    - **Suspected papilledema:** document acuity and fields, measure blood pressure, and arrange urgent brain imaging with venography to exclude structural disease and CVST. If imaging permits and LP is appropriate, measure opening pressure in lateral decubitus and check CSF composition. Worsening fields or acuity require urgent specialist management.
    - **Isolated peripheral-pattern facial palsy:** look for vesicles, ear infection, exposure compatible with Lyme disease, recurrence, bilateral involvement or gradual progression. Those findings change the workup. Typical new Bell palsy usually needs neither routine imaging nor routine labs.

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Treat typical adult Bell palsy and protect the cornea"
    - **Prednisolone 25 mg PO twice daily for 10 days** is one trial-supported adult regimen when started within **72 hours**. Review contraindications and glycemic risk. This regimen is for idiopathic Bell palsy after evaluation for alternative causes.
    - **Eye protection:** start preservative-free lubricating drops during the day and lubricating ointment at bedtime if closure is impaired. Demonstrate gentle taping with the lid fully closed or use a moisture chamber. Eye pain, redness, photophobia or blurred vision needs prompt ophthalmology.
    - Antiviral monotherapy has no role in idiopathic Bell palsy. An adjunct can be discussed with selected patients. Vesicles or other evidence of zoster warrants a separate diagnosis and treatment plan.

## Disposition

Send acute sight-threatening or vascular presentations for emergency workup. For uncomplicated Bell palsy, confirm that the patient can protect the eye, arrange follow-up, and reassess with any new neurologic findings or ocular symptoms. Refer for incomplete recovery at three months.

!!! tip "TRICARE — outpatient treatment and follow-up"
    Verify the exact product through the [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst) and route specialty visits or infusions through the correct benefit. See the [prior authorization guide](../military/tricare.md).

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Document acuity, fields, diplopia in primary gaze, and ability to close the eye. Record onset and any injury for EPTS/LOD. Apply [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guidance](../military/deployability.md) and [destination-specific deployment standards](../military/deployment-medications.md). Refer flight and other vision-critical duties to the applicable authority.

## Discover

AAO treats acute symptomatic retinal arterial occlusion as an emergency stroke referral. The Bell palsy steroid recommendation rests on randomized trials. Pupil sparing is less reassuring in a new third nerve palsy than older bedside rules suggest, so let vascular imaging answer the aneurysm question.
