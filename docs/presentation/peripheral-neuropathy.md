# Peripheral neuropathy and entrapment

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Draw the distribution before you order the panel. Burning feet, a numb thumb and a new foot drop are different consults.

!!! danger "Move the workup up the calendar"
    - Rapidly progressive weakness, bulbar symptoms, dyspnea or major dysautonomia: use the [acute weakness pathway](acute-weakness.md).
    - New bladder dysfunction, saddle symptoms or a sensory level: urgent [spinal evaluation](../acute-myelopathy.md).
    - Painful, asymmetric, stepwise deficits in multiple nerves or new motor loss: expedite neurology assessment for vasculitic, inflammatory, compressive or other treatable causes. After trauma or with severe limb pain/swelling, evaluate for a surgical emergency.

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Map the pattern"
    Check pin and vibration, ankle reflexes, distal strength, gait and foot skin. In a focal complaint, compare muscles supplied by different nerves but the same root. Check occupational pressure, training load, weight loss, diabetes, alcohol, supplements such as B6, and neurotoxic medications.

| Pattern | Useful next step |
|---|---|
| Slow, symmetric symptoms starting at the toes with reduced distal sensation | Evaluate distal symmetric polyneuropathy. Foot protection and falls prevention can start today. |
| Burning, allodynia or pin/temperature loss with preserved vibration and strength | Consider small-fiber neuropathy. Normal routine NCS can occur. Refer for targeted testing when the result will affect management. |
| Nocturnal thumb, index and middle finger paresthesia, sometimes with thenar weakness | Median neuropathy at the wrist. Use the clinical pattern or CTS-6 in typical cases. Assess for proximal disease or other neuropathy when findings are atypical. |
| Ring/small finger symptoms with elbow flexion or pressure and weak finger abduction | Consider ulnar neuropathy. Compare dorsal ulnar sensation and proximal muscle findings to help localize. |
| Foot drop with weak dorsiflexion and eversion | Consider common fibular neuropathy. Weak inversion or hip abduction raises concern for L5 or a more proximal lesion. Inspect around the fibular head and see [root and plexus patterns](../radiculopathy-plexopathy.md). |

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Order tests that answer the question"
    - **Unexplained distal symmetric polyneuropathy:** check glucose or A1c, vitamin B12 with methylmalonic acid when indicated, and serum protein immunofixation. Add CBC and metabolic testing to look for systemic contributors as appropriate. Consider glucose tolerance testing when routine glucose tests are unrevealing in a compatible sensory phenotype.
    - **EMG/NCS:** order when localization is uncertain, a new motor deficit is unexplained, or axonal versus demyelinating physiology would change the plan. Ask a specific question, such as fibular neuropathy versus L5 radiculopathy. If an early study is unrevealing after acute injury, discuss timing and repeat testing with the lab.
    - **Typical carpal tunnel:** the AAOS supports the CTS-6 as a clinical diagnostic tool in lieu of routine ultrasound or EMG/NCS. Atypical symptoms, competing localization and motor loss can change the testing or referral decision.

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Protect function and treat the cause"
    - Correct a documented deficiency or remove a likely toxic or compressive exposure as appropriate. Address diabetes care and foot surveillance. Use PT or OT for balance, strength and adaptations.
    - For mild typical carpal tunnel, try a neutral wrist splint at night. For ulnar symptoms at the elbow, reduce direct pressure and prolonged flexion. For foot drop, address fall risk and consider an ankle-foot orthosis while clarifying the cause. Progressive weakness or atrophy warrants prompt referral.
    - For **painful diabetic neuropathy** in a suitable adult, **duloxetine 60 mg PO daily** is one labeled option. A lower starting dose can improve tolerability. Avoid in chronic liver disease, substantial alcohol use or GFR **under 30 mL/min**. Review interactions and orthostatic symptoms. If benefit is inadequate or adverse effects limit therapy, consider a different evidence-supported class. Avoid opioids for this indication.

## Disposition

Arrange expedited workup for motor, asymmetric, rapidly evolving or systemic features. For stable sensory symptoms or mild entrapment, make a follow-up plan around gait, pain interference, skin integrity and objective strength. Give clear instructions to return for progression, falls, new weakness or foot wounds.

!!! tip "TRICARE — drugs and testing"
    Check the exact drug and strength in the [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst). For EMG/NCS, orthoses, therapy or surgery, confirm referral and authorization through the applicable benefit. See the [TRICARE guide](../military/tricare.md).

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Document falls, gait on uneven ground, boot and orthosis tolerance, grip and dexterity. Record onset and exposure or injury details for EPTS/LOD. Tie profile limitations to the task and use the [retention and MEB/IDES guide](../military/deployability.md) and [deployment medication screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) to assess the condition and any sedating therapy.

## Discover

The AAN laboratory evidence favors a focused initial workup for distal symmetric polyneuropathy. AAOS supports clinical diagnosis in typical carpal tunnel. For painful diabetic neuropathy, several drug classes help on average. Choose by comorbidity, adverse effects and the patient’s functional goals.
