# Motor Neuron Disease

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: October 2026</span>

**One-liner:** the prescriptive approach to ALS and its motor-neuron-disease cousins —
how to diagnose them fast, which disease-modifying drugs are actually on the table in
2026 (riluzole, edaravone, tofersen for SOD1), the Relyvrio rise-and-fall every patient
will ask about, the SMA drug cabinet, and the respiratory/nutrition/symptom orderset
that determines survival. For the on-call neurologist or PCP seeing progressive weakness
in clinic, on admit, or in the ED.

!!! danger "🚩 Red flags — don't miss these"
    | Pattern | Worry | Do now |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Bulbar-onset ALS** (dysarthria, dysphagia, tongue fasciculations) with weight loss | Aspiration, rapid nutritional collapse | Same-visit swallow screen, dietitian, discuss PEG early — do not wait for the weight to fall off |
    | **Respiratory symptoms** (orthopnea, morning headache, daytime somnolence, weak cough) in known or suspected ALS | Impending ventilatory failure | ABG, overnight oximetry, pulmonary referral for NIV evaluation — NIV is the single biggest survival lever |
    | Weakness with **sensory signs, sphincter involvement, or a sensory level** | This is **not** MND — myelopathy or something else | MRI spine now; do not anchor on ALS until the spine is cleared |
    | **Conduction block** on NCS in an asymmetric pure-motor syndrome | **Multifocal motor neuropathy** (anti-GM1) — IVIG-responsive, treatable | EMG/NCS with conduction-block protocol; anti-GM1 antibodies; neurology — do not label this ALS |
    | Rapidly progressive weakness + **ophthalmoplegia or fatigability** | Myasthenia gravis / LEMS, not MND | Ice-pack / edrophonium-era thinking is over — get AChR/MuSK antibodies, RNS, and neurology now |
    | Bulbar weakness + **perioral fasciculations, gynecomastia, hand tremor in a man** | **Kennedy disease** (SBMA) — CAG repeat in the androgen receptor | Androgen-receptor CAG repeat testing; genetic counseling; it mimics ALS but the management and prognosis differ |

## Diagnosis / classification

Use the **Gold Coast criteria** (Shefner et al., 2020) — they replaced the El Escorial
"possible/probable/definite" ladder and are what trials now enroll on:

1. **Progressive motor impairment** — documented by history or repeated examination.
2. **UMN + LMN signs in at least one body region, OR LMN signs in at least two body
   regions** (bulbar, cervical, thoracic, lumbosacral).
3. **Investigations exclude other disease processes.**

Body regions are bulbar, cervical, thoracic, lumbosacral. In practice: if you see
tongue fasciculations (LMN bulbar) plus brisk jaw jerk (UMN bulbar), you are already
most of the way there — the EMG then confirms LMN involvement in clinically silent
regions (fibrillations, positive sharp waves, chronic reinnervation).

**Variants that share the page but change the counseling:**

| Entity | What differs | Prognosis note |
|---|---|---|
| Classic ALS (limb or bulbar onset) | Mixed UMN/LMN | Median 3–5 years from symptom onset |
| **Progressive muscular atrophy (PMA)** | Pure LMN | Often evolves into ALS; treat as ALS for trial purposes |
| **Primary lateral sclerosis (PLS)** | Pure UMN, ≥4 years without LMN signs | Much slower; diagnosis is retrospective |
| **Progressive bulbar palsy** | Bulbar-predominant | Usually declares itself as ALS within 1–2 years |
| **Spinal muscular atrophy (SMA)** | SMN1 loss, childhood/adult-onset | Disease-modifying drugs exist — see SMA section below |

## Rapid approach

1. **Exclude the mimics that change everything** — cervical spondylotic myelopathy
   (sensory level, sphincters), multifocal motor neuropathy (conduction block), Kennedy
   disease, myasthenia, inclusion body myositis. MND is a diagnosis of pattern +
   exclusion; the MRI cervical spine and the EMG/NCS are not optional.
2. **Confirm the motor-neuron pattern clinically** — mixed UMN (spasticity, hyperreflexia,
   Babinski, pseudobulbar affect) and LMN (fasciculations, atrophy, weakness) signs across
   regions. Tongue fasciculations and split-hand wasting (thenar > hypothenar) are
   high-yield bedside findings.
3. **Send genetics on every ALS patient** — C9orf72, SOD1, TARDBP, FUS at minimum. A
   SOD1 result unlocks tofersen; a C9orf72 result changes family counseling. Do not
   restrict testing to "familial" cases — ~10% of apparently sporadic ALS is genetic.
4. **Start riluzole while you work the patient up** — the rest of the disease-modifying
   decisions (edaravone, tofersen) and all of the survival-determining care (NIV,
   nutrition, symptom control) run in parallel, not in series.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Confirm pattern + exclude mimics** — exam across all four regions, MRI brain and
   cervical spine (thoracic if indicated), EMG/NCS with a motor-neuron protocol.
2. **Baseline the patient** — ALSFRS-R, FVC (sitting and supine), MIP/SNIP, weight,
   swallow screen, orthopnea/morning-headache review, depression and PBA screen.
3. **Start riluzole now** (Step 1 below) — do not wait for genetics or the full workup.
4. **Send the ALS gene panel** and route by result: SOD1-positive → tofersen pathway;
   all others → edaravone discussion.
5. **Set the survival infrastructure in parallel** — pulmonary (NIV thresholds), GI
   (PEG timing), speech therapy, PT/OT, palliative care introduced early by name.
6. **Run the symptom orderset** — sialorrhea, spasticity, cramps, PBA, pain, sleep,
   constipation. Symptoms are what the patient feels; function is what the scales
   measure.
7. **Disposition and follow-up** — ALS clinic every 1–3 months; FVC and weight at every
   visit; advance-directive and goals-of-care conversations before a crisis.

## Workup

!!! orderset "ALS workup — order as a set"
    - **MRI brain + cervical spine** (contrast as indicated) — exclude myelopathy, mass, demyelination; thoracic spine if thoracic signs.
    - **EMG/NCS** — motor-neuron protocol: fibrillations/positive sharp waves + chronic reinnervation across regions; NCS to hunt conduction block (MMN), demyelination (CIDP), sensory involvement (not ALS).
    - **Labs:** CK (often mildly elevated), TSH, B12, SPEP/immunofixation, serum/urine protein electrophoresis; anti-GM1 if MMN suspected; Lyme/HTLV as indicated by exposure.
    - **Genetics:** ALS gene panel (C9orf72 repeat expansion, SOD1, TARDBP, FUS minimum) for every patient; androgen-receptor CAG repeats if Kennedy disease suspected; SMN1 testing if SMA in the differential.
    - **Pulmonary baseline:** FVC sitting and supine, MIP, SNIP, overnight oximetry; ABG if hypercapnia suspected.
    - **Swallow/nutrition baseline:** bedside swallow screen → videofluoroscopy if abnormal; weight, albumin/prealbumin trend.

!!! warning "Avoid"
    - **Do not diagnose ALS on weakness alone without the UMN/LMN pattern and the mimic
      workup.** Cervical myelopathy with superimposed LMN signs from foraminal disease
      is the classic trap — the spine MRI comes before the label.
    - **Do not delay riluzole for genetics.** The panel takes weeks; the drug does not need it.
    - **Do not prescribe Relyvrio to new patients** — withdrawn from the US/Canadian market
      in April 2024 after PHOENIX failed. Patients will ask; have the saga straight (below).

## Management

!!! orderset "Step 1 — riluzole (start here for every ALS patient)"
    - **Riluzole 50 mg PO BID**, 1 hour before or 2 hours after meals — the only
      drug with a replicated survival signal (~2–3 months median). Cheap, generic,
      well tolerated.
    - **Monitoring:** LFTs at baseline, monthly ×3, then every 3 months — hold/stop if
      ALT >5× ULN or bilirubin rises; CBC if febrile (neutropenia is rare but real).
    - → Continue indefinitely; add Step 2 agents on top, not instead.

!!! orderset "Step 2a — edaravone (for most ALS patients)"
    - **Radicava ORS (oral) 105 mg (5 mL) PO or via feeding tube once daily.**
      Cycle 1: 14 days on, 14 days off. Subsequent cycles: 10 days on, 14 days off.
      Take on an empty stomach (≥1 hour before or ≥2 hours after food).
    - **Radicava IV 60 mg IV over 60 minutes** if oral is not an option — Cycle 1:
      daily ×14 then 14-day drug-free period; then 10 of 14 days, then 14-day drug-free.
    - Oral is bioequivalent to IV and is the default; reserve IV for dysphagia without
      tube access. Contraindicated in sulfite hypersensitivity (anaphylaxis risk).
    - → Functional-decline slowing; continue with riluzole.

!!! orderset "Step 2b — tofersen (for SOD1-ALS only)"
    - **Indication:** adults with SOD1-mutation ALS — requires a positive SOD1 genetic
      result (~2% of ALS). FDA accelerated approval April 2023 on the NfL biomarker.
    - **Qalsody 100 mg/15 mL intrathecal bolus over 1–3 minutes.** Loading doses on
      days 1, 15, and 29, then **every 28 days** indefinitely. Remove ~10 mL CSF before
      each injection; warm to room temperature before administration.
    - **Why it matters:** VALOR's 28-week ALSFRS-R missed significance, but plasma NfL
      fell 55% vs a 12% rise on placebo (p<0.0001) with 35% CSF SOD1 knockdown — and
      long-term open-label data show early starters declining meaningfully slower
      than delayed starters across function, strength, vital capacity, and
      death-or-ventilation risk.
    - **Watch for:** myelitis/radiculitis, increased intracranial pressure with
      papilledema, aseptic meningitis — labeled neurologic serious adverse events
      (~8–9% in trials); LP-related headache/back pain is common.
    - → Give with riluzole/edaravone as tolerated; this is an add-on, not a swap.

!!! warning "The Relyvrio saga — what to tell patients who ask"
    - **Relyvrio (sodium phenylbutyrate + taurursodiol, AMX0035)** was FDA-approved in
      September 2022 on a single small phase 2 trial (CENTAUR, n=137) after an
      intense patient-advocacy campaign — an unusually thin evidence base for approval.
    - The confirmatory phase 3 **PHOENIX trial (n=664, 48 weeks) failed**: no
      significant ALSFRS-R difference vs placebo (P=.667), no secondary endpoints met.
    - **Amylyx voluntarily withdrew Relyvrio from the US and Canadian markets in
      April 2024.** It is not available to new patients. Existing patients could
      transition to a free-drug program. The company cut ~70% of staff; the drug is
      now being studied in Wolfram syndrome, not ALS.
    - Counseling line: *"It was approved on early data, the big trial showed it
      doesn't work, and it's off the market. The approved options today are riluzole,
      edaravone, and tofersen if you carry SOD1."*

### Spinal muscular atrophy — the drug cabinet

SMA is the motor neuron disease with the most disease-modifying options, and adult
patients are increasingly in neurology clinics (Medscape is running CME on adult SMA
care this month — the population is no longer just pediatric).

| Agent | Who | Dosing | Key cautions |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nusinersen (Spinraza)** — SMN2 splicing ASO | All ages, all SMA types | **12 mg/5 mL intrathecal**; loading days **0, 14, 28, 63**, then **every 4 months** | LP burden forever; thrombocytopenia/renal monitoring per label |
| **Risdiplam (Evrysdi)** — oral SMN2 splicing modifier | ≥2 months | Daily PO: <2 yr **0.20 mg/kg**; ≥2 yr and <20 kg **0.25 mg/kg**; ≥2 yr and ≥20 kg **5 mg** | Take after a meal at the same time daily; the only oral option |
| **Onasemnogene abeparvovec (Zolgensma)** — one-time AAV9 gene therapy | **<2 years**, biallelic SMN1, ≤3 SMN2 copies | **1.1×10¹⁴ vg/kg IV once** | Screen AAV9 antibodies first; **prednisolone 1 mg/kg/day** starting the day before, ≥30 days then taper; monitor LFTs, platelets, troponin-I (hepatotoxicity, TMA, cardiac signals) |

### The survival infrastructure — respiratory, nutrition, symptoms

These orders determine survival more than any disease-modifying drug. Build them in
parallel with Steps 1–2, not after.

!!! orderset "Respiratory — the NIV and cough plan"
    - **Start NIV when:** orthopnea/dyspnea or morning headache/daytime somnolence,
      **or** FVC <50% predicted (consider at <80% with symptoms), **or** MIP <60 cmH₂O,
      **or** sniff nasal pressure <40 cmH₂O, **or** nocturnal SpO₂ <90% for ≥5% of the night.
    - **Cough assist (mechanical insufflation-exsufflation)** when peak cough flow
      <270 L/min; below 160 L/min the cough is ineffective — do not wait for pneumonia.
    - NIV is the single largest survival intervention in ALS. Refer to pulmonary
      **before** the crisis; the evaluation visit should happen while FVC is still >50%.

!!! orderset "Nutrition — the PEG plan"
    - **Offer PEG when:** dysphagia with weight loss (≥10% baseline), prolonged
      mealtimes, or aspiration events — **place it before FVC falls below 50%** if
      possible (anesthesia risk climbs steeply below that line).
    - Videofluoroscopy when the bedside screen is abnormal; dietitian at every ALS
      clinic visit; weigh the patient every visit without exception.

!!! orderset "Symptom control — run all of these"
    - **Sialorrhea:** glycopyrrolate **1–2 mg PO TID–QID** (max 8 mg/day) → atropine
      1% drops **1–2 drops SL q4–6h** → scopolamine **1.5 mg patch q72h** → botulinum
      toxin to salivary glands → low-dose radiotherapy as last resort.
    - **Pseudobulbar affect:** dextromethorphan/quinidine (Nuedexta) **20/10 mg — 1
      capsule daily ×7 days, then 1 capsule q12h**; QTc caution from quinidine.
      SSRIs are a second-line option for PBA.
    - **Spasticity:** baclofen titrated to effect; tizanidine if sedation is an issue;
      intrathecal baclofen pump for severe refractory spasticity.
    - **Cramps:** trial quinine is out — use mexiletine **150 mg BID** (cardiac
      monitoring) or levetiracetam; stretch and hydration first.
    - **Pain:** NSAIDs/acetaminophen → gabapentinoids for neuropathic components →
      opioids when indicated; do not undertreat end-of-life pain.
    - **Sleep/anxiety/depression:** treat directly and early; screen at every visit.
      Constipation from immobility and anticholinergics needs a standing bowel regimen.

!!! tip "TRICARE — motor neuron disease prescribing"
    Riluzole is generic and typically preferred with minimal barriers — it should never
    be the drug that waits on paperwork. Radicava ORS, Qalsody, Spinraza, Evrysdi, and
    Zolgensma are branded specialty agents and are PA-gated with neurology-specialty
    requirements; expect step-therapy documentation and prior-authorization lead time,
    so start the PA the same visit you decide. *Verify current status at the TRICARE
    Formulary Search before counseling — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Disposition

- **ALS clinic every 1–3 months** — multidisciplinary (neurology, pulmonary, GI,
  speech, PT/OT, dietitian, social work, palliative care). FVC, weight, and ALSFRS-R
  at every visit.
- **Admit** for: respiratory decompensation, aspiration pneumonia, dehydration from
  dysphagia, fall with injury, or caregiver crisis. Most ALS care is outpatient until
  it suddenly isn't — the threshold for admission is lower than it looks.
- **Goals-of-care conversations early and explicitly:** NIV vs tracheostomy with
  invasive ventilation, PEG acceptance, code status, hospice triggers. Document while
  speech is intact.
- **PMA/PLS:** same clinic structure; PLS patients need less respiratory urgency but
  equal spasticity and mobility management.

!!! military "Military medicine — motor neuron disease"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03**, **DoDI 1332.18**,
    service deployment/aeromedical policy (and any relevant JTS CPG) before counseling.*

    - **Deployability:** ALS/PMA are progressive neurodegenerative diseases — not
      deployable once diagnosed; duty limitations begin at the workup stage.
    - **Profile (DA Form 3349):** permanent P3 profile typical; functional limits on
      lifting, carrying, marching, and duties requiring reliable speech, swallow, or
      respiratory reserve.
    - **EPTS / LOD:** document onset and duty status carefully; ALS in service members
      is rare and every case will be scrutinized — the clinical timeline is the evidence.
    - **Retention (AR 40-501 / DoDI 6130.03):** progressive motor neuron disease fails
      retention standards → MEB/IDES referral; initiate promptly rather than watching decline.
    - **MEB / IDES:** refer at diagnosis; the documentation that carries the board is
      the neurologist's longitudinal record — ALSFRS-R trend, FVC trend, and the
      genetic result.
    - **Aeromedical / special duty:** not waiverable for continued flight or special
      duty; ground/remove from status.
    - **VA note:** VA presumes ALS service-connected for veterans with ≥90 days of
      continuous active duty (any era) — and rates ALS at 100% disabling. Make sure the
      service member knows this before separation; the VSO conversation should start
      during the MEB, not after discharge.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

*Written from primary guidelines and trials; source articles are a reading list, not copied.*

- **The ALS drug story of the last decade is tofersen and Relyvrio in opposite
  directions.** Tofersen earned the first accelerated approval for a genetic cause of
  ALS on a biomarker (NfL) after missing its clinical primary — and the long-term
  open-label data now show early treatment meaningfully slowing decline, validating
  the biomarker-first bet. Relyvrio took the opposite path: approved on a thin phase 2
  dataset under advocacy pressure, then killed by its own confirmatory trial and
  withdrawn in 2024. The lesson for the next ALS drug: confirmatory data before
  celebration.
- **Genetics is now therapeutic, not just prognostic.** Every ALS patient gets a panel;
  SOD1 opens the tofersen door, and presymptomatic SOD1 carriers are in the ATLAS
  trial testing whether treatment before symptoms changes the disease course.
- **The ALS pipeline remains a graveyard** — anti-TREM2 (Novartis, ASTRALS phase 2
  miss), NX210c (SEALS phase 2 biomarker miss), and others in 2025–2026. Counsel
  trial participation honestly: most ALS trials fail, and that is still worth doing.
- **Survival is won in the symptom clinic**, not the infusion suite: NIV timing,
  PEG before FVC <50%, sialorrhea control, and early palliative integration move the
  needle more than any current disease-modifying drug except possibly early tofersen.

**Key sources:** Shefner et al. Gold Coast criteria 2020; Miller et al. VALOR (NEJM
2022) and long-term tofersen OLE (JAMA Neurol 2025); CENTAUR and PHOENIX trials for the
Relyvrio record; AAN/EFNS/EAN/NICE respiratory guidance for NIV thresholds.
