# Seizure & status epilepticus

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: July 2026</span>

**One-liner:** a seizure is sudden, hypersynchronous cortical firing; **status epilepticus is the
neurologic code** — treat it on a **clock**, escalate on schedule, and **don't under-dose the
benzo** (the single most common mistake). This page covers the code, the first-seizure work-up, and
picking an antiseizure medication (ASM).

> *The ER consult usually sounds like:* "Known-or-unknown epileptic, two witnessed seizures, got 4 of
> Ativan, now not responding — should I load them with something, and you're admitting, right?" The
> answer to "load them?" is **yes, now** — and check whether that "not responding" is **postictal or
> ongoing *nonconvulsive* status.**

!!! danger "🚩 Status epilepticus — recognize it and start the clock"
    - **Definition (ILAE two-timepoint):** treat at **t1** (the seizure won't self-terminate) —
      **5 min for a generalized tonic-clonic** seizure, but **10 min for focal SE with impaired
      awareness** and 10–15 min for absence; **or ≥2 seizures without recovery** between them. **t2**
      (risk of long-term injury) is ~30 min for GTC. Bottom line: **GTC at 5 min = treat now**, and
      don't wait 30 minutes for anything.
    - **"Stopped but won't wake up" = nonconvulsive status until proven otherwise** — get **urgent
      cEEG.** Persistent altered consciousness after visible convulsions stop is NCSE in up to ~20%.
    - **Under-dosing benzodiazepines** (and then blaming the "refractory" seizure) is the classic
      error — give the **full weight-based dose.**
    - **Find and fix the cause in parallel** — a seizure from **hypoglycemia, hyponatremia, a toxin,
      or eclampsia** won't stop on ASMs until the cause is corrected.

## Status epilepticus — the timed escalation ladder (the code)

!!! orderset "Step 0 — Stabilize (0–5 min)"
    - **ABCs**, O₂, position, suction; **IV access ×2**; **continuous ECG, BP, SpO₂.**
    - **Point-of-care glucose** — if low, **D50W 50 mL IV** (or local IV dextrose protocol), with prompt
      glucose recheck. Give thiamine promptly in malnutrition/alcohol-use risk, **but never delay
      correction of hypoglycemia while waiting for thiamine**.
        - **100 mg IV is prophylaxis.** If Wernicke encephalopathy is *suspected* rather than merely
          being prevented, that dose is inadequate — give the **treatment** regimen instead:
          **thiamine 500 mg IV over 30 min every 8 h**. See
          [rapidly progressive dementia](../dementia/rapidly-progressive.md#management).
    - **STAT labs:** CBC, BMP, Ca, Mg, glucose, **ASM levels**, ± LFTs, tox screen (blood + urine),
      ABG, β-hCG, PO₄; **ECG.**

!!! orderset "Step 1 — First-line benzodiazepine (5–20 min) — give the FULL dose"
    Pick by access; **these are frequently under-dosed** — use the real weight-based dose and **repeat
    once** if still seizing.

    - **IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg/dose), may repeat ×1** — first choice if IV is in.
    - **No IV? IM midazolam 10 mg** (>40 kg) — **RAMPART** showed IM midazolam is **at least as good as
      IV lorazepam** and faster to give; also **intranasal/buccal midazolam.**
    - **IV diazepam 0.15–0.2 mg/kg (max 10 mg)**, or **rectal diazepam 0.2 mg/kg** if no IV.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Second-line ASM (20–40 min) — the three are EQUIVALENT (ESETT)"
    **ESETT (NEJM 2019) found levetiracetam, fosphenytoin, and valproate stop ~45–47% of
    benzo-refractory status — no winner.** So **pick by the patient**, give the full load IV:

    - **Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV (max 4500 mg)** — fewest interactions/contraindications; a good
      default (esp. cardiac/hepatic disease, pregnancy).
    - **Fosphenytoin 20 mg PE/kg IV (ESETT maximum 1500 mg PE; ≤150 mg PE/min)** — **avoid** in hypotension/arrhythmia; watch
      the BP and QT.
    - **Valproate 40 mg/kg IV (ESETT maximum 3000 mg)** — **avoid** in hepatic disease and
      pregnancy/childbearing potential.
    - *(Phenobarbital 15 mg/kg IV if none of the above is available.)*
    - **Broadening landscape:** **IV lacosamide** and **brivaracetam** are increasingly used as
      second-line (lacosamide was **non-inferior to fosphenytoin** for nonconvulsive seizures in
      TRENdS, with a **favorable hemodynamic profile**) — reasonable alternatives, though the formal
      evidence base still rests on the ESETT triad.
    - **Continue the second-line agent** as you escalate; **arrange cEEG.**

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Refractory status (>40 min, or 2 lines failed) — anesthetize + cEEG"
    **Intubate**, admit to **ICU**, start **continuous cEEG**, and begin a **continuous anesthetic
    infusion.** **Target seizure suppression, not routine burst-suppression** — induced burst
    suppression wasn't associated with better outcomes (Fisch 2023), and seizure suppression alone
    tracked with better function; reserve burst-suppression for **seizures recurring despite adequate
    anesthetic dosing.**

    - **Midazolam** — load 0.2 mg/kg, then 0.05–2.9 mg/kg/h.
    - **Propofol** — load ~1–2 mg/kg, then 1–15 mg/kg/h (watch **propofol-infusion syndrome** —
      **avoid in suspected mitochondrial disease or on a ketogenic diet**; limit dose/duration).
    - **Ketamine** — increasingly used (NMDA-mediated late status), usually **with** a
      GABAergic agent.
    - Keep the second-line ASM on board for maintenance.

!!! orderset "Step 4 — Super-refractory (>24 h, or recurs on weaning)"
    - **Pentobarbital/thiopental** infusion (load 5 mg/kg → titrate; **hypotension is expected** —
      pressors ready), titrated to seizure/burst suppression.
    - **Hunt hard for the cause** — **autoimmune/paraneoplastic (NORSE/FIRES)**, CNS infection,
      PRES/RCVS, toxic-metabolic; consider **immunotherapy** (steroids/IVIg/PLEX) empirically in NORSE.
    - **Ketogenic diet** is used adjunctively in super-refractory SE (especially **FIRES/NORSE**).
    - **Don't reach for unproven add-ons:** **hypothermia adds nothing** beyond standard therapy, and
      the **allopregnanolone (brexanolone) RCT was negative** — no proven pharmacologic agent exists
      for super-refractory SE beyond anesthetics (+ immunotherapy for autoimmune causes).
    - Wean anesthetics slowly once seizure-free ~24–48 h; neurology + epileptology on board.

!!! orderset "Copy-paste — first 20 minutes"
    - **Glucose now**; D50 + thiamine if low.
    - **Lorazepam 4 mg IV** (or **midazolam 10 mg IM** if no IV) — **repeat ×1** if seizing at ~5 min.
    - **Levetiracetam 60 mg/kg IV** (or fosphenytoin/valproate by patient) as the seizure continues.
    - **cEEG, ICU, and treat the cause.** No sub-therapeutic benzos, no delay to second-line.

!!! warning "Toxin-driven seizures — the antidote matters"
    - **INH (isoniazid):** benzodiazepine **+ pyridoxine (vitamin B6) ~70 mg/kg, max 5 g.**
    - **Tricyclic antidepressants:** wide QRS → **sodium bicarbonate.**
    - **Organophosphates:** **atropine + pralidoxime** (+ benzodiazepine).
    - **Alcohol/benzo withdrawal:** escalating benzodiazepines. **Eclampsia: magnesium** (+ deliver).

## Is it actually a seizure? (mimics)

Before you commit to lifelong ASMs, exclude the imitators — **syncope** (convulsive syncope after a
faint; get an ECG/cardiac workup — cardiac syncope can kill), **psychogenic non-epileptic seizures
(PNES)**, TIA, transient global amnesia, migraine, and sleep disorders.

- **PNES tells (not diagnostic alone; confirm with video-EEG):** **side-to-side head shaking**, eye
  **closure** (epileptic seizures usually have eyes open), pelvic thrusting, **asynchronous** flailing,
  a **waxing–waning** course, prolonged duration, crying/stuttering, **preserved awareness despite
  bilateral movements**, and resistance to eye opening. Manage with **diagnosis delivery + behavioral
  health**, not more ASMs.

## First seizure — work-up & whether to treat

!!! orderset "First unprovoked seizure — the work-up (outpatient is OK if back to baseline, no deficits, low infection concern)"
    - **Labs:** CBC, BMP, Ca/Mg, glucose, LFTs, tox, ± ESR/CRP; **β-hCG.**
    - **Imaging:** **MRI brain (with & without contrast) is the study of choice**; CT in the ED (and
      before LP) to exclude a bleed/mass.
    - **EEG** — best **within 48 h** (raises yield); urgent if consciousness stays altered.
    - **LP only if infection/SAH suspected.**

!!! tip "Start an ASM after a *first* seizure? — individualize (AAN/AES 2015)"
    Immediate ASM **reduces recurrence over the first 2 years but does NOT improve long-term remission
    or quality of life.** Recurrence after a first unprovoked seizure is **~21–45% at 2 years.**

    - **Treat now if recurrence risk is high:** a **prior brain insult** (stroke/tumor/trauma), an
      **epileptiform EEG**, a **relevant lesion on MRI**, or a **nocturnal** seizure.
    - **Reasonable to wait** in a low-risk, normal-work-up first seizure — with a shared decision
      (weigh ASM adverse effects, ~7–31%).
    - An **acute *provoked* (symptomatic) seizure** — fix the provocation; long-term ASM usually isn't
      needed.

## Picking an ASM (by seizure type & the patient)

!!! orderset "Match the drug to the seizure *and* the person"
    **SANAD II reset the defaults** (Lancet 2021): **lamotrigine is first-line for focal epilepsy**
    (beat levetiracetam on treatment failure, tolerability, and cost), and **valproate is the most
    effective for generalized epilepsy** (with the childbearing caveat). **Levetiracetam is the
    alternative when you need *rapid* titration** (lamotrigine must be titrated slowly for rash) — e.g.,
    the ED first seizure of unknown type.

    | Situation | Preferred → alternative |
    |---|---|
    | **Focal (most adults)** | **Lamotrigine** (best tolerated/cost) → **levetiracetam** (when rapid titration needed) or **lacosamide**; oxcarbazepine/carbamazepine |
    | **Generalized (GTC)** | **Valproate** (most effective) → **lamotrigine** or **levetiracetam** *(avoid valproate when clinically feasible in people who could become pregnant; involve an epilepsy specialist and do not abruptly stop effective therapy during pregnancy)* |
    | **Absence** | Ethosuximide, valproate, lamotrigine |
    | **Myoclonic (e.g., JME)** | **Valproate, levetiracetam** *(avoid carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine/phenytoin — worsen myoclonus/absence)* |
    | **Childbearing potential** | **Lamotrigine or levetiracetam** — **avoid valproate** (neural-tube defects, low IQ) |
    | **Older adults** | **Lamotrigine or levetiracetam** (fewer interactions, better tolerated) |
    | **Comorbid depression/mood** | Lamotrigine (avoid levetiracetam's irritability); **renal** → lamotrigine; **hepatic** → levetiracetam/gabapentin |

    - **Screen HLA-B\*15:02** before carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine in at-risk (Southeast/East Asian)
      ancestry (SJS/TEN); some guidelines also screen **HLA-A\*31:01** (European/Japanese ancestry —
      DRESS/SJS).

    !!! tip "Drug-resistant focal epilepsy — cenobamate is the standout newer ASM"
        Among the newer adjuncts, **cenobamate** gives the **best chance of seizure freedom** (~32% at
        12 months; out-performs brivaracetam, lacosamide, and perampanel on 50%-responder rate even
        after many failed ASMs). The trade-offs: **titrate slowly (DRESS risk with fast titration)**
        and a **higher overall adverse-effect rate.** Worth it for refractory focal epilepsy.

    - **Refractory (failed 2 appropriate ASMs) = drug-resistant epilepsy** → refer to an **epilepsy
      center.** The **2022 ILAE surgical-referral consensus** is emphatic: **offer a surgical
      evaluation to *every* drug-resistant patient up to age 70**, regardless of epilepsy duration,
      seizure type, localization, or comorbidity (including psychiatric disease or comorbid PNES) —
      plus consider VNS/RNS/DBS and video-EEG.

## Classify it (ILAE 2025 update)

Describe **seizure type → epilepsy type → epilepsy syndrome**. The 2025 seizure classification
uses **focal, generalized, unknown whether focal or generalized, and unclassified**. For focal and
unknown seizures, document **preserved or impaired consciousness**, assessed through awareness
(recall) and responsiveness; describe the chronological semiology. Translate older records using
2017 “aware/impaired-awareness” terms rather than treating them as new diagnoses. Seizure type
still drives ASM choice: some sodium-channel agents aggravate generalized/myoclonic syndromes.

## Counseling, rescue plan & driving

- **Driving is restricted after a seizure** — the seizure-free interval is **state/country-specific
  (commonly ~3–12 months; 6 months is typical)**; some states have **mandatory physician reporting.**
  Document the counseling.
- **Safety:** no swimming/bathing alone, heights, or operating dangerous machinery until controlled;
  sleep, alcohol moderation, adherence.

!!! orderset "Give a seizure action plan with a rescue medication (for cluster-prone patients)"
    Prescribe an **outpatient rescue benzodiazepine** and a written action plan — standard of care for
    epilepsy at risk of clusters/prolonged seizures. FDA-approved options:

    - **Midazolam nasal spray (Nayzilam), age ≥12:** **5 mg** in one nostril. If prescribed and needed, **5 mg** in the other nostril after **10 minutes**. Maximum **2 doses** per cluster. Do not give the second dose with trouble breathing or unusually excessive sedation. [FDA labeling on DailyMed](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2b29422e-54d5-4a49-8522-e9cf752368c3)
    - **Diazepam nasal spray (Valtoco), age ≥2:** use the labeled age/weight device table (**0.5 mg/kg** age 2–5, **0.3 mg/kg** age 6–11, **0.2 mg/kg** age ≥12, rounded to the dispensed device dose). If prescribed, a second dose is **at least 4 h** later. Maximum **2 doses** per episode. [FDA prescribing information](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/211635Orig1s012corrected_lbl.pdf)
    - **Rectal diazepam gel, age ≥2:** use its own age/weight prescribing table and caregiver action plan. Do not substitute nasal device strengths or redosing rules.

    **Write the EMS trigger explicitly.** A convulsion lasting 5 minutes, recurring without recovery, or associated with breathing difficulty needs emergency care. The interval before a repeat outpatient rescue dose does not justify waiting to call EMS.

!!! warning "SUDEP — discuss it early, not just when uncontrolled"
    Sudden unexpected death in epilepsy is **~1/1,000 adults with epilepsy per year, rising to ~6–9/
    1,000 in drug-resistant epilepsy.** Per the ILAE SUDEP task force, **offer SUDEP communication to
    *every* patient at diagnosis and revisit it periodically** (not only the uncontrolled). **Modifiable
    risk factors:** **uncontrolled generalized tonic-clonic seizures** (the biggest), **nocturnal
    seizures**, **medication non-adherence**, and **lack of nocturnal supervision** — target each.

## Disposition

Status epilepticus → **ICU with cEEG.** A first seizure back to baseline with a reassuring work-up can
be **discharged with urgent neurology follow-up, safety/driving counseling, and a decision on ASM.**
Admit for a prolonged postictal state, an unremitting/nonconvulsive picture, a concerning cause, or an
unsafe home situation.

!!! tip "TRICARE — verified rules and the current form"
    Use the exact ASM, strength and formulation in the live formulary. This review did not establish a blanket PA requirement for newer ASMs or unrestricted coverage for every generic. Document seizure type, previous trials and the rescue plan for any requested exception. Do not delay status treatment for outpatient PA.

    [Current PA/MN details, benefit routing and packet](../military/tricare.md) · [Live Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst). Check the exact product, beneficiary and venue on the prescribing date.

!!! military "Military medicine — duty, retention and deployment"
    - **Profile / function:** Restrict driving and duties involving weapons, heights, water, flight or isolated operations after a seizure until the appropriate clinician/authority clears them. Record state-specific driving advice separately.
    - **Retention / MEB–MAR2:** Diagnosed epilepsy: DoDI V2 **5.26.k**, action on diagnosis; provoked seizures: **5.26.j**. Army **3-31i** applies. Distinguish acute symptomatic events, epilepsy and nonepileptic attacks. Use the [retention framework](../military/deployability.md); permanent P3/4 does not by itself select DES versus MAR2.
    - **Deployment / medications:** CENTCOM seizure activity in the preceding year is not waiverable; stable treatment with a year seizure-free may be considered. ASM indication and controlled-substance status matter separately. See [the disease AND drug screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the current CENTCOM rules and waiver packet.
    - **Special duty:** flight, diving and other special assignments have separate standards and waiver authorities; a general duty profile does not clear them.
    - **LOD / EPTS:** document onset, duty status, exposures and aggravation objectively. The designated authority determines LOD/EPTS; neither in-service diagnosis nor a genetic finding settles it automatically.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

- **Treat status on a clock, not on a hunch.** The **AES 2016** algorithm: stabilize (0–5 min) →
  **full-dose benzo (5–20 min)** → **second-line ASM (20–40 min)** → **anesthetize + cEEG (>40 min).**
- **The second-line three are equivalent (ESETT 2019)** — levetiracetam ≈ fosphenytoin ≈ valproate
  (~45–47% each); **choose by comorbidity**, and give the **full** weight-based load. Levetiracetam is
  the cleanest default.
- **Benzos are under-dosed** — that's the commonest "refractory status" that wasn't. **RAMPART**:
  IM midazolam ≥ IV lorazepam when there's no IV.
- **"Won't wake up" → cEEG** for nonconvulsive status.
- **First seizure:** immediate ASM cuts early recurrence but not long-term remission (**AAN/AES
  2015**) — treat when recurrence risk is high (lesion, epileptiform EEG, nocturnal), otherwise a
  shared decision.
- **Second-line is broadening** — **lacosamide** (non-inferior to fosphenytoin, TRENdS; kinder
  hemodynamics) and brivaracetam are increasingly used alongside the ESETT triad.
- **Refractory SE: target *seizure* suppression, not routine burst-suppression** (Fisch 2023); no
  proven drug beyond anesthetics (**allopregnanolone RCT negative**; hypothermia adds nothing);
  ketogenic diet in FIRES/NORSE.
- **SANAD II reset the maintenance defaults** — **lamotrigine first-line for focal** epilepsy,
  **valproate most effective for generalized** (levetiracetam is the rapid-titration alternative). For
  **drug-resistant focal epilepsy, cenobamate** gives the best seizure-freedom odds (slow titration,
  DRESS risk).
- **Two failed ASMs = drug-resistant → refer for surgical evaluation** — the **2022 ILAE consensus**:
  offer it to *every* such patient up to 70, regardless of type/duration/comorbidity.
- **Give a rescue plan** (Nayzilam/Valtoco/Diastat) and **discuss SUDEP at diagnosis** (biggest
  modifiable factor: uncontrolled GTC seizures).
