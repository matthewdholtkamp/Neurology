# Deployed neurology and evacuation

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** Stabilize what you can, track what is changing, and call the destination that can provide the next intervention. Run consultation and evacuation planning in parallel.

!!! danger "Start transfer coordination early"
    Falling consciousness, a new focal deficit, ongoing or recurrent convulsions without recovery, threatened airway from bulbar weakness, or suspected cord compression requires urgent escalation. Use the applicable theater and JTS clinical guidance. Ask whether the current site and the planned transport team can manage the next likely deterioration.

## Before the first call

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Stabilize and capture the baseline"
    1. Use the local resuscitation pathway for airway, breathing and circulation. Check oxygenation, BP, temperature and glucose when available. Address hypoxia, hypotension and low glucose promptly. Keep oral intake on hold when swallowing is unsafe.
    2. Record last known well or injury time, exam time, GCS components, pupil size and reaction, and motor findings by side. Document seizure duration, recovery between events, respiratory effort, cough and secretion handling as relevant. Record sedation and paralytic timing before interpreting a changed exam.
    3. Repeat the same focused exam after interventions, with any deterioration, and at the agreed monitoring interval. Use findings and times, especially if the baseline was obtained before sedation. A trend with times gives the receiving team more than “neuro unchanged.”

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Pick the receiving capability and call early"
    | Working problem | Ask the receiving team for |
    |---|---|
    | Suspected stroke without local CT | Time-sensitive stroke consultation and a destination with appropriate brain and vascular imaging. Record last known well, glucose and antithrombotics. Confirm hemorrhage exclusion and eligibility before any thrombolysis. |
    | TBI with a falling exam or signs of mass effect | Trauma/neurosurgery advice, monitoring and definitive capability. Use the current JTS TBI guideline and local resuscitation protocol. Report pupils, exam trends and treatment already given. |
    | Persistent impaired awareness after a convulsion with no EEG | Critical care/neurology advice, assessment for ongoing seizure and a destination that can provide EEG and ventilation as needed. |
    | Progressive weakness, poor cough, or severe autonomic instability | A monitored destination with airway and ventilatory capability. Document respiratory trends and airway protection. Refer to the [acute weakness guide](../presentation/acute-weakness.md). |
    | Fever plus altered consciousness or meningismus | A destination that can provide critical care and diagnostic workup. Arrange early empiric treatment according to the clinical protocol when indicated. |
    | Back pain plus new bilateral deficits, a sensory level or retention | Urgent imaging and spine capability. Coordinate precautions and transfer through the local clinical pathway. |

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Package, monitor, and hand off"
    - Use the local transfer request and patient packaging checklist. State the suspected syndrome, intervention needed, current capability gap, and what would change the urgency. Confirm acceptance, monitoring needs, clinician skill level and backup plans with the movement team.
    - Prepare the airway, oxygen, suction, pumps, power, medication supply and monitoring to cover the transfer and foreseeable delays. Secure lines and equipment. Confirm that the transport team can administer the planned rescue treatment under its authorization.
    - Give the receiving team findings with timestamps, glucose, BP and oxygenation trend, access, drugs and last doses, imaging or reports, allergies, and the agreed deterioration plan. Use approved channels for patient data.
    - If communications are intermittent, keep a written record with the patient. Follow standing clinical protocols, keep reassessing, and use the alternate approved consultation route. Document who accepted care and the latest plan.

## Example handoff

“New left arm and leg weakness at 14:05 local. Last known well 13:42. Alert and following commands with worsening left arm drift over 15 minutes. Glucose checked. No local CT. Last anticoagulant dose unknown. Request stroke-capable receiving site and advice while preparing transport.”

## Disposition

Base movement urgency on clinical trajectory and the time to an intervention unavailable locally. Recheck the exam and physiologic stability with the transport team. If deterioration occurs while waiting, notify the receiving team and update the transfer plan.

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Use theater clinical and evacuation procedures for the current episode. Document duty context and injury timeline for EPTS/LOD. Use the [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guide](deployability.md) and [deployment medication standards](deployment-medications.md) after stabilization and recovery.

## Discover

JTS resources cover clinical consultation, patient packaging and interfacility transport. The 2024 deployed stroke paper linked below was written by its listed authors and hosted on the JTS website. Documenting what is unavailable and what the patient needs next helps the receiving team choose resources and prepare for arrival.
