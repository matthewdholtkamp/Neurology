# Deployment — check the disease AND the medication

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Policy checked: 22 November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** “stable on treatment” starts the discussion. It does not finish the deployment screen. The destination, job, resupply, monitoring and consequences of a missed dose all matter.

## Which decision are you making?

| Decision | Authority / action |
|---|---|
| Retention in Service | Current DoDI 6130.03 V2 + Service standards; [profiles and DES/MAR2 guide](deployability.md) |
| Medical readiness category | DoDI 6025.19 distinguishes deployable, deployable with limitations, temporarily nondeployable, and permanently nondeployable; use the formal criteria and readiness system |
| A particular deployment | Current combatant-command/component requirements, assignment and waiver authority |
| Aviation / dive / other special duty | Separate Service standards and waiver process, even if the member can deploy in another role |

See [aeromedical and Reserve Component routing](special-duty-reserve.md) for special-duty and component decisions. For an acute illness while deployed, open [deployed neurology and evacuation](deployed-neurology.md).

**The table below is specifically CENTCOM MOD 18 Tab A.** Do not export its time intervals or drug exclusions to every theater. The controlling theater/component publication at the time of screening takes precedence over this summary.

## Disease screen — CENTCOM

| Condition | What MOD 18 says / what to document |
|---|---|
| **Seizure disorder** | Seizure activity **within the last year is not waiverable**. A stable anticonvulsant regimen with **one year seizure-free** may be considered for waiver; current anticonvulsant use for prior seizures requires review. This theater rule does not replace retention action for diagnosed epilepsy |
| **Migraine** | Waiver when frequent/severe attacks disrupt duty. Include attack frequency, severity, functional impact, response with/without therapy, abortive success and previous/current regimens; neurologist endorsement is encouraged |
| **TBI** | Predeployment evaluation is required for any severity. MOD 18 lists release after **24 symptom-free hours after one mTBI**, **7 symptom-free days after a second within 12 months**, and neurologic **and** psychological evaluation after **three TBIs of any severity**. These are theater screening requirements, not replacements for TBICoE progressive return-to-activity or proof of recovery |
| **MS** | Waiver request must address stability, functional limitations, heat vulnerability and medication requirements. Apply Service retention standards independently |
| **Recurrent syncope / vertigo** | Document cause, control and incapacity risk; recurrent syncope requires waiver review. Vertiginous disorders need review unless controlled on medication available in theater |
| **History of heat stroke / rhabdomyolysis** | Waiver assessment includes recency, recurrence, organ damage/sequelae and ability to perform rigorous duty in similar heat. Do not clear from a normalized CK alone |
| **Stroke / TIA / CVST / dissection** | Assess deficits, recurrence, cause and monitoring; therapeutic anticoagulation or nonaspirin antiplatelet therapy creates a separate medication issue below. A healed image alone does not settle clearance |
| **MG / LEMS / CIDP / NMOSD / MOGAD / inflammatory myopathy** | No blanket clearance based on outpatient stability. Apply general condition criteria plus infusion/injection, immunosuppression, infection, lab, cold-chain and missed-dose risks. Explain rescue needs and whether the receiving location can actually provide them |
| **Progressive neurologic disease / dementia** | Duty and retention assessment comes first. Respiratory, bulbar, mobility or cognitive impairment and dependence on specialty care can preclude deployment even without a named theater paragraph |
| **Pregnancy** | Listed in MOD 18 Tab A §7.A.13 as a condition precluding routine clearance. Route through obstetrics and the component medical authority. After birth, check the separate Service deferment. [Army Directive 2025-02 §4.d](https://h2f.army.mil/Portals/141/pdfs/ARN43152-ARMY_DIR_2025-02-000-WEB-1.pdf) provides a 365-day operational and training deferment for Soldiers who give birth, including deployment and mobilization |
| **Neuro-oncology / cancer** | MOD 18 Tab A §7.E lists continuing treatment, need for specialist exam or lab/imaging during the planned deployment, or less than one year of complete remission as conditions precluding routine clearance. Document remission dates, residual deficits and treatment or surveillance needs |
| **Recovery after cardiac arrest** | Assess the cause and recurrence risk under the applicable theater criteria. Document ongoing cardiac care, any device or medication requirements, and residual cognitive, motor or endurance limitations. The cause and ability to perform duties drive the disposition |
| **OSA** | **Mild** (AHI and RDI <15/h) generally requires no waiver. **Moderate** (AHI 15–30/h) can proceed without waiver when asymptomatic, adherent, and Epworth <10. **Severe** (AHI >30/h) requires a waiver. **AHI >60/h** also needs a deployment recommendation from a pulmonologist, neurologist or sleep specialist. Symptomatic OSA is not waiverable. Complex/central apnea and ASV/AVAPS are nondeployable under this policy. Document PAP ≥4 hours on >70% of nights over 30 days for moderate/severe OSA, and bring rechargeable backup and supplies |
| **Acute encephalitis / meningitis / botulism / nerve-agent injury / new cord syndrome** | Stabilize, treat and evacuate as indicated. Return-to-duty/deployment decisions follow recovery, residual deficits, treatment and current theater requirements; discharge from hospital is not deployment clearance |

MOD 18 general criteria include stability, low likelihood of deterioration/evacuation, feasible treatment, and no more than quarterly routine visits. A diagnosis absent from this table can still be disqualifying under those criteria.

## Medication screen — CENTCOM

| Drug or class | Practical rule under MOD 18 Tab A section I |
|---|---|
| **Therapeutic anticoagulants** (warfarin, apixaban, rivaroxaban; also assess other agents) | The policy calls the named therapeutic anticoagulants **strictly disqualifying**. Do not promise an available waiver. Obtain the component medical authority’s disposition; do not stop indicated anticoagulation to pass screening |
| **Nonaspirin antiplatelets** (e.g., clopidogrel, ticagrelor, prasugrel) | Listed as disqualifying medications requiring waiver review. Medically indicated aspirin use must be documented. The policy lists dabigatran in this section, but **clinically it is an anticoagulant**, not an antiplatelet |
| **Asundexian (Sunharbo)** | Newly approved after MOD 18 was issued. Apply the general anticoagulant, bleeding-risk and medication criteria through the component medical authority. Its absence from the named examples does not establish deployment clearance |
| **Antiseizure drugs for epilepsy or behavioral health** | Medication and underlying condition require review. CENTCOM’s one-year seizure rule applies separately |
| **Anticonvulsants for migraine/neuropathic pain** | Generally **not intrinsically limiting** when the condition meets theater criteria. **Named exceptions: valproate/divalproex, carbamazepine, lamotrigine** still require medication waiver review. A separate controlled-substance, adverse-effect or monitoring rule can still apply |
| **Topiramate / gabapentin for eligible non-BH conditions** | Not automatically disqualifying solely because they are anticonvulsants. Check cognition, balance, heat intolerance/oligohidrosis, stones, sedation and mission fit; topiramate is not a heat-exposure waiver |
| **Controlled substances, CSA I–V** | Waiver category includes benzodiazepines, opioids, sedative-hypnotics, stimulants and other controlled agents. **Pregabalin, lacosamide, brivaracetam, cenobamate, modafinil/armodafinil and lasmiditan** require assessment under this rule even when a different drug-class exception seems favorable. Confirm the current schedule of the exact drug |
| **CGRP antibodies; other SC/IM/IV therapies** | **Injectable medication of any type requires waiver**, except EpiPens. This includes home injections; self-administration does not erase the rule. Address storage, supply and injection/infusion support |
| **Botulinum toxin** | Current/recent use for severe pain is specifically listed; injections also trigger the general injectable rule. Include indication, response, interval, wear-off and consequences of delay |
| **Immunosuppressants / immune modifiers** | Chronic systemic steroids, relevant antineoplastics even for noncancer use, and biologic modifiers are listed. Describe infection risk, vaccines, CBC/LFT/Ig monitoring, and missed-dose/relapse consequences for anti-CD20, complement/FcRn therapies and other immune treatments |
| **Dopamine agonists** (ropinirole, pramipexole, etc.) | Specifically listed. Assess sudden sleep, hallucinations, impulse-control and orthostatic effects; levodopa is assessed on its own indication, effects and support needs |
| **Antipsychotics / lithium** | Specifically listed regardless of the nonpsychiatric reason for use; quetiapine for sleep is still quetiapine |
| **Any medicine needing titration, lab monitoring or special storage; any with dangerous missed-dose consequences** | General medication criteria can require waiver even if the drug is absent from the examples. Include the **indication**, dose, route, stability interval, adverse effects, monitoring and resupply plan |
| **Oral triptan / gepant / beta-blocker / antidepressant** | No universal drug-class clearance. Evaluate migraine or other diagnosis, adverse effects, monitoring and mission. Absence from a named list is not proof of deployability |

**Do not destabilize effective care to make a form easier.** MOD 18 itself favors requesting a waiver over changing the route of a stable effective treatment. Explain the options to the member and coordinate with the waiver authority. Pharmacy coverage and deployment suitability remain separate decisions.

## A waiver packet that answers the question

1. Destination, dates, duty/MOS, austere exposure, medical support and evacuation access.
2. Diagnosis, objective confirmation, event/relapse dates, stability interval, residual examination and functional limits.
3. Drug list with indication, route, dose, last change, adverse effects, cold-chain limits, monitoring frequency and next scheduled treatment.
4. Previous failures and why the current regimen is needed; consequences and rescue plan if medication or a visit is delayed.
5. Supply plan through deployment and transit, storage/power needs, realistic local follow-up and contingency care.
6. Specialist assessment of likely deterioration, incapacitation, mission impact and evacuation risk; current profile and relevant tests.
7. Route through the **Service component medical waiver channel**. The theater/component surgeon or designated authority decides; the treating clinician recommends and supplies evidence.
