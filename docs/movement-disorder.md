# Movement Disorders

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: October 2026</span>

**One-liner:** the high-yield, prescriptive management of tardive dyskinesia (VMAT2 inhibitors),
Parkinson disease motor fluctuations and adjuncts, neurogenic orthostatic hypotension, Parkinson
disease psychosis, Huntington's chorea, and the atypical-parkinsonism (MSA/PSP/CBS) red flags that
should make you reconsider a Parkinson diagnosis. For the on-call neurologist or PCP seeing these
in clinic, on admit, or in the ED.

!!! danger "🚩 Red flags — don't miss these"
    | Pattern | Worry | Do now |
    |---|---|---|
    | Parkinsonism + **early severe autonomic failure** (nOH, urinary retention/incontinence, erectile dysfunction) within the first years | **MSA** — not idiopathic PD | Orthostatics + post-void residual; brain MRI (pons, MCP, putamen); neurology referral — do not anchor on PD |
    | Parkinsonism + **early postural instability with unprovoked (often backward) falls within ~1 year**, slowed vertical saccades / downgaze palsy | **PSP** | Test vertical saccade speed/range at bedside; MRI midbrain (hummingbird sign); neurology referral |
    | **Markedly asymmetric** rigidity/akinesia **plus a cortical sign** (limb apraxia, cortical sensory loss, alien-limb phenomenon) | **Corticobasal syndrome** | Document the cortical sign explicitly; MRI asymmetric frontoparietal atrophy; neurology referral |
    | Acute dystonic reaction, stridor, or dysphagia after starting/changing a dopamine blocker | **Drug-induced movement emergency** | Stop/adjust the offending agent; airway assessment; treat per drug-induced movement disorder protocol |
    | Chorea + depression/suicidal ideation on a VMAT2 inhibitor | **Boxed-warning neuropsychiatric risk** | Urgent safety assessment — do not simply up-titrate; see Huntington's/TD sections |

## Rapid approach

1. **Is this drug-induced?** Review antipsychotics, antiemetics (metoclopramide, prochlorperazine),
   and recent starts/stops. Drug-induced parkinsonism and tardive syndromes are common and reversible
   causes — don't label idiopathic PD until you've cleared the med list.
2. **Is it Parkinson disease or an atypical syndrome?** Apply the red-flag screen above. Atypical
   features change prognosis, counseling, and management — and some (MSA autonomic failure) need
   same-visit workup.
3. **Match the problem to the treatment lane:** TD → VMAT2 inhibitor; PD wearing-off → adjunct by
   phenotype; nOH → pressor strategy; PDP psychosis → pimavanserin/clozapine/quetiapine; Huntington's
   chorea → VMAT2 inhibitor with boxed-warning monitoring.
4. **Reassess atypical parkinsonism longitudinally** — early on, all three atypical syndromes can
   closely mimic PD and each other; MRI signs are highly specific but insensitive early, so a normal
   scan does not exclude atypical parkinsonism.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Confirm the phenomenology** (tremor, bradykinesia/rigidity, chorea, dystonia, myoclonus,
   tics) and **exclude drugs** as the cause.
2. **Screen for atypical parkinsonism** (red-flag box) before settling on PD — document falls
   chronology, urinary symptoms, orthostatics, vertical gaze, cortical signs, and levodopa response.
3. **Treat the dominant problem first** using the tiered order sets below (TD → PD fluctuations →
   nOH → psychosis → chorea).
4. **If parkinsonism is atypical:** work up the suspected syndrome (orthostatics + PVR for MSA;
   targeted MRI review; autonomic testing/MIBG where ambiguous), refer to neurology, and counsel on
   prognosis — disease-modifying therapy for MSA/PSP/CBD remains investigational (see Discover).
5. **Disposition + follow-up:** most management is outpatient with structured follow-up (AIMS for TD,
   motor diaries for PD fluctuations, supine/upright BP logs for nOH). Admit for drug-induced
   emergencies, severe psychosis, or unsafe swallowing/falls.

## Diagnosis / classification

**Parkinson disease** (MDS clinical diagnostic criteria, Postuma et al. 2015): bradykinesia plus
rest tremor and/or rigidity, with supportive criteria and absence of absolute exclusions. A
**sustained, substantial levodopa response** supports PD — but an initial response does **not**
exclude MSA or a PSP variant.

**Tardive dyskinesia:** involuntary choreoathetoid movements (classically orofacial) emerging during
or after dopamine-receptor-blocking therapy. Document a **baseline AIMS** (items 1–7) and functional
impact before treatment; a VMAT2 inhibitor can be used while antipsychotic treatment continues.

**Atypical parkinsonism:** MSA (α-synuclein, glial cytoplasmic inclusions), PSP and CBD (4R-tau).
**CBS names the clinical presentation; CBD names the pathology** — CBS may result from CBD, PSP,
Alzheimer disease, or another pathology, and CBD need not present as CBS. Across clinicopathologic
series, **7–35% of clinically diagnosed cases are misdiagnosed**; the hardest early distinction is
MSA-P vs PSP-P.

## Tardive dyskinesia — VMAT2 inhibitors

Both **valbenazine (Ingrezza)** and **deutetrabenazine (Austedo/Austedo XR)** are FDA-approved for
TD with level-A evidence and similar AIMS efficacy (roughly 2–3 point placebo-adjusted reduction;
responder rates ~33–50%). **There is no head-to-head trial** — choose on dosing, titration
granularity, CYP2D6/hepatic considerations, QTc, renal function, and formulary, not on assumed
efficacy differences. Both are **contraindicated in congenital long QT syndrome** or arrhythmias
associated with QT prolongation.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — start a VMAT2 inhibitor (pick one)"
    - **Valbenazine** — **40 mg PO once daily × 1 week, then 80 mg PO once daily**; 40 or 60 mg
      once daily may be used per response/tolerability. With or without food. **CYP2D6 poor
      metabolizer or strong CYP2D6 inhibitor → 40 mg once daily.** Strong CYP3A4 inhibitor →
      40 mg once daily. Moderate/severe hepatic impairment → 40 mg once daily. Contraindicated with
      MAOIs and strong CYP3A4 inducers; avoid in severe renal impairment.
    - **Deutetrabenazine** — **Austedo XR 12 mg PO once daily** (with or without food) **or
      immediate-release 6 mg PO BID with food**; **increase by 6 mg/day at weekly intervals** to
      lowest effective dose, **max 48 mg/day**. **CYP2D6 poor metabolizer or strong CYP2D6
      inhibitor → max 36 mg/day.** Do not crush/chew/break. **Contraindicated in hepatic
      impairment** (any degree). Unaffected by renal function — preferred in renal impairment.
    - Baseline and serial **AIMS** (same examiner/method); assess eating, speaking, social
      interaction — not just the score. Expect response to build over weeks; symptoms recur toward
      baseline within ~4 weeks of stopping valbenazine.
    - **QTc:** valbenazine — assess QT before dose increases in at-risk patients (effect grows with
      exposure, e.g., CYP2D6 PM or interacting drugs). Deutetrabenazine — labeled QT effect not
      clinically significant within recommended dosing, but avoid with congenital long QT, history
      of arrhythmia, bradycardia, or uncorrected hypo-K/Mg. Review the antipsychotic and full med
      list — neither choice is QT-neutral.
    - → Inadequate response or intolerable adverse effect on one agent → consider switching to the
      other (no head-to-head data guides this; re-baseline AIMS).

!!! tip "TRICARE — VMAT2 inhibitors for TD"
    Both valbenazine and deutetrabenazine are specialty-tier agents that typically require prior
    authorization on TRICARE, usually with neurologist or psychiatrist involvement and documented
    TD diagnosis with baseline AIMS. Agent selection is often driven by formulary preference and
    PA criteria — check which the plan prefers before the patient leaves. *Verify current status at
    the TRICARE Formulary Search — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Parkinson disease motor fluctuations — choosing the adjunct

All three are **once-daily add-ons to carbidopa-levodopa** that reduce OFF time. None is an
**antidyskinetic** — safinamide's trial endpoint of ON time *without troublesome dyskinesia* does
not mean it treats established dyskinesia. If dyskinesia itself is the dominant problem, reassess
levodopa dose/timing and consider **amantadine** (the only agent that reduces both dyskinesia and
OFF time).

| Phenotype | Preferred adjunct | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Troublesome dyskinesia** + OFF time | **Istradefylline** or **safinamide** (paired with a plan to monitor/adjust levodopa) | Non-dopaminergic (A2A) and MAO-B/glutamate mechanisms rank more favorably for low dyskinesia risk; **opicapone carries the highest dyskinesia signal** (↑ levodopa Cmax 43–44%, AUC 62–94%) — use with particular caution here |
| **Non-troublesome wearing-off** (predictable end-of-dose) | **Opicapone** reasonable first pick | Largest OFF-time reduction (~1 hr vs placebo; ON time without troublesome dyskinesia ↑ ~1.1 hr), convenient once-daily; safinamide a reasonable alternative |
| **On a strong CYP3A4 inducer** (carbamazepine, phenytoin, rifampin) | **Opicapone** or **safinamide** — **avoid istradefylline** | Rifampin ↓ istradefylline AUC 81%, abolishing efficacy; label advises against the combination. (St. John's wort also rules out safinamide — serotonergic contraindication — leaving opicapone.) |

!!! orderset "Step 1 — start the phenotype-matched adjunct"
    - **Istradefylline (Nourianz)** — **20 mg PO once daily, may increase to 40 mg once daily**.
      **Max 20 mg/day** with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor or in moderate hepatic impairment; **avoid**
      in severe hepatic impairment and with strong CYP3A4 inducers. No labeled contraindications,
      but can worsen dyskinesia/hallucinations — caution in major psychotic disorder.
    - **Opicapone (Ongentys)** — **50 mg PO at bedtime, no food 1 hour before or after**.
      **25 mg/day** in moderate hepatic impairment; avoid in severe hepatic impairment and in
      end-stage renal disease. **Contraindicated** with nonselective MAOIs and with
      pheochromocytoma/paraganglioma/catecholamine-secreting tumors (selective MAO-B inhibitors
      permitted). Watch for worsened dyskinesia, hypotension/syncope, and interactions with
      COMT-metabolized drugs (catecholamine vasopressors).
    - **Safinamide (Xadago)** — **50 mg PO once daily, may increase to 100 mg once daily after
      2 weeks**. **Max 50 mg/day** in moderate hepatic impairment; **contraindicated** in severe
      hepatic impairment. **Contraindicated** with other MAOIs/linezolid, tramadol, methadone,
      meperidine, SNRIs, TCAs/tetracyclics, cyclobenzaprine, stimulants, methylphenidate/
      amphetamines, St. John's wort, dextromethorphan (serotonin syndrome / hypertensive risk).
      Taper 100 → 50 mg × 1 week before stopping. Avoid very-high-tyramine (>150 mg) foods.
    - → OFF time persists → reassess levodopa dose/timing and adherence before stacking adjuncts;
      refer to neurology rather than combining multiple adjuncts empirically.

!!! tip "TRICARE — PD adjuncts"
    Istradefylline, opicapone, and safinamide are all branded adjuncts that generally require prior
    authorization on TRICARE, typically with neurologist involvement and documented motor
    fluctuations on carbidopa-levodopa. Step-therapy requirements vary by agent — document the
    phenotype (troublesome dyskinesia vs wearing-off) and any CYP3A4-inducer use, since that drives
    the medical-necessity justification. *Verify current status at the TRICARE Formulary Search —
    DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension (nOH)

**Goal:** fewer symptomatic upright episodes — not normalizing every reading. **Before drugs:**
medication review, volume/salt, abdominal binder/compression, avoid heat/large meals/prolonged
standing. Then **one pressor**, chosen largely by **supine-hypertension risk**. Droxidopa (and
off-label pyridostigmine/atomoxetine) is preferred when supine HTN is significant; fludrocortisone
and midodrine are avoided in that setting.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — start one pressor (choose by supine-BP risk)"
    - **Droxidopa (Northera)** — **100 mg PO TID** (on arising, midday, late afternoon);
      **increase by 100 mg/dose every 24–48 hours** to symptoms/BP, **max 600 mg TID
      (1,800 mg/day)**. **Last dose ≥3 hours before bedtime.** Take consistently with or without
      food. **Boxed warning: supine hypertension** — check supine BP before treatment and after
      increases; if supine HTN persists despite head-of-bed elevation, **reduce or discontinue**.
      **Efficacy beyond 2 weeks not established** — reassess continued benefit periodically.
      Carbidopa can blunt peripheral conversion to norepinephrine — assess individual response;
      higher doses may be needed with carbidopa/levodopa. Caution in CHF, arrhythmia, chronic renal
      failure; no dosing recommendation if GFR <30. Avoid with nonselective MAOIs/linezolid.
    - **Midodrine** — alternative first-line pressor: start **2.5 mg PO 2–3 times daily during
      waking hours**, titrate to **10 mg TID**; **last dose ≥4 hours before bedtime** (some guidance
      says 5 hours). Short half-life (3–4 h). **Boxed warning: supine hypertension** — indirect
      meta-analysis shows a greater supine-HTN signal than droxidopa. Contraindicated in persistent
      excessive supine HTN, urinary retention, acute renal disease, severe organic heart disease.
    - **Fludrocortisone** — off-label volume-expanding adjunct (no requirement to try first):
      **0.1 mg PO each morning**; **max 0.2 mg/day** (>0.2 mg/day not recommended).
      Monitor **potassium, edema, weight, supine BP**. **Contraindicated in heart failure**; poor
      choice with supine HTN (effect not confined to upright hours). Adrenal suppression risk above
      0.3 mg/day.
    - → Inadequate on one pressor → may switch agents or cautiously combine (combination ↑ HTN
      risk; limited comparative evidence) — involve neurology/autonomic specialist.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — manage coexistent supine hypertension"
    Supine HTN coexists in a majority of autonomic-failure patients; balance orthostatic
    symptom/fall burden against long-term CV risk.

    - **Non-pharmacologic first:** avoid lying flat after daytime pressors; **elevate head of bed
      ≥10°**; last pressor dose well before bedtime (droxidopa ≥3 h; panel consensus ≥5 h);
      bedtime carbohydrate snack; home BP diary or ABPM to distinguish sustained nocturnal vs
      post-pressor HTN.
    - **If nocturnal supine HTN remains severe (≥180 mmHg systolic or ≥110 diastolic)** despite
      measures, a specialist may consider a **short-acting bedtime antihypertensive** (e.g.,
      losartan or a nitroglycerin patch removed in the morning) — limited evidence; morning BP and
      nighttime fall risk must guide. Avoid reflex prescribing.

!!! tip "TRICARE — nOH pressors"
    Droxidopa is a specialty agent that generally requires prior authorization on TRICARE with
    documented neurogenic orthostatic hypotension and often neurologist involvement; midodrine and
    fludrocortisone are generically available with fewer barriers. Document supine/upright BPs and
    failed non-pharmacologic measures to support the PA. *Verify current status at the TRICARE
    Formulary Search — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Parkinson disease psychosis

**First:** distinguish persistent PDP from **delirium** (abrupt change, infection, new meds, sleep
disruption). If hallucinations are mild with retained insight, drug treatment may not be needed —
review and taper expendable contributors first (anticholinergics → amantadine → dopamine agonists →
MAO-B inhibitors → COMT inhibitors → levodopa last), but **reflex dopaminergic reduction that
sacrifices motor control has little effect on psychosis and worsens function** — the
severity-stratified approach is increasingly favored.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — mild-to-moderate, bothersome but not crisis psychosis: pimavanserin"
    - **Pimavanserin (Nuplazid)** — **34 mg PO once daily**, with or without food; **no titration**.
      Only FDA-approved treatment for hallucinations/delusions of PDP (selective 5-HT2A inverse
      agonist — no dopaminergic antagonism, so motor benefit is preserved). Steady state ~10–12
      days; clinical effect may take **4–6 weeks**; if no benefit by ~8 weeks, discontinuation is
      reasonable. **With a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor → 10 mg once daily**; **avoid** moderate/strong
      CYP3A4 inducers. Caution if CrCl <30.
    - **QTc:** prolongation is a **Warnings/Precautions** item (not boxed) — **~5–8 msec at
      34 mg**, concentration-dependent. Baseline ECG; correct hypo-K/Mg; avoid with known QT
      prolongation, congenital long QT, arrhythmia history, symptomatic bradycardia, or other
      QT-prolonging drugs.
    - **Boxed warning (class effect):** increased all-cause mortality in elderly patients with
      dementia-related psychosis — this does **not** prohibit the PDP indication, including PDP
      with dementia. Monitor peripheral edema, confusional state, falls.
    - → Crisis-level, severe/refractory, or pimavanserin failure/intolerance → **Step 2**.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — severe/refractory psychosis or need for rapid response"
    - **Low-dose clozapine** — most robust efficacy (highest-ranked in network meta-analysis):
      **6.25–50 mg/day** (do NOT use schizophrenia titration in a frail PD patient). Least motor
      side effects. **Boxed warnings:** severe neutropenia, orthostatic hypotension/bradycardia/
      syncope, seizures, myocarditis/pericarditis/cardiomyopathy, dementia-related mortality.
      **The clozapine REMS was removed in 2025 — ANC monitoring was not:** weekly months 1–6,
      every 2 weeks months 7–12, then monthly with normal counts. Off-label for PDP in the US;
      requires close monitoring infrastructure.
    - **Quetiapine** — when clozapine monitoring is impractical and rapid/sedating effect is
      acceptable: start **12.5–25 mg PO qHS**, titrate cautiously. **Off-label; RCT evidence has
      not reliably established efficacy** (possibly inferior to placebo in NMA). Reassess whether
      it is actually helping — don't continue solely because it's easy to prescribe. Sedation and
      orthostatic hypotension are especially problematic in PD.

!!! tip "TRICARE — PDP agents"
    Pimavanserin is a specialty-tier branded agent requiring prior authorization on TRICARE,
    typically with neurologist or psychiatrist involvement and documented PDP. Clozapine (generic)
    and quetiapine (generic) have fewer formulary barriers but clozapine's mandatory ANC monitoring
    infrastructure must be in place. *Verify current status at the TRICARE Formulary Search — DoD
    P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Huntington's disease chorea — tetrabenazine vs deutetrabenazine

Both are VMAT2 inhibitors FDA-approved for Huntington's chorea with an **identical boxed warning
for depression and suicidality** (prompted historically by a suicide in the tetrabenazine phase III
program). **Screen for depression/suicidal ideation before treatment and repeatedly during
titration**; involve caregivers; **contraindicated** in actively suicidal or untreated/inadequately
treated depression. Neither is disease-modifying. **No blinded head-to-head trial** establishes
superior efficacy — choose on dosing/adherence, tolerability, CYP2D6 simplicity, hepatic function,
and cost.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — start chorea treatment (pick one VMAT2 inhibitor)"
    - **Deutetrabenazine** (often the practical first pick — simpler CYP2D6 handling, BID or
      once-daily XR): **Austedo XR 12 mg PO once daily** (with or without food) **or
      immediate-release 6 mg PO BID with food**; **+6 mg/day at weekly intervals**; **max
      48 mg/day**. **CYP2D6 poor metabolizer or strong CYP2D6 inhibitor (fluoxetine, paroxetine,
      quinidine, bupropion) → flat cap 36 mg/day** (~3-fold active-metabolite AUC rise). No routine
      genotyping required. **Contraindicated in hepatic impairment.** Do not combine with another
      VMAT2 inhibitor, an MAOI, or reserpine.
    - **Tetrabenazine** (reasonable when tolerated/working, or when cost/access favors the
      generic): **12.5 mg PO each morning × 1 week → 12.5 mg PO BID week 2 → +12.5 mg/day at
      weekly intervals**; split BID, and **TID once ≥37.5 mg/day**. **CYP2D6 genotyping required
      before exceeding 50 mg/day:** EM/IM → **max 100 mg/day, 37.5 mg single dose**; PM or strong
      CYP2D6 inhibitor → **max 50 mg/day, 25 mg single dose**. **Contraindicated in hepatic
      impairment.** Do not combine with another VMAT2 inhibitor, an MAOI, or reserpine.
    - **Overnight conversion** tetrabenazine → deutetrabenazine is supported (ARC-HD): start
      deutetrabenazine the day after stopping tetrabenazine using the conversion table; chorea
      control is preserved and improves with titration. No taper needed for either agent; re-titrate
      deutetrabenazine if interrupted >1 week.
    - Monitor: depression/suicidality, sedation, akathisia/restlessness, parkinsonism/rigidity,
      swallowing, falls — and whether reduced chorea actually improves function. Review QT risk and
      sedating/QT-prolonging co-meds.

!!! tip "TRICARE — Huntington's chorea agents"
    Deutetrabenazine (branded) generally requires prior authorization on TRICARE with neurologist
    involvement and documented Huntington's chorea; generic tetrabenazine typically has fewer
    formulary barriers and may be preferred on cost grounds. The boxed-warning depression screening
    should be documented regardless of agent. *Verify current status at the TRICARE Formulary
    Search — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Atypical parkinsonism — MSA, PSP, CBS: the discriminator table

Use this at the bedside when parkinsonism doesn't behave like PD. **Early severe autonomic
failure, recurrent unprovoked falls, or cortical signs** should prompt reassessment — and a
**sustained, substantial levodopa response** supports PD, though an initial response doesn't exclude
MSA or PSP-parkinsonism.

| Feature | MSA | PSP | CBS/CBD |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earliest distinguishing pointer** | **Early severe autonomic failure** — nOH, erectile dysfunction, urinary urgency/retention (**PVR >100 mL within first 5 years**); up to ~50% lack it at onset | **Vertical supranuclear gaze palsy (especially downgaze)** / slowed vertical saccades; **early postural instability with backward falls** | **Markedly asymmetric** limb rigidity, dystonia, myoclonus **plus a cortical sign** — limb apraxia, cortical sensory loss, alien-limb phenomenon |
| **Levodopa response** | Poor (initial response in ~30%) | Poor (PSP-parkinsonism may briefly resemble PD) | Poor |
| **Autonomic involvement** | **Prominent, rapidly progressive** (preganglionic/central; MIBG usually normal/mildly reduced — up to 1/3 reduced, limiting specificity) | Absent/mild — predominant early autonomic failure points *away* from PSP | Absent/mild — not a defining feature |
| **Other bedside pointers** | Cerebellar signs, stridor, disproportionate antecollis, early marked dysarthria, jerky postural minipolymyoclonus, pyramidal signs | Axial rigidity, "careless"/reckless gait, rocket sign, frontal-executive/verbal-fluency impairment; **RBD argues against tauopathy** (toward synucleinopathy) | Limb dystonia + falls favor non-Alzheimer (CBD/PSP) pathology; **asymmetry alone does not separate CBS from PD** |
| **Characteristic MRI sign** | **Hot cross bun** (cruciform pontine T2), pontine/MCP/cerebellar atrophy; putaminal atrophy or iron-sensitive signal change (MSA-P) | **Hummingbird/penguin** (sagittal midbrain atrophy, pontine sparing), **morning-glory/Mickey-Mouse** (axial), SCP atrophy; **MRPI** highly sensitive/specific for PSP-RS | **Asymmetric posterior frontal–parietal/perirolandic atrophy**, contralateral to the more affected limb; perirolandic + temporal sparing distinguishes CBD from PSP (~91% accuracy) |
| **Pathology** | α-synuclein (glial cytoplasmic inclusions) | 4R-tau | 4R-tau (CBS = syndrome; CBD = pathology) |

**Early MSA-P discriminator (Mov Disord 2019):** postural instability (H&Y ≥3) + orthostatic
hypotension + overactive bladder + urinary retention at early disease — **≥2 of these 4 features
yields high MSA-P probability (AUC 0.94).**

**Two diagnostic traps:** (1) the **hot-cross-bun sign is not specific to MSA** (spinocerebellar
ataxias and others produce it); (2) **normal early MRI does not rule out** an atypical syndrome —
MRI signs are highly specific but insensitive early.

**Workup when atypical parkinsonism is suspected:** document chronology of falls, urinary symptoms,
and levodopa response; examine vertical saccade speed/range, cerebellar function, limb
praxis/cortical sensation; orthostatic vitals + post-void residual; MRI targeted to midbrain, pons,
cerebellar peduncles, putamen, and asymmetric frontoparietal cortex; consider autonomic testing,
cardiac MIBG, and planimetric MRI metrics (MRPI, pons:midbrain ratio) in ambiguous cases.

!!! tip "TRICARE — atypical parkinsonism workup"
    Brain MRI, autonomic testing, and DaTscan/MIBG where indicated are generally covered with
    appropriate diagnosis coding and neurology referral; advanced imaging (planimetric MRI analysis)
    may need prior authorization depending on the facility. *Verify current status at the TRICARE
    Formulary Search — DoD P&T revises tiers periodically.*

## Disposition

- **Outpatient** for TD, PD fluctuations, nOH titration, PDP, and Huntington's chorea — with
  structured follow-up (AIMS, motor diaries, BP logs, psychosis target-symptom tracking).
- **Admit or urgent referral** for: drug-induced movement emergencies (dystonic reaction with
  airway concern), severe/crisis psychosis, active suicidality on a VMAT2 inhibitor, injurious
  falls or unsafe swallowing in atypical parkinsonism, or malignant supine hypertension.
- **Atypical parkinsonism:** early neurology referral; counsel that disease-modifying therapy is
  investigational — see Discover for the ATH434 signal.

!!! military "Military medicine — movement disorders"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03**, **DoDI 1332.18**, service
    deployment/aeromedical policy (and any relevant JTS CPG) before counseling.*

    - **Deployability:** controlled PD on a stable oral regimen may be deployable; regimens
      requiring refrigeration, frequent titration, infusion, or close lab monitoring (e.g., clozapine
      ANC draws) do not travel well. Sedating agents (quetiapine, lasmiditan-like restrictions where
      relevant) and drugs with driving/duty restrictions affect duty assignment.
    - **Profile (DA Form 3349):** temporary profile during diagnosis, titration, or motor
      fluctuation optimization; permanent profile for progressive atypical parkinsonism or
      Huntington's disease with functional limits documented.
    - **EPTS / LOD:** movement disorders arising in service are generally service-connected in
      onset; document timeline of first symptoms, exposures (dopamine blockers, TBI, toxins), and
      any pre-service history for existed-prior-to-service determinations.
    - **Retention (AR 40-501 / DoDI 6130.03):** progressive neurodegenerative movement disorders
      that impair duty performance fall below retention standards; confirm the current edition's
      neurologic thresholds before counseling — do not quote paragraph numbers from memory.
    - **MEB / IDES:** refer when the condition or its treatment prevents full duty performance
      despite optimal therapy; the board packet should carry the phenomenology, AIMS/motor-diary
      or neuropsychological documentation, treatment trials with doses and outcomes, and concrete
      duty impact.
    - **Aeromedical / special duty:** parkinsonism, chorea, and most movement-disorder medications
      (dopaminergics, VMAT2 inhibitors, antipsychotics) are disqualifying for flying duty and many
      special duties; confirm current aeromedical policy and waiver posture before prescribing.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

*Written from primary guidelines and trials; source articles are a reading list, not copied.*

- **VMAT2 inhibitors are first-line for both TD and Huntington's chorea** — valbenazine and
  deutetrabenazine for TD (KINECT 3, AIM-TD/ARM-TD; 2025 network meta-analysis: both first-line,
  valbenazine most consistent data), tetrabenazine and deutetrabenazine for Huntington's chorea.
  No head-to-head trials exist in either indication; choice is driven by dosing, CYP2D6/hepatic
  handling, QTc, and formulary.
- **PD adjunct selection is phenotype-driven.** Istradefylline and safinamide are the more
  dyskinesia-sparing options; opicapone gives the largest OFF-time reduction but the highest
  dyskinesia signal; istradefylline is unusable with strong CYP3A4 inducers. None treats
  established dyskinesia — that's amantadine's lane.
- **nOH management is supine-HTN management.** Droxidopa and midodrine both carry boxed warnings
  for supine hypertension; the Bayesian meta-analysis (Chen 2018) shows the greater supine-HTN
  signal with midodrine. Fludrocortisone is capped at 0.2 mg/day and contraindicated in HF.
- **PDP: pimavanserin first-line when motor preservation matters** (34 mg QD, no titration,
  4–6 weeks to effect, QTc +5–8 msec, dementia-mortality boxed warning as a class effect);
  clozapine most efficacious for severe/refractory disease (6.25–50 mg/day; **REMS removed in
  2025, ANC monitoring continues**); quetiapine convenient but unproven in RCTs.
- **Atypical parkinsonism is a clinical diagnosis supported by imaging, not made by it.** The MDS
  criteria anchor each syndrome (MSA: Wenning 2022; PSP: Höglinger 2017; CBD: Armstrong 2013);
  the ≥2-of-4 early MSA-P rule (AUC 0.94) and PVR >100 mL are the most actionable bedside numbers.
- **MSA disease modification — ATH434 signal (early, unverified):** additional analyses from the
  randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled **ATH434-201 Phase 2** trial in MSA, presented as a
  poster at the **2026 MDS Congress (Seoul)**, were announced via company press release (Oct 5,
  2026): ATH434 50 mg slowed functional decline by **~52% versus placebo on the 11-item UMSARS at
  Week 52**, with baseline CSF neurofilament light chain as a covariate; baseline CSF NfL predicted
  the rate of decline. **This is company-reported topline from a press release, not peer-reviewed
  data** — verify against the poster/publication before citing the numbers, and treat the CEO's
  "clinically meaningful" framing as company characterization. A Phase 3 confirmatory trial is
  planned.
