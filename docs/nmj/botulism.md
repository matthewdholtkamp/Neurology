# Botulism

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: August 2026</span>

**One-liner:** a **presynaptic toxin** that cleaves the machinery of acetylcholine release,
producing **symmetric, descending, flaccid paralysis** with prominent bulbar and autonomic features
— and a normal sensorium throughout. The treatment is **antitoxin, given on suspicion**, plus
ventilatory support.

**Who this is for:** the ED or ICU provider facing acute descending weakness. Botulism is rare,
time-critical, and a **reportable public-health and potential-bioterrorism event** — recognizing it
fast is the entire game.

!!! danger "🚩 Recognize it and treat before you confirm"
    **The classic picture:** an afebrile, alert patient with **acute, symmetric, descending
    paralysis** that starts with the **bulbar "4 D's" — diplopia, dysarthria, dysphagia,
    dysphonia** — plus **dilated, poorly reactive pupils**, dry mouth, and progressive limb
    weakness, **with normal sensation and normal mentation.**

    - **No fever** (unless from a wound or aspiration), **no sensory loss**, **preserved
      consciousness** — these separate it from stroke, GBS, and encephalitis. Across 332 US cases:
      **99% afebrile, 93% descending, 91% normal mentation.**
    - **Descending** (cranial nerves → arms → trunk → legs) and **symmetric** — the opposite of
      Guillain-Barré's ascending pattern.
    - **Dilated, poorly reactive pupils are present in only ~50%** — highly suggestive when there,
      **not exclusionary when absent.**
    - **Antitoxin does not reverse established paralysis — it halts progression.** **Do not wait for
      laboratory confirmation**, which takes days.
    - **Call your state health department / CDC immediately** — antitoxin is released only through
      public health, and reporting is mandatory.

    !!! warning "Atypical features do NOT exclude botulism — this is the commonest way the diagnosis is missed"
        In the same 332-case series, **17% reported paresthesias**, **13% had elevated CSF
        protein**, and **7% of adequately documented cases were frankly atypical** — unilateral
        cranial nerve findings, or even ascending paralysis. Those are exactly the features a
        clinician uses to talk themselves out of the diagnosis.

        **Botulism is the most-missed of the acute flaccid paralyses.** Physicians' differentials
        in those cases named **Guillain-Barré in 99 and myasthenia gravis in 76**; in one
        restaurant outbreak **all 28 patients were initially misdiagnosed**, four of them as
        psychiatric illness. What actually distinguishes botulism is **cranial nerve palsy
        disproportionate to the weakness below the neck**, symmetry, and absent sensory findings.

## Forms — the route changes the antitoxin

| Form | Source | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Foodborne** | Preformed toxin — home-canned/fermented foods | Outbreak potential; **investigate co-exposed contacts** |
| **Wound** | *C. botulinum* in a wound producing toxin in vivo | **Injection drug use, esp. black-tar heroin.** No GI prodrome; **needs antibiotics + debridement** in addition to antitoxin (avoid aminoglycosides) |
| **Infant** | Spore colonization of the gut (honey, soil/dust) | **The "floppy baby"** — constipation, poor feeding, weak cry, hypotonia. **Treat with BabyBIG, not equine antitoxin** |
| **Iatrogenic** | Cosmetic or therapeutic botulinum toxin — **overwhelmingly counterfeit or unlicensed product** | **Now the fastest-growing category — see below.** Mild and localized with authentic product at labelled doses; **severe and systemic with counterfeit** |
| **Inhalational** | Aerosolized toxin | **Not natural — implies bioterrorism.** Botulinum neurotoxin is a **CDC Category A bioterrorism agent** and an **HHS Tier 1 Select Agent (42 CFR Part 73)** — two separate regimes. Presents as descending paralysis with diplopia, dysphagia, ptosis and pupillary dilation progressing to ventilatory failure, **without fever or altered mentation** |

!!! danger "Iatrogenic botulism has changed character — ask every young patient about cosmetic injections"
    "Usually mild and localized" is still true for **FDA-approved product at labelled doses by
    licensed injectors** — typical cosmetic doses of 4–20 units sit orders of magnitude below the
    estimated systemic lethal dose (~40 U/kg). It is **not** true of the counterfeit and unlicensed
    market that now drives the case burden.

    | Where | When | Cases | Severity |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | Hebei, China (single centre) | Jun–Jul 2024 | **195** | **26.2% severe, 16.4% mechanically ventilated**, 44.6% still symptomatic at 30 days |
    | Guangzhou, China | 2024–25 | 145 | Bulbar-predominant, no deaths |
    | North East England | Jun 2025 | 25 | Seized vials assayed at **370 U/vial against a 200 U label** |
    | Türkiye → 4 EU countries | 2023 | >80 | Intragastric toxin for weight loss; ICU care |
    | US (NY, TX, WI) | 2025 | 3 | **Self-injection of product bought online** |

    **It is product quality, not labelled dose, that produces systemic disease** — counterfeit
    preparations of unknown potency, mislabelled potency, research-grade toxin, injection into
    highly vascular targets (intragastric for weight loss, calves for slimming), and **underlying
    NMJ disease** ([myasthenia](index.md), [LEMS](lems.md)) as a contraindication that is
    frequently ignored.

    **A young woman with acute bilateral cranial neuropathies and no food or injection-drug
    history should be asked directly about recent cosmetic injections — including product she
    bought and injected herself.** Antitoxin is indicated, and delay to antitoxin was an
    independent predictor of poor 30-day outcome in the 195-case series.

## Diagnosis

- **Clinical and immediate** — the syndrome above drives treatment; confirmation is retrospective.
- **Confirmatory:** toxin assay and culture from **serum, stool, gastric contents, wound, or food**
  — coordinated through public health. The mouse bioassay remains the reference standard, but
  **CDC's Endopep-MS mass-spectrometry assay was validated for clinical specimens in 2024** with a
  limit of detection equal to or better than the bioassay and 100% inter-serotype specificity,
  intended for distribution to public health laboratories. **Serum assays typically stay positive
  for up to ~16 days**, and toxin has been detected 11–12 days after ingestion.
- **Incubation:** **18–36 h foodborne**, **4–14 days wound**, **~6 h inhalational**, and a median
  of **3 days** for iatrogenic cases.

!!! warning "A negative electrodiagnostic study does not exclude botulism — and must never delay antitoxin"
    The classic triad is **low baseline CMAP with facilitation on high-frequency (20–50 Hz) RNS**,
    like other presynaptic disorders. But:

    - **Facilitation appears in only 50–60% of patients**, and is typically **far less dramatic
      than in [LEMS](lems.md).** In mild disease it may show only pseudofacilitation.
    - **Sensitivity decays with time.** Post-exercise facilitation and the 50 Hz increment fall
      progressively from the acute phase (days 4–8) through the late post-acute phase (days
      66–80), and can vanish while clinical signs persist.
    - **Single-fibre EMG is the sensitive test** and was abnormal in every patient in a series
      where conventional studies were abnormal in only the most severe.

    Use EDx to support the diagnosis and to exclude mimics — **never to rule botulism out.**

- **Rule out the mimics actively:** Guillain-Barré / Miller Fisher (ascending, areflexia,
  albuminocytologic dissociation), myasthenic crisis, brainstem stroke, tick paralysis,
  organophosphate poisoning (which is the **opposite** autonomic picture — wet, not dry; see
  [nerve agent](nerve-agent.md)).

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Recognize** acute, symmetric, descending, afebrile flaccid paralysis with bulbar onset and
   clear sensorium.
2. **Protect the airway** — serial FVC and bulbar assessment; **intubate early** for respiratory or
   bulbar failure. This is the intervention that saves lives.
3. **Call public health NOW** for antitoxin release and reporting — in parallel with airway care.
4. **Give antitoxin on clinical suspicion** — do not wait for confirmation.
5. **Address the source** — wound debridement + antibiotics for wound botulism; supportive care.
6. **Report and investigate** — outbreak and bioterrorism implications.

## Treatment

!!! orderset "Step 1 — airway and supportive care"
    **The airway fails before the bellows do.** This is the single most important structural fact
    about botulism respiratory failure, and it is why the myasthenia numbers do not transfer
    cleanly: because paralysis is **descending and bulbar-first, airway obstruction and aspiration
    usually precede hypoventilation.** In infant botulism the principal indication for intubation
    was **loss of protective airway reflexes**, not hypoxaemia or hypercarbia.

    - **Intubate for loss of gag or cough, inability to handle secretions, or pooling in the
      oropharynx — regardless of the numbers.** A patient with an intact vital capacity and an
      absent gag needs a tube.
    - **Then monitor numbers serially (q2–4 h while progressing):** vital capacity **<20 mL/kg**,
      NIF worse than **−30 cmH₂O**, or a **≥30% fall from the patient's own baseline** — treat any
      of these as a prompt to intubate **now**, not a threshold to wait for.
        - **No botulism-specific study has validated any of these cutoffs.** The 20/30/40 rule
          comes from a Guillain-Barré cohort; applying it here is extrapolation by analogy, and
          the JAMA consensus statement on botulinum toxin as a weapon deliberately gives **no
          numeric cutoff** and recommends anticipatory, controlled intubation instead.
    - **The vital capacity you measure may be falsely low.** Facial diplegia breaks the mouthpiece
      seal — face-mask versus mouthpiece spirometry differs by ~200 mL even in normal subjects.
      **Use a facemask interface if there is facial weakness**, and accept that some bulbar
      patients simply cannot perform the test.
    - **Botulism-specific predictors of respiratory failure** worth acting on: **postprandial
      hypotension (adjusted OR 8.33)**, moderate-to-severe ptosis, dilated or fixed pupils, and a
      short incubation period. **Sporadic single cases are sicker than outbreak cases** — 85% vs
      42% intubated.
    - **The single-breath count** is a validated triage adjunct for a mass-casualty botulism event,
      where spirometry will not be available for everyone.

    !!! danger "Do not reach for BiPAP in botulism"
        **Non-invasive ventilation should be actively discouraged here** — the combination of
        bulbar failure, secretion burden and aspiration risk that *defines* botulism is exactly
        the profile in which NIV fails and becomes dangerous. This is a genuine difference from
        [myasthenic crisis](index.md), where more than a third of NIV trials succeed. No
        botulism-specific NIV evidence exists, and no randomized trial of invasive versus
        non-invasive ventilation in neuromuscular disease exists at all.

    - Meticulous **ICU supportive care** — the paralysis is survivable with good support and is
      largely reversible over time. Expect **weeks to months**: mean ventilation is roughly
      **56 days for type A and 26 days for type B**, though outbreak medians run shorter (14 days).

!!! orderset "Step 2 — antitoxin, as fast as possible"
    **Adults and children >1 year — heptavalent botulinum antitoxin (BAT/HBAT, equine F(ab')₂),**
    covering toxin types **A–G**, US-licensed 2013, held in the Strategic National Stockpile and
    **released only through CDC via the state health department, 24/7.**

    - **The adult dose is the entire contents of ONE vial as a single IV infusion — it is not
      weight-based.** Exposure–response modelling predicts **>95.9% survival across all seven
      serotypes** after one vial.
    - **Children 1–16 years receive a percentage of the adult vial scaled by age band; infants
      under 1 year a weight-based percentage. Transcribe that table from the package insert at the
      time of use — do not reproduce a paediatric percentage from memory.**
    - Diluted **1:10 in 0.9% saline**, infused slowly with stepwise rate increases; **epinephrine
      1:1000 and diphenhydramine at the bedside throughout**, because the product is equine.
      Verify infusion parameters against the insert, which is the controlling document.

    !!! tip "Do not delay BAT to perform skin testing — this is a change from older references"
        The AAAAI/CDC Botulism Clinical Treatment Guidelines Workgroup concluded that **the data
        do not support delaying antitoxin for skin testing.** Predictive values were poor for the
        older non-despeciated products (PPV 0–56%), anaphylaxis with modern BAT runs **~0.6–1.6%**,
        and **no fatal anaphylaxis has been reported with any botulinum antitoxin.**

        Registry safety in 162 patients: **19.1% any adverse event, 4.3% serious, 0.6%
        anaphylaxis.** *Note the historical **3.7% serum sickness** figure comes from whole-IgG
        equine products and should not be quoted for the modern despeciated F(ab')₂ product.*

    - **A second dose is not routine and not labelled.** But F(ab')₂ fragments clear faster than
      whole immunoglobulin, and **rebound botulism has been documented 10 days after antitoxin in
      intestinal-colonization disease** — where toxin production is ongoing. **Re-progression after
      initial improvement, in wound or colonization botulism, is a CDC-consultation call**, not a
      protocol.

    **Infants — human botulism immune globulin (BabyBIG)**, from the **Infant Botulism Treatment
    and Prevention Program, California Dept of Public Health (510-231-7600)**. **Do not use equine
    antitoxin in infants** if BabyBIG is available.

    - **1.0 mL/kg (50 mg/kg) as a single IV infusion.** Reconstitute in 2 mL sterile water,
      **start within 2 hours** of reconstitution and complete within 4. **Begin at 0.5 mL/kg/h; if
      no reaction after 15 minutes increase to 1.0 mL/kg/h and do not exceed that rate** (total
      infusion ~68 minutes). **Separate IV line with an in-line filter.** Check BUN/creatinine
      before and after. Half-life ~28 days; one infusion neutralizes toxin for **at least 6
      months**, so **defer live virus vaccines (MMR, MMRV, varicella) for 6 months.**
    - **Labelled for toxin type A or B, in patients under one year.** In practice it is given
      empirically before typing returns — which is correct. **For a confirmed non-A/B infant case,
      BAT is the fallback** (licensed A–G, and the registry included a 32-day-old), but that trades
      into equine sensitization and should be a joint IBTPP/CDC decision.
    - **Give it on suspicion, not on confirmation** — and the urgency is quantified: treating on
      hospital days 0–3 cut length of stay by **3.7 weeks**, days 4–7 by 2.6 weeks, and days 8–10
      by only 1 week (non-significant).

    !!! warning "\"Too late\" is almost never the right call"
        **No interval has been identified beyond which antitoxin stops being beneficial.**
        Circulating toxin has been detected in serum **11–12 days after ingestion**; wound and
        intestinal-colonization botulism involve **ongoing toxin production**, so the exposure is
        not a single bolus; and **progressing paralysis is itself evidence that unbound toxin is
        still circulating.**

        Any antitoxin reduced mortality with an **OR of 0.16**; within 48 hours, **OR 0.12**.
        Treatment within 24 hours of onset gave a median of **0 ventilator days against 21**.
        Documented benefit exists at **day 7, day 9 and day 15** in iatrogenic cases.

        **Give antitoxin at any point while paralysis is progressing or exposure may be ongoing.**
        Withhold only when the deficit has clearly plateaued after a single, finite, remote
        exposure — and even then, discuss with CDC.

!!! warning "Wound botulism specifics — and why the order matters"
    **Antitoxin first, then antibiotics.** This is not sequencing preference: **antibiotic-induced
    lysis of vegetative *C. botulinum* releases preformed intracellular neurotoxin**, which is only
    safe once neutralizing antitoxin is aboard.

    - **Penicillin G 20 million units/day IV divided q4–6h** (the labelled clostridial dose;
      penicillin G is FDA-labelled for botulism as adjunctive therapy to antitoxin).
    - **Penicillin-allergic: metronidazole 15 mg/kg IV load (~1 g at 70 kg), then 7.5 mg/kg IV q6h**
      (~500 mg); usual anaerobic course 7–10 days. Metronidazole reduces *C. botulinum* viability
      **without enhancing neurotoxin production.**
    - **Early debridement is not optional** — it removes the anaerobic niche sustaining toxin
      production. Duration is expert consensus, not trial-derived.
    - **Avoid aminoglycosides, clindamycin, lincomycin, polymyxins and tetracyclines** — all impair
      neuromuscular transmission and compound the toxin's presynaptic blockade. An infant with
      botulism became apnoeic and died on gentamicin. **Extend this caution to any incidental
      antibiotic** given for a coincidental pneumonia or UTI, not just the botulism-directed agent.
    - **Think of it in people who inject drugs** — especially black-tar heroin. Wound botulism is
      under-recognized because it looks like opioid intoxication.

!!! note "Antibiotics are NOT indicated in foodborne or infant botulism"
    Foodborne disease is preformed toxin — there is no infection to treat. In **infant** botulism,
    empirical antimicrobials made things worse: hospitalization **20 days vs 12** in a multicentre
    series. Treat the toxin, not a culture.

## Prognosis — and what to tell the patient afterwards

**Survival is now the expectation.** Untreated case-fatality was **40–50%**; with modern critical
care it is around **4%**, and deaths in the antitoxin programmes were attributed to underlying
illness rather than the drug.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Intubation rate** | 67% type A · 52% type B · 39% type E; ~34% across foodborne outbreaks |
| **Ventilation duration** | mean ~56 days (type A), ~26 days (type B); median 14 days in outbreak settings |
| **Effect of early antitoxin** | treatment <24 h → **median 0 ventilator days and 10 hospital days**; >24 h → 21 and 41; none → 28 and 56 |
| **Tracheostomy in infants** | essentially unnecessary — **0 of 60** in a 10-year series despite mean intubation of 21 days |

**Recovery starts long before it finishes, and the page's readers will be asked about this.**
Improvement in strength begins at a median of ~5 days after antitoxin, but ventilatory and upper
airway muscle strength recovers mostly over the **first 12 weeks and continues improving to 1
year.** Resolution of half the original symptoms took an average of **5 months**, and 13 of 14
patients still reported symptoms at 13 months.

**Long-term morbidity is real and is the most under-counselled part of this illness.** At a median
of **4.3 years** after illness, **68% of 217 patients reported worse health than before, against
17% of controls** (matched OR 17.6) — and **mechanical ventilation during the acute illness
independently predicted worse long-term health.** A systematic review of sequelae found **fatigue
in 66%, general weakness in 57%, and dyspnoea in 43%**, with psychosocial dysfunction outlasting
physical impairment.

*Mechanistically, recovery is biphasic: nerve terminal sprouts appear within days and carry
transmission by ~4 weeks, then a second phase returns vesicle turnover to the original parent
terminals and eliminates the sprouts. Serotype sets the duration — **type A is the longest**, type
E the shortest. Single-fibre EMG jitter persists beyond clinical recovery.*

!!! note "Infant botulism — supportive care that actually changes outcome"
    - **Nasogastric or nasojejunal feeding is standard** — 43 of 44 patients in one series needed
      it; transpyloric placement reduces aspiration risk given the impaired gag and ileus.
    - **Avoid cathartics and enemas** — glycerin contamination renders toxin assays inconclusive,
      and treatment must not be delayed to obtain a specimen.
    - **Watch for SIADH** (18% in one series) and for **autonomic instability, which outlasts
      apparent motor recovery** — heart-rate variability stays abnormal for weeks after the infant
      looks better. The one death in that series was progressive bradycardia despite adequate
      ventilation.
    - **Discharge on full oral intake with a strong gag and cough**, not on extubation.
    - Organism excretion in stool persists a median of **5.9 weeks (type A)** after clinical
      recovery — relevant to infection control, not to treatment.

!!! military "Military medicine — botulism"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 2**, and applicable
    force-health-protection / CBRN guidance. See
    [Profiles, MEB/MAR2 & deployability](../military/deployability.md).*

    - **There is no botulism vaccine — say this out loud, because the record may suggest
      otherwise.** The investigational **pentavalent (ABCDE) botulinum toxoid** given to at-risk
      laboratory and Army personnel under **IND 161** (CDC) and **IND 3723** (US Army) was
      **discontinued by CDC effective 30 November 2011** — falling immunogenicity of several
      serotype components, rising local reactogenicity on boosting, and a manufacture more than
      thirty years old. **No FDA-licensed botulism vaccine exists.** The recombinant **rBV A/B**
      successor completed Phase I/II, is **not licensed**, and today mainly immunizes plasma donors
      for BabyBIG.
        - **Do not treat a documented toxoid series as protection.** It does not raise the
          threshold for suspicion and it does not delay antitoxin by one minute.
        - **AR 40-562** (7 Oct 2013), the joint immunization regulation, contains **no botulinum
          toxoid entry** — there is nothing to enforce, waive, or counsel against. Force health
          protection here is **entirely non-pharmaceutical**: detection, avoidance, respiratory
          protection, food and water surveillance, and post-exposure antitoxin.
    - **This is an operational and public-health event before it is a disposition question.**
      Botulinum neurotoxin is a **CDC Category A bioterrorism agent** *and* an **HHS Tier 1 Select
      Agent (42 CFR Part 73)** — two separate regimes, and the second one creates independent
      reporting obligations. A single case is a sentinel event.
        - **What says "release" rather than "home canning":** any case of **inhalational** botulism
          (it does not occur naturally); a **cluster of acute flaccid paralysis with prominent
          bulbar palsy** — CDC's syndromic indicator of intentional release; a common-source
          cluster with no shared food; or an unusual toxin type.
        - **Notify in parallel with treating:** the **servicing MTF** and its preventive-medicine /
          public-health element; the **Public Health Emergency Officer**; the chain as a
          **reportable medical event**; and, where intentional release is in question, medical
          intelligence via the **National Center for Medical Intelligence (NCMI)**. A suspected
          **laboratory** exposure adds a **Federal Select Agent Program** report, separate from the
          clinical one.
        - **How the Soldier actually gets antitoxin: BAT is not stocked at your pharmacy and is not
          ordered.** It is held in the **Strategic National Stockpile** and **released by CDC
          through the state or territorial health department, 24/7.** Call the state health
          department first; CDC's Emergency Operations Center is the backstop. **Make that call in
          parallel with airway management, not after it.**
        - **OCONUS and deployed:** work the same request through the servicing MTF and the
          combatant command surgeon. *The specific OCONUS pathway and any theater-held stocks are
          not in publicly releasable doctrine — confirm locally before you need it.*
        - **A planning note.** Open-source global surveillance for **2017–2024 attributes 56% of
          human botulism outbreaks to Ukraine** — food preservation and cold-chain disruption under
          wartime conditions. Supporting operations in or near that AOR means knowing the antitoxin
          request pathway **before** you need it.
    - **Retention — the hook is 3-31k, not a bare "3-31," and it turns entirely on residual
      deficit.** Botulism is **named in no military medical standard** — it appears nowhere in
      AR 40-501 or in either volume of DoDI 6130.03. Cite it as a reasoned analogy and say so.
        - **AR 40-501 para 3-31k, "Other neurologic conditions"** is the catch-all — *"any other
          neurologic conditions when, after adequate treatment, there remain residual symptoms and
          impairments such as … weakness, paralysis, or atrophy of important muscle groups … as to
          meet the definition … as in **paragraph 3-1**."* Name the 3-1 limb you actually meet —
          normally **3-1a** (duty interference substantiated by the commander). **3-1b and 3-1d are
          usually NOT met** — there is no maintenance drug, no infusion schedule, no indefinite
          monitoring. Do not pad the memo.
        - **DoD floor: DoDI 6130.03 Vol 2 para 5.26.i** — *"peripheral neuropathy or paralytic
          disorders resulting in permanent functional impairment."* **Its permanence qualifier,
          which works against you in [LEMS](lems.md), is the right fit here**, because residual
          deficit at MRDP is precisely the question. **Do not cite 5.26.c** — botulism is
          peripheral and the sensorium is normal.
        - **Not an "upon diagnosis" condition.** Only 5.26.d (dementia) and 5.26.k (epilepsy) carry
          *"Paragraph 5.26.a. does not apply."* Document both 5.26.a prongs — but here "persists
          despite appropriate treatment" means **a documented rehabilitation plateau** after
          antitoxin and supportive care, not a failed drug trial.
        - **The structural difference from MG and LEMS: the diagnosis carries no retention
          consequence at all.** **A Soldier who recovers fully has no permanent profile, no MEB and
          no MAR2** — the temporary profile closes and the episode ends there.
    - **Profile (DA Form 3349) — the convalescence *is* the disposition, so write it properly.**
      Profiles on the **P** factor; add **E** while accommodative paresis, mydriasis or blurred
      near vision persist (prominent in botulism and late to resolve), and **L/U** for residual
      girdle weakness.
        - **Temporary** through ICU care, weaning, and all of rehabilitation. **≤90 days per
          instrument; link every extension** so the days-on-profile count is real.
        - **Do not close the profile at extubation.** Ventilation alone averages **~56 days in type
          A** and ~26 in type B, and **68% of survivors report worse health at a median 4.3 years**,
          with mechanical ventilation independently predicting worse long-term health. Close on
          **documented functional recovery** — FVC and NIF back to baseline, exercise tolerance
          restored, orthostasis and accommodation resolved — not on the ventilator being gone.
        - **Expect the 12-month clock to matter more than the diagnosis.** A long ventilator course
          plus a fatigue and dysautonomia tail will often run out the **administrative MRDP**.
          Anticipate it at the first extension, and watch the **120 / 180 / 240-day** review gates.
        - Section 4 limits, functionally written: no sustained load-carriage or road march; no duty
          requiring reliable respiratory reserve or sustained protective-mask wear; no unrestricted
          work in high heat; **no duty requiring sharp near vision or reliable accommodation**
          (weapons sighting, NVD use, ATC/UAS consoles); no prolonged standing in formation while
          orthostasis persists; no duty requiring reliable speech or swallow while bulbar weakness
          persists.
    - **EPTS / LOD — EPTS is a non-issue here; LOD may not be.** Botulism is an acute intoxication
      with a datable exposure and onset, so there is nothing to date back to pre-accession and the
      6-month accession/retention fork does not arise. **State LOD affirmatively** and record the
      exposure factually — food source, wound, procedure, date, co-exposed personnel, duty status,
      and the antitoxin product, serotype coverage, lot and date.
        - **Anticipate the misconduct question in three settings**, and document neutrally rather
          than conclusorily: **wound botulism associated with injection drug use**; **foodborne
          botulism acquired off duty**; and **iatrogenic botulism after an elective cosmetic
          injection.** Off-duty is **not** the same as not-in-line-of-duty — the LOD authority
          adjudicates that, not you. Your job is a clean, dated exposure history.
        - **A slow LOD is not excludable time** on the IDES clock.
        - **Record the antitoxin as a durable fact.** BAT is **equine-derived** — flag the equine
          exposure for any future re-administration, and watch for **serum sickness roughly 1–2
          weeks out**, after the patient has left the ICU and may be back at their unit. A delayed
          febrile rash with arthralgias in a botulism survivor is not a new infection until proven
          otherwise.
    - **MEB / MAR2 — run the fork, and expect the answer to be "neither."**
        - **Full recovery, the usual outcome: no board.** Close the linked temporary profile on
          documented functional recovery. **Do not write a permanent profile to "document the
          history."**
        - **Residual deficit at MRDP, still meets retention standards but cannot perform the PMOS →
          MAR2**, an administrative review.
        - **Residual deficit meeting 3-31k / para 3-1 → DES.** The permanent **P-3** in e-Profile
          **is** the referral (AR 40-501 3-4).
        - **Do not refer before MRDP** — rehabilitation must have plateaued or the 12-month clock
          must have run. Premature referrals get bounced and delay the Soldier.
        - Document increments a board can act on: **serial FVC/NIF**, ventilator days, weaning and
          ambulation dates, timed walk or exercise tolerance, and **objective autonomic findings**.
          Botulism recovery is gradual and cannot be reconstructed retrospectively.
    - **Aeromedical — temporary suspension, not disqualification. Do not copy the MG/LEMS chain.**
      **AR 40-501 4-27** names no botulism or peripheral-intoxication standard, and **DoDI 6130.03
      Vol 1 6.26.q is the wrong hook — it requires a *chronic* nervous system disorder.** The
      accession hook that feeds 4-27 is **6.26.n**, *"history of paralysis, weakness, lack of
      coordination … or other specified paralytic syndromes, including, but not limited to,
      Guillain-Barré Syndrome"* — GBS being named makes it the closest published analogy for an
      acute paralytic illness that recovers.
        - **Use 4-27g as the structural model**: for Classes 2, 2F, 2P, 3 and 4 its disposition is
          *"until complete recovery without residual neurological deficits or other sequelae."*
          So: **ground on suspicion, treat it as a temporary medical suspension from aviation
          service pending complete recovery.**
        - **USAAMA** reviews and recommends *"qualified, qualified with waiver, or medical
          suspension from aviation service"* and issues the governing **Aeromedical Policy
          Letters** — send the case rather than deciding it. Any condition producing a **PULHES
          code greater than 1 requires a complete work-up per the APLs.**
        - **What keeps an aircrew member grounded after the limbs recover is ocular and
          autonomic** — accommodative paresis, mydriasis, blurred near vision, orthostatic
          intolerance. If **binocular diplopia** persists, check **AR 40-501 3-7b** separately. The
          same findings are decisive for **ATC and UAS operators** (4-34).
    - **Deployability — time-limited, not permanent, and that is the difference from every other
      page in this section.** Non-deployable through the acute illness and all of rehabilitation.
      What actually fails downrange, and outlasts limb strength:
        - **Impaired sweating from cholinergic autonomic blockade** → reduced thermoregulation and
          real heat-injury risk in a hot AOR, invisible on a strength exam.
        - **Reduced respiratory reserve vs sustained protective-mask wear.**
        - **Accommodative paresis, mydriasis, blurred near vision** — weapons sighting, NVD use,
          console and map work.
        - **Orthostatic intolerance** under load and in formation; **dry mouth and dry eyes** in a
          desert or high-particulate environment.
        - **What does *not* fail:** no maintenance drug, no cold chain, no infusion schedule, no
          immunosuppression, no live-vaccine conflict, and nothing that has to be monitored
          forward. **Once recovery is documented, full deployability is the expected endpoint** —
          reassess against sweating, respiratory reserve, accommodation and orthostasis, not
          against ventilator status alone.

!!! tip "TRICARE — botulism: the access path is public health, not pharmacy"
    **There is nothing here to look up on the formulary.** **BAT is not prescribed, not ordered,
    and not purchased.** It is a **Strategic National Stockpile** product **released by CDC through
    the state or territorial health department, 24/7, at no cost to the patient.** No TRICARE tier,
    no prior authorization, no pharmacy-versus-medical-benefit split to reason about. **Do not
    spend a minute of the therapeutic window looking for a way to order it — make the public health
    call.**

    - **BabyBIG is different.** It comes **only** from the **CDPH Infant Botulism Treatment and
      Prevention Program**, and the program **bills for the product** — for a TRICARE-eligible
      dependent infant that is a **coverage** question, not a formulary one. Involve the MTF or
      regional contractor early, and **treat first.** *Current BabyBIG pricing and TRICARE billing
      treatment were not verified for this page — confirm with the IBTPP and the regional
      contractor.*
    - **The rest of the episode is inpatient benefit** — ICU care, ventilation, and debridement
      plus antibiotics for wound botulism. **Do not delay any of it for an authorization.**
    - **Nothing here is a maintenance prescription.** The post-acute cost centre is
      **rehabilitation**, which is where the authorization effort actually belongs.
    - **Verify current coverage at the [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst)
      and with the regional contractor before you counsel.**

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

**One of the most potent toxins known, with a precise molecular target.** Botulinum neurotoxin is
taken up by cholinergic nerve terminals, where its light chain is a **protease that cleaves SNARE
proteins** (SNAP-25, syntaxin, synaptobrevin) — the fusion machinery that docks acetylcholine
vesicles to the presynaptic membrane. With SNARE cleaved, **no acetylcholine is released**, and the
muscle is chemically denervated. Recovery is not a matter of clearing the toxin; it requires the
terminal to **sprout and rebuild new release sites**, which is why paralysis lasts weeks to months
even after antitoxin. The same mechanism, dosed and localized, is what makes botulinum toxin a
therapeutic — from [chronic migraine](../headache/migraine.md) to [cervical dystonia](index.md) —
and what makes MG a **contraindication** to receiving it.

**Why "treat on suspicion" is not just caution.** Antitoxin can only neutralize toxin that is still
**circulating and unbound.** Once toxin has entered the nerve terminal, no antibody can retrieve it.
Every hour of diagnostic delay converts reversible circulating toxin into fixed, bound toxin — so
the therapeutic window is measured against the clock, and the decision to treat is made clinically,
before any test returns.

**There is no licensed botulism vaccine, and this is not widely known.** The investigational
**pentavalent (ABCDE) botulinum toxoid was discontinued by CDC on 30 November 2011** — declining
immunogenicity of some serotype components, rising local reactogenicity with boosters, and
manufacture more than thirty years earlier. It had been given under **IND 161** (CDC, at-risk
laboratory workers) and **IND 3723** (US Army). Its successor, **recombinant rBV A/B**, completed
Phase I and II but has never been licensed; its current job is **immunizing the plasma donors who
supply BabyBIG** rather than protecting anyone directly. **Previously immunized personnel must not
be assumed protected.**

**The treatment pipeline is real but nowhere near the bedside.** Four oligoclonal human monoclonal
antibody antitoxins have completed **Phase 1** with no drug-related serious adverse events — and a
nine-antibody formulation covering A, B and E is **lyophilized and stable at 50 °C for a year**,
which is operationally striking for austere storage. **None has entered Phase 2 and none is
available.** Separately, **3,4-diaminopyridine** — already approved for [LEMS](lems.md) — has
strong preclinical repurposing data, reversing respiratory depression at terminal stages in mice
and, combined with antitoxin, **extending the therapeutic window past the point where antitoxin
alone did nothing.** No human trial in botulism has been reported. Watch this space; do not
prescribe from it.

**Key references:**

- **CDC Clinical Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Botulism, 2021** (*MMWR* Recomm Rep) —
  still in force, with no formal update identified.
- **Heptavalent botulinum antitoxin (BAT)** — types A–G, equine F(ab')₂, US-licensed 2013.
- **BabyBIG** — human botulism immune globulin for infant botulism (California IBTPP). *Now
  derived from plasma of donors immunized with recombinant rBV A/B rather than the original
  pentavalent toxoid; indication and dosing unchanged.*
- **AAAAI/CDC workgroup systematic review** — skin testing should not delay antitoxin.
- **Endopep-MS** — mass-spectrometric toxin detection, validated for clinical specimens 2024.
