# Vascular cognitive impairment

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: August 2026</span>

*Part of [Approach to cognitive impairment](index.md).*

**One-liner:** cognitive impairment caused by cerebrovascular disease — the **only common dementia
with a genuinely modifiable cause**, which is why it is worth identifying even when it coexists with
Alzheimer's pathology, as it usually does.

**Who this is for:** the provider looking at a scan full of white matter change wondering whether it
explains the patient, and the one deciding what to actually *do* about it. The answer is mostly on
the [stroke prevention page](../stroke/prevention.md) — this page is about recognising the syndrome
and knowing which lever to pull.

!!! danger "🚩 What changes the plan"
    | Pattern | Worry | Do |
    |---|---|---|
    | **Cognitive decline within 3–6 months of a stroke** | Post-stroke cognitive impairment | Formal cognitive assessment — it is routinely missed in stroke follow-up |
    | **Lobar microbleeds and superficial siderosis** | **[Cerebral amyloid angiopathy](../stroke/cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy.md)** — not just "small vessel disease" | **Changes anticoagulation decisions and anti-amyloid eligibility.** Apply Boston v2.0 |
    | **Young patient, migraine with aura, family history, confluent anterior temporal white matter change** | **CADASIL** and other monogenic small vessel disease | Genetic referral; do not write it off as hypertensive change |
    | **Gait disturbance leading, urinary symptoms, "magnetic" gait** | NPH vs vascular vs both | These overlap and coexist; do not assume |
    | **Rapid stepwise decline with headache or systemic features** | Vasculitis, endocarditis, hypercoagulable state | Go to [rapidly progressive dementia](rapidly-progressive.md) |

## Diagnosis

**The requirement is a plausible causal link, not merely coexistence.** White matter
hyperintensities are near-universal in older adults; finding them does not make the dementia
vascular.

**What supports a vascular contribution:**

- **A temporal relationship** — decline beginning within months of a clinical stroke, or a
  **stepwise** rather than smoothly progressive course.
- **A matching cognitive profile** — **processing speed and executive dysfunction lead**, with
  memory relatively better preserved and, critically, **helped by cueing** (a retrieval rather than
  encoding failure — the opposite of [Alzheimer's](alzheimers.md)).
- **Imaging burden proportionate to the deficit** — strategic infarcts (thalamus, angular gyrus,
  caudate, genu of internal capsule), multiple lacunes, or **confluent white matter
  hyperintensity**, not a few scattered dots.
- **Corroborating findings** — gait disturbance, early urinary urgency, pseudobulbar affect,
  focal signs.

**Subtypes worth distinguishing** because they behave differently: **post-stroke** cognitive
impairment; **subcortical ischaemic** small vessel disease, the commonest and most insidious;
**multi-infarct**; **strategic single infarct**; **hypoperfusion**; and **haemorrhagic**, which
brings [CAA](../stroke/cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy.md) into the frame.

!!! note "Mixed is the default, not the exception"
    Over 80, **coexisting Alzheimer's and vascular pathology is the commonest finding at autopsy**,
    and the two are more than additive — vascular burden lowers the threshold at which Alzheimer's
    pathology produces symptoms.

    **Practical consequence:** a patient can have both, and treating the vascular half is worthwhile
    even when the amnestic syndrome points to AD. **"Mixed dementia" is an honest diagnosis**, not a
    hedge — and it justifies both a cholinesterase inhibitor and aggressive vascular risk reduction.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Confirm the syndrome is executive- and speed-led**, and that memory improves with cueing.
2. **Image, and read the scan properly** — burden, location, strategic infarcts, **and the
   microbleed pattern.** Lobar microbleeds mean [CAA](../stroke/cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy.md),
   which changes decisions.
3. **Work the stroke aetiology** if there has been a clinical event — this is a
   [stroke work-up](../stroke/index.md), not a dementia work-up.
4. **Attack the modifiable risk factors hard.** This is the therapeutic core of the page.
5. **Screen for and treat depression and apathy** — both are more common here than in AD and both
   are treatable.
6. **Consider a cholinesterase inhibitor** where mixed pathology is likely.
7. **Rehabilitate** — gait, falls, and cognitive rehabilitation where available.
8. **Military disposition** — dementia is disqualifying upon diagnosis; the vascular risk factors
   have their own retention implications.

## Management

!!! orderset "Step 1 — vascular risk factor control (this is the treatment)"
    **Full regimens are on the [stroke prevention page](../stroke/prevention.md); this is the
    cognitive-specific framing.**

    - **Blood pressure control is the single best-supported intervention** for preventing and
      slowing vascular cognitive impairment. Target per current stroke-prevention guidance.
      **Avoid over-treatment causing orthostatic hypotension** in a falls-prone older patient —
      check standing pressures.
    - **Lipids, diabetes, smoking cessation, atrial fibrillation detection and anticoagulation** —
      as for secondary stroke prevention.
    - **Antithrombotics per the stroke indication, not for the dementia.** There is **no indication
      for antiplatelet therapy to treat cognitive impairment itself**, and in a patient with lobar
      microbleeds or suspected CAA the calculus shifts toward harm.
    - **Physical activity** — supported for both vascular and cognitive endpoints.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — symptomatic and supportive"
    - **Cholinesterase inhibitors and memantine are not FDA-labelled for vascular dementia.** Their
      use here is **off-label**, and the effect where present is small. **The defensible case is
      probable mixed pathology**, where you are treating the Alzheimer's component — say that
      explicitly rather than treating "vascular dementia" with an AD drug.
    - **Treat depression and apathy** — both common post-stroke and both under-treated. An SSRI is
      the usual choice.
    - **Gait, falls and continence** — physiotherapy, falls assessment, home safety.
    - **Pseudobulbar affect** — recognise it and explain it to the family; it is distressing and
      frequently mistaken for depression.

!!! warning "Avoid"
    - **Treating white matter hyperintensities as a diagnosis.** They are a finding; the patient is
      the diagnosis.
    - **Antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy prescribed *for cognition*** — no indication, and
      real harm where CAA is present.
    - **Aggressive BP lowering without checking standing pressures** — orthostatic hypotension
      causes falls and may worsen cerebral hypoperfusion.
    - **Anticholinergics and benzodiazepines** — as everywhere in this section.
    - **Assuming "vascular" excludes Alzheimer's.** It usually does not.

!!! tip "TRICARE — vascular cognitive impairment"
    - **Everything with evidence behind it is generic and formulary** — antihypertensives, statins,
      metformin, antiplatelets, and the SSRIs. **This is the cheapest dementia to treat well**,
      because the treatment is cardiovascular risk management.
    - **Anticoagulants** for atrial fibrillation follow the [stroke pages](../stroke/prevention.md);
      DOAC tier and PA status change, so verify.
    - **Cholinesterase inhibitors used off-label** for presumed mixed pathology are generic and
      unlikely to be challenged, but **document the mixed-pathology reasoning** rather than coding
      an AD indication you have not established.
    - **Cardiac rehabilitation, physiotherapy and falls programmes** are usually the higher-value
      authorization requests here.
    - **Verify current status at the
      [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst).**
      *Not independently verified for this page.*

!!! military "Military medicine — vascular cognitive impairment"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 1 and Vol 2**,
    **DoDI 1332.18**, and service aeromedical policy. See
    [Profiles, MEB/MAR2 & deployability](../military/deployability.md).*

    - **The distinctive military feature is that the risk factors are the disposition.** Unlike the
      degenerative dementias, vascular cognitive impairment usually arrives with a documented
      cerebrovascular event or a treatable risk profile — and **the stroke, the hypertension and the
      atrial fibrillation each carry their own retention standards independent of the cognition.**
      Cite them separately; see [stroke](../stroke/index.md) and
      [deployability](../military/deployability.md).
    - **Retention:** if the impairment meets the threshold for dementia, **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 2 para
      5.26.d** applies **upon diagnosis** (*"Paragraph 5.26.a. does not apply"*), with the Army hook
      at **AR 40-501 3-31k** closing on **para 3-1**. **Below that threshold**, the disposition is
      driven by the **stroke or cardiovascular standard** and by residual deficit — which is a very
      different and much more common conversation.
    - **The realistic active-duty case is post-stroke cognitive impairment in a 40s–50s service
      member**, not late-life small vessel disease. **Formal cognitive assessment is routinely
      omitted from stroke follow-up**, and in a service member that omission decides whether a real
      deficit is documented before a return-to-duty decision. **Test it deliberately.**
    - **Profile:** **P** factor, with **S** where cognitive/behavioural effects dominate. Temporary
      through the post-stroke recovery window — deficits improve for months — then permanent at
      MRDP if impairment persists. Section 4 limits: **driving, weapons access, independent duty,
      and any safety-sensitive task.**
    - **EPTS / LOD:** vascular risk factors frequently predate service or accrue during it, and
      **hypertension documented on PHAs over years is the usual evidence trail.** Where a stroke
      occurred on duty the LOD is normally straightforward; where the substrate is chronic
      uncontrolled hypertension, expect the question.
    - **MEB / DES:** if cognition meets the dementia threshold, refer on diagnosis. Otherwise run
      the ordinary post-stroke pathway — **MRDP, then the MAR2-versus-DES fork.** Do not board a
      recovering stroke patient prematurely.
    - **Deployability:** driven by the stroke standard, the anticoagulation requirement if any, and
      the cognitive deficit — **address all three, not just the last.**
    - **Aeromedical:** disqualifying pending **USAAMA** adjudication; the cerebrovascular event
      alone will do it.
    - **The prevention argument belongs in the military box.** This is the one dementia where
      **force health protection genuinely applies** — blood pressure, lipids, tobacco and weight
      management in mid-career service members are cognitive interventions as well as
      cardiovascular ones. It is worth saying that to a 45-year-old whose father had a stroke.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

**The only dementia you can meaningfully prevent.** Alzheimer's disease has a modifiable component;
vascular cognitive impairment is *mostly* modifiable component. Blood pressure control has the
strongest supporting evidence, and the interventions are the same ones already being pushed for
cardiovascular reasons — which makes this the rare neurology page whose main therapeutic message is
"do the cardiology properly."

**The diagnostic trap is the scan.** White matter hyperintensities are so prevalent in older adults
that finding them explains nothing on its own, and "small vessel disease" has become a radiological
shrug that closes down thinking. The discipline is to ask whether the **burden and location** are
proportionate to the **deficit and its profile** — and to notice when the microbleed pattern is
**lobar**, because that is [cerebral amyloid angiopathy](../stroke/cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy.md),
which changes anticoagulation decisions and anti-amyloid eligibility rather than just adding a
descriptor.

**And the honest position on most older patients is "mixed."** Coexisting Alzheimer's and vascular
pathology is the commonest autopsy finding above 80, and the two interact — vascular burden lowers
the threshold at which amyloid and tau produce symptoms. That is not a diagnostic failure to
apologise for; it is a reason to treat both halves, and it is why the vascular risk factors deserve
attention in every dementia clinic, not just this page.

**Key sources:** vascular cognitive impairment consensus criteria; the stroke-prevention evidence
base as carried on the [stroke prevention page](../stroke/prevention.md); Boston v2.0 criteria for
CAA.
