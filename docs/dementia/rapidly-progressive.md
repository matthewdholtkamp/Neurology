# Rapidly progressive dementia

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: August 2026</span>

*Part of [Approach to cognitive impairment](index.md).*

**One-liner:** dementia evolving over **weeks to months rather than years** — a presentation that is
**not a variant of the degenerative dementias but a separate emergency**, because a meaningful
proportion of causes are **treatable and the window closes.**

**Who this is for:** anyone whose "memory clinic" referral has deteriorated between the referral and
the appointment. **The question is never "which dementia is this" — it is "what treatable thing am I
about to miss."**

!!! danger "🚩 Treat this as an emergency, not a clinic problem"
    **Any of these means admit or expedite, today:**

    - **Cognitive decline over weeks to a few months**, particularly with a datable onset.
    - **Myoclonus, ataxia, or new visual disturbance** with cognitive decline.
    - **Seizures**, especially faciobrachial dystonic seizures.
    - **New psychiatric presentation with movement disorder, autonomic instability or hyponatraemia.**
    - **Fever, headache, or systemic illness** with cognitive change.
    - **Known cancer**, immunosuppression, or new focal deficit.

    **The single most important decision is early: is this autoimmune?** Autoimmune encephalitis is
    the commonest treatable cause of rapidly progressive dementia, and **outcome depends on how
    quickly immunotherapy starts.** Waiting for antibody results before treating is the classic and
    costly error.

    **Do not anchor on prion disease.** CJD is the famous cause; it is not the commonest, and
    treating it as the default costs treatable patients their window.

## The differential — work it systematically

A structured sweep prevents the two failure modes: missing a treatable cause, and over-investigating
a degenerative one. Work these categories deliberately rather than by association.

| Category | Examples | Clues |
|---|---|---|
| **Autoimmune / paraneoplastic** | Anti-NMDAR, LGI1, CASPR2, GAD65; paraneoplastic (Hu, Ma2, CV2); **steroid-responsive encephalopathy with autoimmune thyroiditis** | Seizures, psychiatric onset, movement disorder, autonomic instability, **hyponatraemia** (LGI1), **faciobrachial dystonic seizures** (LGI1) |
| **Infectious** | HSV, VZV, HIV, neurosyphilis, PML, fungal, TB, **Whipple**, Lyme, subacute sclerosing panencephalitis | Fever, immunosuppression, exposure, CSF pleocytosis |
| **Neoplastic** | CNS lymphoma, intravascular lymphoma, gliomatosis, carcinomatous meningitis | Focal signs, imaging, weight loss, systemic disease |
| **Toxic / metabolic** | B12, thiamine (**Wernicke**), thyroid, hepatic, renal, hyponatraemia, heavy metals, drugs | Nutrition, alcohol, dialysis, polypharmacy |
| **Vascular** | Vasculitis, **CADASIL**, [CAA-related inflammation](../stroke/cerebral-amyloid-angiopathy.md), multiple emboli, endocarditis | Headache, stepwise decline, systemic inflammation, murmur |
| **Prion** | Sporadic **CJD**, variant, familial, iatrogenic | Myoclonus, ataxia, akinetic mutism, characteristic MRI |
| **Systemic / iatrogenic** | Sarcoid, SLE, medication effect, **chemotherapy or immune checkpoint inhibitor toxicity** | Systemic features, drug timeline |

!!! note "Two that are missed most often, for opposite reasons"
    - **Wernicke encephalopathy** — missed because it is looked for only in overt alcoholism.
      Consider it in **bariatric surgery, hyperemesis, malignancy, malnutrition and prolonged
      vomiting.** **If it crosses your mind, give thiamine** — the treatment is safe and the damage
      is not reversible once established.
    - **Autoimmune encephalitis** — missed because the presentation is psychiatric and the patient
      goes to psychiatry. **A first psychotic episode with seizures, movement disorder, autonomic
      instability, or a rapidly abnormal course is not primary psychiatric illness.**

## Workup

!!! orderset "Step 1 — the immediate set, in parallel, not in sequence"
    - **MRI brain with and without contrast, including DWI and FLAIR.** DWI is essential — the
      cortical ribboning and basal ganglia restriction of prion disease live there, and it will also
      show the medial temporal changes of autoimmune limbic encephalitis.
    - **EEG** — non-convulsive status is a treatable cause of subacute decline and is invisible
      without it. Also looks for the periodic patterns of prion disease and the extreme delta brush
      described in NMDAR encephalitis.
    - **Lumbar puncture** — cell count, protein, glucose, **oligoclonal bands**, cytology and flow
      cytometry, infectious studies, and **paired serum and CSF autoimmune/paraneoplastic panels.**
      **Send CSF for both — serum alone misses cases, and CSF alone misses others.**
    - **Bloods:** CBC, CMP, **thyroid function and TPO antibodies**, **B12 and thiamine**, ESR/CRP,
      ANA and ENA, ANCA, HIV, RPR/treponemal, and drug/toxicology as indicated.
    - **Malignancy screen** — CT chest/abdomen/pelvis, and consider **whole-body FDG-PET** where
      paraneoplastic disease is suspected. Testicular ultrasound and pelvic imaging as appropriate.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — targeted, when the picture points"
    - **Prion-specific: CSF RT-QuIC** — the assay that changed this diagnosis. It detects the
      pathological prion protein directly and has **substantially better specificity than 14-3-3**,
      which was never specific and drove years of false reassurance and false alarm. **RT-QuIC has
      superseded 14-3-3 as the CSF test of choice**; total tau adds supporting information.
    - **Brain biopsy** — reserved for when the treatable causes have been pursued and a diagnosis
      would change management, most often suspected **CNS vasculitis, lymphoma or CAA-related
      inflammation.** A negative biopsy does not exclude.
    - **Genetic testing** — familial prion disease, CADASIL, and the young-onset degenerative
      dementias.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Confirm the tempo.** Weeks to months, with a datable onset, from a collateral historian.
2. **Admit, or expedite to the point of functional admission.** This does not work as a series of
   outpatient appointments.
3. **Run Step 1 in parallel** — MRI, EEG, LP with paired panels, bloods, malignancy screen.
4. **Give thiamine now** if there is any nutritional risk. Do not wait.
5. **Ask the autoimmune question explicitly**, and answer it clinically rather than serologically.
6. **If autoimmune encephalitis is plausible, start immunotherapy — do not wait for antibodies.**
   Antibody results take days to weeks; outcome tracks time to treatment.
7. **Look for the tumour**, and keep looking if the first screen is negative and suspicion persists.
8. **Only when the treatable causes are excluded** should the conversation turn to prion or
   degenerative disease.
9. **Prognosis and planning** — for prion disease especially, the trajectory is fast and families
   need honesty early.

## Management

!!! orderset "Treat the treatable — and treat empirically where waiting costs more than acting"
    - **Thiamine** — give it on suspicion of Wernicke, **before glucose**, parenterally, at the
      high-dose regimen your institution uses for suspected Wernicke rather than a maintenance dose.
      *Doses are institution-protocol dependent and are not quoted here; use your local Wernicke
      protocol.*
    - **Aciclovir empirically** for suspected HSV encephalitis while PCR is pending. **The cost of
      empiric treatment is trivial; the cost of delay is not.**
    - **Autoimmune encephalitis — first-line immunotherapy started on clinical grounds:**
      high-dose corticosteroids, IVIG, and/or plasma exchange, alone or in combination, per your
      neuroimmunology pathway. **Escalate to second-line (rituximab, cyclophosphamide) if the
      response is inadequate** — early escalation is associated with better outcomes.
      *Regimens are not dosed on this page because they were not label-verified at drafting; use
      your institutional protocol or the current consensus guidance.*
    - **Remove the tumour** where a paraneoplastic cause is found — in ovarian teratoma-associated
      NMDAR encephalitis, resection is part of the treatment, not just the oncology.
    - **Treat the infection, the metabolic derangement, or the drug** — obvious, and still missed.
    - **Non-convulsive status** — treat as [status epilepticus](../seizure/index.md).

!!! warning "Avoid"
    - **Waiting for antibody results before starting immunotherapy** in a plausible case. This is
      the single commonest management error in this presentation.
    - **Anchoring on CJD.** It ends the search prematurely, and the search is where the treatable
      patients are found.
    - **Attributing a first psychiatric presentation with neurological signs to psychiatry** without
      excluding autoimmune encephalitis.
    - **Glucose before thiamine** in a malnourished patient.
    - **Ordering 14-3-3 as the prion test.** It lacks specificity; **RT-QuIC is the test.**
    - **Brain biopsy before the non-invasive treatable work-up is complete.**

!!! tip "TRICARE — rapidly progressive dementia"
    - **This is an inpatient problem, and the inpatient benefit covers it.** Do not delay MRI, LP,
      EEG or empiric treatment for an authorization.
    - **The specialty items to anticipate** are the **autoimmune and paraneoplastic antibody
      panels** (send-out, expensive, and frequently queried), **CSF RT-QuIC** (sent to a specialist
      prion laboratory), **whole-body FDG-PET** for occult malignancy, and **IVIG or plasma
      exchange** — which run through the **medical benefit** and, in the outpatient continuation
      phase, need authorization.
    - **Second-line immunotherapy** (rituximab, cyclophosphamide) is high-cost, off-label for most
      autoimmune encephalitis indications, and should be argued as **off-label medical necessity
      with literature support**, not filed as a formulary request.
    - **Verify current coverage at the [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst)
      and with the regional contractor before counselling — DoD P&T revises criteria without
      notice.** *Not independently verified for this page.*

!!! military "Military medicine — rapidly progressive dementia"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 1 and Vol 2**,
    **DoDI 1332.18**, and applicable force-health-protection guidance. See
    [Profiles, MEB/MAR2 & deployability](../military/deployability.md).*

    - **This is the one page in this section that is genuinely an active-duty problem.** Autoimmune
      encephalitis and CNS infection are **diseases of young adults**, and a service member with a
      subacute psychiatric-then-neurological presentation is a realistic scenario in a way that
      Alzheimer's disease is not.
    - **The military-specific risk is a psychiatric admission.** A young service member with new
      psychosis, agitation or behavioural change is very likely to reach behavioural health before
      neurology. **Autoimmune encephalitis presenting to a military behavioural health service and
      treated as a primary psychiatric illness is the failure mode this page exists to prevent.**
      Seizures, movement disorder, autonomic instability, hyponatraemia, or a course that does not
      behave like psychiatry should trigger neurology and an LP.
    - **Disposition follows the cause and the residual deficit, not the presentation.** Do not apply
      the dementia standard reflexively — **many of these patients recover substantially.** If the
      cause is treated and deficit resolves, the endpoint may be **full return to duty**; the
      retention question arises only from **residual impairment at MRDP**, under **AR 40-501 3-31k**
      closing on **para 3-1**. **If a true dementia is established, DoDI 6130.03 Vol 2 5.26.d acts
      upon diagnosis** — but establish it first.
    - **Profile:** **temporary** through the diagnostic work-up, treatment and recovery — which can
      run many months. Link every extension; **do not close it at hospital discharge**, and expect
      the **12-month administrative MRDP** to become the driver in a slow recoverer.
    - **EPTS / LOD:** onset is datable here, unlike the degenerative dementias, which makes LOD
      relatively straightforward. **Document the exposure or trigger where one exists**, and, in
      infectious cases, whether it was deployment-associated — that determination has downstream
      consequences.
    - **Deployability: non-deployable** during work-up, treatment and recovery. **Reassess against
      documented cognitive recovery, seizure freedom and medication requirement**, not against
      discharge from hospital.
    - **Aeromedical:** grounded; **USAAMA** adjudicates, and a seizure or a residual cognitive
      deficit will each independently keep an aircrew member grounded.
    - **The CBRN and outbreak angle** — a **cluster** of subacute encephalopathy in a deployed or
      garrison population is a public health event, not a series of individual cases. Notify
      preventive medicine and the public health emergency officer, as for
      [botulism](../nmj/botulism.md).

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

**Speed changes the differential completely.** A dementia that takes years is almost always
degenerative; a dementia that takes weeks is a different problem with a different question. The whole
value of separating rapidly progressive dementia as a category is that it forces the clinician to
stop pattern-matching against Alzheimer's disease and start a systematic hunt — because a meaningful
fraction of these patients have something that can be treated, and the treatment window is measured
in weeks.

**Autoimmune encephalitis reorganised this field twice.** First by revealing that a large group of
patients previously labelled psychiatric, or written off as viral encephalitis of unknown cause, had
identifiable antibody-mediated disease. Then by establishing that **outcome depends on time to
immunotherapy** — which means the correct clinical behaviour is to **treat on clinical suspicion and
let the antibody result arrive later.** That is uncomfortable, and it is right.

**And prion diagnosis finally became reliable.** For years the CSF test in use was **14-3-3**, a
non-specific marker of neuronal injury that generated both false alarm and false reassurance.
**RT-QuIC detects the misfolded prion protein itself**, and its specificity transformed a diagnosis
that had previously rested on pattern recognition and time. The clinical consequence is not
therapeutic — there is still no treatment — but a confident, earlier diagnosis spares families a
futile investigative marathon and lets planning begin while it can still involve the patient.

**Key sources:** consensus criteria for autoimmune encephalitis; CSF RT-QuIC validation literature
for sporadic CJD; the rapidly progressive dementia differential literature.
