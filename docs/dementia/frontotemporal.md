# Frontotemporal dementia

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: August 2026</span>

*Part of [Approach to cognitive impairment](index.md).*

**One-liner:** a group of **young-onset** degenerative syndromes that begin with **behaviour,
personality or language** rather than memory — frequently misdiagnosed as psychiatric illness for
years, frequently **inherited**, and the dementia most likely to present in someone still working.

**Who this is for:** the provider seeing a **45–65-year-old** whose personality has changed, whose
job performance has collapsed, or whose speech is failing — and who has already been through
psychiatry. In a military population this is **the one degenerative dementia that can genuinely
present on active duty.**

!!! danger "🚩 The pattern that should stop you calling it psychiatric"
    | Finding | Why it matters |
    |---|---|
    | **Personality change in a 50-year-old with no psychiatric history** | New-onset primary psychiatric illness in mid-life is uncommon. **Late-onset "depression" that is really apathy, or "mania" that is really disinhibition, is bvFTD until proven otherwise** |
    | **Loss of empathy, socially inappropriate behaviour, loss of insight** | Not a mood disorder. The family, not the patient, brings the complaint |
    | **New compulsive or ritualised behaviour; dietary change, especially carbohydrate craving** | Highly characteristic and rarely volunteered — ask |
    | **Progressive language failure with preserved memory** | Primary progressive aphasia |
    | **Any of the above + fasciculations, weakness, dysphagia** | **FTD–ALS overlap.** Look for it deliberately; it changes prognosis and counselling entirely |
    | **A parent or sibling with young-onset dementia, ALS, or "early Alzheimer's"** | Up to a third of FTD is autosomal dominant — **take a three-generation family history** |

    **The commonest error is years of psychiatric treatment before anyone images the brain.** In a
    mid-life patient with new behavioural change and declining function, **imaging is not
    over-investigation.**

## Diagnosis

**Behavioural-variant FTD (bvFTD)** — the commonest form. Diagnosis rests on **early and progressive
change in behaviour or personality**, with features drawn from: **disinhibition**, **apathy or
inertia**, **loss of sympathy or empathy**, **perseverative, stereotyped or compulsive behaviour**,
**hyperorality and dietary change**, and a **dysexecutive neuropsychological profile with relative
sparing of memory and visuospatial function.** Supportive imaging shows **frontal and/or anterior
temporal atrophy or hypometabolism.**

**Primary progressive aphasia (PPA)** — language is the earliest and most prominent deficit:

| Variant | Core deficit | Typical pathology |
|---|---|---|
| **Nonfluent/agrammatic** | Effortful, halting speech; agrammatism; apraxia of speech | Usually FTD (tau) |
| **Semantic** | Loss of word *meaning*; impaired naming and single-word comprehension; surface dyslexia | Usually FTD (TDP-43) |
| **Logopenic** | Word-finding pauses, impaired sentence repetition | **Usually Alzheimer's disease** — see [AD](alzheimers.md) |

**The logopenic caveat matters:** a PPA syndrome does not mean FTD pathology. If the variant is
logopenic, the underlying disease is most often AD, and that changes both prognosis and whether
amyloid biomarkers are worth pursuing.

**FTD–ALS overlap:** motor neuron disease develops in a meaningful minority of FTD patients, and
cognitive/behavioural change occurs in a substantial proportion of ALS patients. **Examine for
fasciculations, wasting, weakness, brisk reflexes and bulbar signs at every visit** — the finding
transforms the conversation about prognosis and about advance planning.

!!! note "Genetics — ask, then decide whether to test"
    A large minority of FTD is **autosomal dominant**, with three genes accounting for most of it:
    **C9orf72** (also the commonest genetic cause of ALS, and the classic FTD–ALS link), **MAPT**
    (tau) and **GRN** (progranulin).

    - **Take a three-generation family history**, and ask specifically about ALS and about
      psychiatric illness — affected relatives are often mislabelled.
    - **Genetic testing is a counselling event, not a lab test.** It has implications for adult
      children, for insurance, and — in a service member — potentially for career. **Refer to
      genetic counselling before testing**, not after the result.

**What supports the diagnosis:** **MRI** showing frontal and/or anterior temporal atrophy, often
asymmetric; **FDG-PET** showing frontotemporal hypometabolism, which is particularly useful when MRI
is equivocal and when the question is **FTD versus AD**; and **neuropsychological testing** showing
a dysexecutive or language-led profile with relatively preserved episodic memory. **Amyloid
biomarkers are useful mainly to argue *against* AD** in an ambiguous case.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Take the collateral history.** In bvFTD the patient's account is not merely unreliable — the
   loss of insight is a diagnostic feature.
2. **Image early.** MRI brain; add **FDG-PET** if the clinical picture is ambiguous or the MRI is
   unremarkable.
3. **Examine for motor neuron signs** — and repeat it at follow-up.
4. **Neuropsychological testing** to establish the profile and a baseline.
5. **Take a three-generation family history**; refer to **genetic counselling** if the pattern
   suggests inheritance.
6. **Stop the drugs that will not help and may harm** — cholinesterase inhibitors, antipsychotics
   where avoidable.
7. **Treat the behaviour that most endangers the patient or the family**, non-pharmacologically
   first, then an SSRI.
8. **Address safety early and bluntly** — driving, firearms, finances, and the patient's capacity to
   make major decisions, which is often already impaired at diagnosis.
9. **Support the caregiver.** Caregiver burden in bvFTD exceeds that in AD at equivalent stages,
   because behaviour is harder to live with than forgetfulness.
10. **Military disposition** — and this is the dementia most likely to arise on active duty.

## Management

**There is no disease-modifying treatment for FTD.** Everything below is symptomatic, and the most
valuable interventions are non-pharmacological.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — non-pharmacological, and deprescribing"
    - **Identify triggers and restructure the environment.** Behaviour in bvFTD is often
      stimulus-driven and routine-dependent; changing the environment works better than changing the
      neurochemistry.
    - **Caregiver education is the intervention.** Families need to hear that the behaviour is the
      disease, not a choice and not a personality flaw — that reframing changes outcomes.
    - **Stop cholinesterase inhibitors if someone has started one.** They are **not indicated in
      FTD**, do not help, and **may worsen behavioural symptoms** — a common inherited error from an
      assumed Alzheimer's diagnosis.
    - **Memantine is not indicated in FTD** and has not shown benefit.
    - **Review every sedating and anticholinergic drug.**

!!! orderset "Step 2 — if medication is needed for behaviour"
    - **SSRIs are the usual first choice** for disinhibition, compulsive and repetitive behaviours,
      overeating and irritability. **Sertraline** is the pragmatic default; citalopram carries
      dose-dependent **QT prolongation**. All are **off-label for FTD**.
    - **Trazodone** is used for agitation and sleep, also off-label.
    - **Antipsychotics: avoid where possible.** The **boxed warning for increased mortality in
      elderly patients with dementia-related psychosis** applies, and **patients with FTD may be
      unusually sensitive to extrapyramidal effects.** If genuinely required, use the lowest dose of
      an atypical for the shortest time, with a documented review date.
    - **Do not treat apathy with a stimulant reflexively** — the evidence is thin and the
      cardiovascular and behavioural risks are real.

!!! warning "Avoid"
    - **Cholinesterase inhibitors** — no benefit, possible worsening.
    - **Memantine** — no demonstrated benefit in FTD.
    - **Antipsychotics as a default for behaviour** — boxed warning, extrapyramidal sensitivity.
    - **Benzodiazepines** — disinhibition can worsen.
    - **Assuming a PPA syndrome is FTD** — the logopenic variant is usually Alzheimer's disease.
    - **Genetic testing without counselling**, particularly in a service member.

!!! tip "TRICARE — FTD prescribing"
    - **There is no disease-modifying therapy to authorize**, and the symptomatic agents — SSRIs,
      trazodone — are **generic and formulary.** The prescribing side of this page is inexpensive.
    - **The cost and coverage questions here are diagnostic and supportive**, not pharmaceutical:
      **FDG-PET**, **neuropsychological testing**, **genetic testing and counselling**, speech and
      language therapy for PPA, and — the largest by far — **long-term care and caregiver support**
      for a patient who may be decades from retirement age.
    - **Young-onset disease creates benefit problems that older-onset disease does not** — dependent
      children, a working spouse, and an active-duty or recently separated status that determines
      which system the family lands in. **Involve case management early**; this is the single most
      useful thing an MTF can do for these families.
    - **Verify current coverage at the
      [TRICARE Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst)
      and with the regional contractor.** *Not independently verified for this page.*

!!! military "Military medicine — frontotemporal dementia"
    *Framework — verify against current **AR 40-501**, **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 1 and Vol 2**,
    **DoDI 1332.18**, and service aeromedical policy. See
    [Profiles, MEB/MAR2 & deployability](../military/deployability.md).*

    - **This is the dementia that can actually present on active duty**, and it is the reason this
      section is not purely a beneficiary topic. Peak onset spans the **late 40s to early 60s** —
      squarely within a senior NCO or field-grade officer career.
    - **The realistic presentation is a disciplinary or performance problem, not a medical one.**
      Uncharacteristic misconduct, financial irresponsibility, inappropriate remarks, apathy read as
      laziness, or a sudden collapse in evaluation reports. **A previously exemplary senior Soldier
      whose conduct changes in mid-life deserves a neurological thought before an administrative
      one.** By the time the diagnosis is made, the record may already contain adverse actions that
      the disease caused.
    - **Retention: disqualifying upon diagnosis** — **DoDI 6130.03 Vol 2 para 5.26.d** carries
      *"Paragraph 5.26.a. does not apply"*; Army hook **AR 40-501 3-31k** closing on **para 3-1**.
      **No MRDP to wait out.**
    - **Profile:** **S** or **P** factor depending on whether the presentation is behavioural or
      neurologic — state which and why. Section 4 limits must address **weapons access, driving,
      independent duty, financial responsibility and supervisory authority.** In bvFTD, **judgment
      and impulse control are impaired before cognition tests abnormal**, which is precisely the
      combination that makes weapons access and command responsibility unsafe early.
    - **EPTS / LOD:** genuinely contested here. Symptoms precede diagnosis by **years**, and the
      earliest evidence is usually in **evaluation reports, counselling statements and financial or
      disciplinary records** rather than in medicine. Review them and date the first attributable
      change. **In a familial case, ask directly about family history** — autosomal dominant disease
      raises real pre-service questions, and **genetic testing in a service member should follow
      counselling and a considered decision, not curiosity.**
    - **MEB / DES:** refer on diagnosis. **The documentation problem specific to FTD is that the
      standard cognitive screens can be normal** while the patient is grossly impaired —
      **neuropsychological testing with an executive and behavioural battery, plus collateral and
      command corroboration, is what carries the board.** A MoCA of 27 does not exclude bvFTD, and a
      board reading only the MoCA will get it wrong.
    - **Where misconduct has already occurred**, the interaction between the medical diagnosis and
      any administrative or UCMJ action is a **legal and command question, not a medical one** —
      document the neurological findings and their timeline factually and promptly, and let the
      appropriate authorities weigh them. **Do not editorialise about culpability in the medical
      record.**
    - **Deployability: non-deployable.** Impaired judgment, disinhibition and weapons access are
      disqualifying independent of any memory deficit.
    - **Aeromedical: disqualifying** pending **USAAMA** adjudication; likewise **ATC and UAS.**

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

**FTD is the dementia that arrives disguised as a character change.** Because the earliest damage is
to the frontal and anterior temporal regions that govern behaviour, empathy and language rather than
memory, the first people to notice are a spouse, a supervisor or a bank — not a doctor, and not the
patient, whose loss of insight is itself part of the syndrome. The result is a diagnostic delay
measured in years, frequently spent in psychiatric care, and often accompanied by damage to
relationships, finances and careers that a timely diagnosis would have limited.

**It is also the dementia with the most genetics.** A large minority is autosomal dominant, and
**C9orf72** links it to ALS — which is why the motor examination belongs in every FTD visit and why
the family history is a clinical instrument rather than a formality. That inheritance turns the
diagnosis into a family event, which is exactly why testing belongs downstream of counselling.

**And it is the dementia where treatment has least to offer and management has most.** There is no
disease-modifying therapy, cholinesterase inhibitors do not help and may harm, and antipsychotics
carry real risk. What does change outcomes is unglamorous: naming the disease early, restructuring
the environment, coaching the family to stop attributing the behaviour to choice, protecting the
patient's finances and safety while there is still something to protect, and supporting a caregiver
whose burden is measurably heavier than in Alzheimer's disease.

**Key sources:** international consensus criteria for behavioural-variant FTD and for the primary
progressive aphasias; FTD genetics literature for C9orf72, MAPT and GRN.
