# Acute ischemic stroke

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: July 2026</span>

**One-liner:** sudden focal neurologic deficit from arterial occlusion — **"time is brain."** Every
minute of an untreated large-vessel occlusion loses ~1.9 million neurons (Saver's oft-cited
MCA-territory estimate). Your job on the code is to move
fast, in order: **last-known-well → glucose → CT → blood pressure → reperfusion decision.**

> *The ER consult usually sounds like:* "Older patient, weak on one side, started a couple hours
> ago — they're in the scanner now, no NIHSS yet, no labs." Your value is imposing the checklist on
> the chaos. Be fast and a little abrupt; it saves brain.

!!! danger "🚩 The clock is the whole game — establish these first"
    - **Last known well (LKW), not "time found."** The entire reperfusion algorithm keys off this
      number. Nail it from the patient, family, EMS, or phone records.
    - **Check a glucose immediately** — hypo/hyperglycemia is the great stroke mimic. It is the
      **only mandatory lab** before thrombolysis.
    - **Get the patient to non-contrast CT now** (+ CTA head/neck if no contraindication) — you are
      excluding hemorrhage and finding a large-vessel occlusion.
    - **Don't over-treat blood pressure.** In a possible reperfusion candidate the target is
      **<185/110**; otherwise leave it alone (permissive hypertension) unless **>220/120**.
    - **Mimics:** hypoglycemia, seizure/Todd's, complex migraine, Bell's palsy, functional,
      toxic-metabolic. But **a focal deficit is a stroke until proven otherwise** — don't stall the
      code chasing a mimic.

## Acute stroke checklist — what I want done, in order

!!! orderset "Code stroke — the sequence"
    1. **LKW + glucose** — the two numbers that gate everything.
    2. **Focused exam + NIHSS** (compact scale below). Note anticoagulant/antiplatelet use.
    3. **STAT non-contrast CT head; CTA head & neck** (skip CTA only if true contrast contraindication
       — most kidneys tolerate it, don't wait on a creatinine in a code).
    4. **Labs** (do not delay reperfusion for routine results **unless history or examination raises
       concern for anticoagulant exposure, thrombocytopenia, or coagulopathy**): CBC/platelets, PT/INR/aPTT, BMP, troponin, type
       & screen, hCG if applicable; **ECG + telemetry**.
    5. **Two large-bore IVs.** Manage **BP to <185/110** *only if* pursuing lytic (labetalol 10–20 mg
       IV or nicardipine gtt 5–15 mg/h).
    6. **Reperfusion decision** — thrombolysis? thrombectomy? both? (below). **Consent in parallel,
       don't let it delay the bolus.**
    7. **Weigh the patient** (dosing is weight-based; don't guess high).
    8. **If LVO:** activate neuro-interventional / arrange transfer **immediately** — in parallel with
       giving lytic ("drip and ship").

!!! orderset "Time goalposts (AHA/ASA *Target: Stroke*)"
    Beat these clocks — every step is a race:

    | Milestone | Goal |
    |---|---|
    | Door → stroke team / physician | ≤10–15 min |
    | **Door → CT initiated** | **≤25 min** |
    | Door → CT read | ≤45 min |
    | **Door → needle (thrombolysis)** | **≤60 min (standard); ≤45 min aspirational** |
    | Door → arterial puncture (EVT) | ≤90 min (direct arrivals) |
    | Door-in–door-out (transfer for EVT) | ≤60 min |

    *The published guideline standard is **door-to-needle ≤60 min**, with **≤45 min** aspirational;
    some centers push a ≤30-min institutional goal, but that is not the guideline benchmark.*

### Compact NIHSS (rapid field version)

A fast, practical aid — not a substitute for the formal 15-item NIHSS you'll document.

| Domain | Quick check |
|---|---|
| **1a–1c LOC** | Alert / drowsy / stupor; month + age; blink eyes + squeeze hand |
| **2 Gaze** | Eyes midline, partial gaze palsy, or forced deviation |
| **3 Visual fields** | Full / partial / complete / bilateral hemianopia |
| **4 Facial palsy** | Symmetric / minor / partial / complete |
| **5–6 Motor arm/leg** | Arm at 90° (10 s), leg at 30° (5 s): drift → falls → no movement, each limb |
| **7 Ataxia** | Finger-nose, heel-shin — out of proportion to weakness |
| **8 Sensory** | Pinprick: normal / mild / severe loss |
| **9 Language** | Aphasia: none / mild / severe / mute-global |
| **10 Dysarthria** | Normal / slurred / unintelligible |
| **11 Extinction/neglect** | None / one modality / profound |

*NIHSS ≤3 = "minor"; ≥6 with cortical signs (gaze, aphasia, neglect) predicts a **large-vessel
occlusion** — get the CTA and call for thrombectomy.*

## Imaging & the reperfusion fork

- **Non-contrast CT:** rules out hemorrhage; look for early ischemic change / ASPECTS.
- **CTA head & neck:** finds the **LVO** (ICA, M1/M2, basilar) and any tandem/dissection.
- **Advanced imaging:** perfusion imaging or MRI mismatch helps select extended-window lysis and
  some EVT candidates. **It is not mandatory for every 6–24-hour thrombectomy candidate** under
  current pathways; contact the receiving stroke team and do not delay transfer to obtain a study
  they do not need.

Two independent decisions, made in parallel — **a patient can get both:**

## Reperfusion — Step 1: IV thrombolysis

!!! orderset "IV thrombolysis — offer within 4.5 h of LKW (Class 1)"
    **Two agents, co-equal Class 1 within 4.5 h (2026 AHA/ASA); tenecteplase is the practical default:**

    - **Tenecteplase (TNK) 0.25 mg/kg IV, single bolus (max 25 mg)** — one push, no infusion, no
      line to babysit; easier for transfers. **Preferred where available.**
    - **Alteplase (tPA) 0.9 mg/kg IV (max 90 mg)** — **10% as a bolus over 1 min, remainder over
      60 min.**

    !!! warning "Label vs. guideline — a 3-hour nuance for tenecteplase"
        The FDA approved **TNKase (tenecteplase) for AIS on 3 Mar 2025.** The *indication* itself is
        broad — "acute ischemic stroke in adults," **no time window stated** — but the label's
        **Recommended Dosage section says to "initiate treatment as soon as possible and within
        3 hours after onset."** So using TNK in the **3–4.5 h** window is **guideline-supported
        (2026 AHA/ASA, Class 1) but beyond the labeled dosing timing** — fine to do, just know the
        distinction and document it. (Alteplase is similar: ≤3 h labeled, 3–4.5 h by guideline/
        evidence.) The agents are clinically co-equal across 4.5 h; the labeling nuance is a
        timing footnote, not a reason to prefer one drug.

    **Before the bolus:** verify hemorrhage-excluding imaging, glucose, BP **<185/110**, eligibility/
    bleeding-risk checklist, and weight-based dose. **Obtain ECG and troponin in parallel; neither
    should delay eligible reperfusion.**

    !!! danger "Absolute contraindications (do NOT give lytic)"
        - **Hemorrhage on CT**, or extensive early infarct >⅓ MCA territory.
        - **BP that won't come below 185/110** despite treatment.
        - **Glucose <50** and deficit resolves with correction.
        - **Platelets <100k, INR >1.7, elevated aPTT / therapeutic heparin ≤48 h, or a DOAC ≤48 h**
          (unless specific reversal/normal assay).
        - **Ischemic stroke, intracranial/spinal surgery, or serious head trauma within 3 months;**
          known intracranial neoplasm (intra-axial), AVM, or aneurysm at risk; **active internal
          bleeding**; infective endocarditis; suspected **aortic dissection**.

    !!! note "Historically 'absolute,' now RELATIVE (weigh case-by-case — the 2026 guideline liberalized these)"
        Rapidly-improving or mild-but-disabling deficits, **seizure at onset** (if deficit is from
        the stroke, not the postictal state), major surgery <14 d, GI/GU bleed <21 d, recent MI,
        pregnancy/early postpartum, recent arterial puncture. **Don't reflexively exclude — discuss
        with stroke/neuro.**

    **The "million-dollar presentation" (say this to consent/activate):** *"\_\_-year-old with
    \[risk factors] presents with acute \[deficit/syndrome], LKW \[time]. BP \_\_, NIHSS \_\_, glucose
    \_\_, INR \_\_, platelets \_\_. CT shows \[no bleed], CTA shows \[occlusion/patent]. No lytic
    contraindication."*

    !!! quote "Consent, in plain language (benefit is at 90 days, not at the bedside)"
        Thrombolysis is the best chance of returning to independence after stroke — roughly a **~10%
        absolute increase in good outcome at 3 months.** The risk is bleeding: symptomatic
        intracranial hemorrhage — historically **~6% with alteplase** (NINDS definition) vs ~1%
        without, though **contemporary series (and the stricter ECASS-III definition) run ~2–3%,
        including with tenecteplase.** *Part of that "6% → 2–3%" gap is definitional, not purely the
        drug.* **Overall mortality is not increased.** The benefit is not seen immediately — it shows
        up over the following weeks.

!!! orderset "Extended-window thrombolysis — call stroke neurology"
    Advanced imaging can identify patients eligible beyond the standard window. The time anchor, vessel, imaging criteria and access to thrombectomy determine the protocol.

    - **Unknown onset/wake-up:** MRI DWI–FLAIR mismatch supports treatment in selected patients within **4.5 h of symptom recognition**. Perfusion selection is a separate pathway for **4.5–9 h from last known well**, or **within 9 h of the midpoint of sleep** for wake-up stroke, under local protocol.
    - **4.5–24 h with LVO and unavailable EVT:** TRACE-III supports tenecteplase in a selected population with salvageable tissue. Continue to pursue transfer and use an approved stroke-team pathway.
    - **4.5–24 h without LVO:** OPTION found greater excellent outcome (44% vs 34%) but more symptomatic ICH (2.8% vs 0%) in perfusion-selected patients. This 2026 trial is not a blanket indication for late TNK. Discuss eligibility and hemorrhage risk with the stroke team. [OPTION trial](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.0210)
    - **4.5–24 h with immediately available EVT:** avoid routine late bridging lysis. TNK-PLUS in selected anterior LVO did not improve functional independence, and ATTENTION LATE in basilar occlusion did not improve its primary functional outcome. Neither trial changes early-window lysis for otherwise eligible patients. [TNK-PLUS](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.4292) · [ATTENTION LATE](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2026.15496)

!!! warning "Recent asundexian (Sunharbo) exposure"
    Ask specifically about this newly approved FXIa inhibitor. The label states that exposure in the **48 h before thrombolysis has not been studied** and bleeding risk is unknown. Obtain urgent stroke-team advice rather than applying the ordinary antiplatelet pathway. The label advises **withholding Sunharbo for at least 24 h after thrombolysis**. [Current prescribing information](https://labeling.bayerhealthcare.com/html/products/pi/Sunharbo_PI.pdf).

## Reperfusion — Step 2: endovascular thrombectomy (LVO)

!!! orderset "Mechanical thrombectomy — for large-vessel occlusion; give lytic too if eligible"
    Thrombolysis and thrombectomy are **not either/or** — a lytic-eligible LVO patient gets **both**
    (don't wait to see if the lytic works). Activate neuro-IR / transfer the moment CTA shows an LVO.

    **Current 2026 AHA/ASA selection anchors:**

    - **ICA/M1, ≤6 h:** Class I with NIHSS ≥6, prestroke mRS 0–1 and **ASPECTS 3–10**.
    - **ICA/M1, 6–24 h:** Class I for selected patients with ASPECTS ≥6; the guideline also recommends EVT for selected **ASPECTS 3–5** patients who are **<80**, have NIHSS ≥6, prestroke mRS 0–1 and no significant mass effect.
    - **ICA/M1, ASPECTS 0–2 within 6 h:** EVT is reasonable in selected patients under the guideline’s additional criteria. Seek immediate neuro-IR review rather than excluding from transfer solely by the score.
    - **Basilar occlusion, ≤24 h:** Class I when baseline mRS 0–1, NIHSS ≥10 and PC-ASPECTS ≥6. Lower NIHSS and other exclusions require individualized assessment.

    These anchors do not replace the full eligibility assessment. DAWN and DEFUSE-3 remain landmark evidence, but the 2019 16–24 h Class IIa restriction is superseded. [2026 AHA/ASA guideline, sections 4.7.2–4.7.3](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000513).

## After reperfusion — the post-lytic order set

!!! orderset "Post-thrombolysis orders (first 24 h)"
    - **ICU/stroke-unit monitoring ≥24 h.** Neuro checks + **BP q15 min ×2 h → q30 min ×6 h → q1 h**
      to complete 24 h.
    - **Keep BP <180/105 ×24 h** (labetalol pushes ± nicardipine gtt).
    - **Hold all antithrombotics ×24 h** — no aspirin/clopidogrel/heparin/prophylactic anticoagulation.
    - **Avoid for 24 h:** central lines, arterial sticks, NG tube, urinary catheter, LP (or any
      non-compressible puncture).
    - **A statin is fine to start** (high-intensity — see secondary prevention).
    - **Repeat non-contrast CT at 24 h** before starting antithrombotics. **Any acute
      decline → STAT CT** (think symptomatic hemorrhage).

!!! danger "Symptomatic ICH after thrombolysis — stop and reverse"
    Sudden decline, new headache, vomiting, or BP spike within 24 h:

    1. **STOP the lytic infusion.** **STAT non-contrast CT.**
    2. **Labs:** CBC/platelets, PT/INR/aPTT, **fibrinogen**, type & cross; repeat q2–4 h until stable.
    3. **Cryoprecipitate 10 units IV** (replaces fibrinogen — first-line); recheck fibrinogen, **goal
       >150 mg/dL**, repeat as needed.
    4. **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV** (or aminocaproic acid) as antifibrinolytic.
    5. **Platelets** if thrombocytopenic; correct any coagulopathy.
    6. **Neurosurgery** for possible decompression/EVD; treat elevated ICP; frequent neuro checks.

## Blood pressure — the rules change by pathway

| Situation | Target | How |
|---|---|---|
| **Before thrombolysis** | **<185/110** | Labetalol 10–20 mg IV, or nicardipine 5–15 mg/h gtt |
| **After thrombolysis (24 h)** | **<180/105** | Same; nicardipine/clevidipine gtt if labile |
| **Post-thrombectomy, successful reperfusion** | Individualized, usually **≤180**; **do NOT drive SBP <140** | Over-lowering a reperfused brain harms — see warning |
| **No reperfusion (permissive HTN)** | **Treat only if >220/120**, then lower ~15% | Restart home meds ~24–48 h once stable |

!!! warning "Don't over-lower after a successful thrombectomy"
    The **2025 ACC/AHA hypertension guideline** carries a **Class 3: Harm** recommendation **against
    lowering SBP <140 mmHg in the first 24–72 h after successful EVT reperfusion** — intensive
    lowering worsened outcomes. Keep it controlled (≤180) but **don't chase a low number.** For an
    *incompletely* reperfused vessel, avoid over-lowering to preserve collateral flow.

## Not a reperfusion candidate? Start antiplatelet therapy

!!! orderset "Antiplatelet — after 24 h if lysed, or now if not lysed (and CT shows no bleed)"
    - **Aspirin 160–325 mg** within 24–48 h (rectal/NG if dysphagic) — the one proven early drug for
      everyone not getting a lytic.
    - **Minor stroke (NIHSS ≤3) or high-risk TIA (ABCD² ≥4): short-course DAPT.** Start **aspirin +
      clopidogrel** (clopidogrel 300–600 mg load → 75 mg/day; ASA 81 mg) for **21 days**, then
      **single-agent** — the benefit is concentrated in the first 21 days (CHANCE/POINT); longer
      DAPT just adds bleeding.
        - **Ticagrelor + aspirin** (THALES) is an alternative, useful with **CYP2C19 loss-of-function**
          (clopidogrel non-responders).

## Secondary prevention — start it before discharge

Secondary prevention is **owned on this admission** and continued in clinic. The **full
mechanism-by-mechanism playbook lives on the [Stroke prevention page](prevention.md)** — the
essentials to start in-hospital:

!!! orderset "Start before discharge (every patient)"
    - **High-intensity statin — atorvastatin 80 mg** (or rosuvastatin 20–40); **target LDL <55 mg/dL for very-high-risk ASCVD, otherwise <70 mg/dL**
      (add ezetimibe ± PCSK9). Start in hospital.
    - **Antithrombotic matched to mechanism** — single antiplatelet for most; **21-day DAPT** for
      minor stroke/high-risk TIA (see above); **DOAC for AF, started early (≤4 days, CATALYST)**;
      never reflexively combine antiplatelet + anticoagulant.
    - **BP toward <130/80** once stable; **diabetes** (GLP-1/SGLT2i), **smoking cessation**, diet/
      exercise, weight.
    - **Determine the mechanism** (cardioembolic / large-artery / small-vessel / ESUS) — it drives
      carotid revascularization, PFO closure <60, and prolonged rhythm monitoring. → full detail on
      the [prevention page](prevention.md).
    - Inpatient basics: **dysphagia screen before anything PO**, DVT prophylaxis, early mobilization.

## TIA — a treated emergency, not "a pass"

A TIA is a transient deficit **without infarction** but with a high, front-loaded early stroke risk —
**same-day work-up and immediately-started prevention.** Full detail (ABCD², the work-up order set,
21-day DAPT, mechanism-directed therapy) is on the **[dedicated TIA page](tia.md).** In brief:
**Urgent brain + vessel imaging and rhythm assessment → mechanism/risk assessment → indicated
antiplatelet/DAPT + statin → disposition through a reliable rapid pathway.** ABCD² alone cannot
identify who may safely skip urgent evaluation. Don't send a real TIA home without a plan.

## Supportive care & admission

!!! orderset "Stroke-unit admission bundle"
    - **Dysphagia screen before any PO** (meds, food, water) — aspiration is a top early killer.
    - **Glucose:** avoid hypoglycemia and treat hyperglycemia — the 2026 guideline doesn't set a
      stroke-specific number; the general inpatient **140–180** target is reasonable (and SHINE
      showed no benefit from tight <110 control). Treat fever (worsens outcome); **normoxia**
      (O₂ only if <94%).
    - **DVT prophylaxis** — **intermittent pneumatic compression** immediately; pharmacologic
      prophylaxis once bleeding risk allows (and ≥24 h post-lytic).
    - **Early mobilization + therapy** (PT/OT/SLP); swallow eval; secondary-prevention teaching.
    - **Telemetry ≥24–48 h** hunting for AF.

## Disposition

Stroke-unit or ICU (all post-lytic/post-thrombectomy). Transfer any **LVO** to a thrombectomy-capable
center emergently. Admit TIAs or observe with an **expedited same-day work-up** — the early recurrence
risk is real.

!!! military "Military medicine — duty, retention and deployment"
    - **Profile / function:** Restrict hazardous duties while evaluating a new vascular event; document residual vision, strength, language, cognition, recurrence risk and bleeding risk.
    - **Retention / MEB–MAR2:** DoDI V2 **5.26.b** and Army **3-31f** guide cerebrovascular retention assessment. Workup for a clotting disorder alone does not establish a permanent disability. Use the [retention framework](../military/deployability.md); permanent P3/4 does not by itself select DES versus MAR2.
    - **Deployment / medications:** Therapeutic anticoagulation is a major CENTCOM exclusion; nonaspirin antiplatelets create a separate waiver issue. Do not stop indicated treatment to make a member deployable. See [the disease AND drug screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the current CENTCOM rules and waiver packet.
    - **Special duty:** flight, diving and other special assignments have separate standards and waiver authorities; a general duty profile does not clear them.
    - **LOD / EPTS:** document onset, duty status, exposures and aggravation objectively. The designated authority determines LOD/EPTS; neither in-service diagnosis nor a genetic finding settles it automatically.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

- **The whole field is time + vessel.** Two parallel decisions: **thrombolysis** (drug, ≤4.5 h or
  perfusion-selected later) and **thrombectomy** (large-vessel occlusion, out to 24 h). Eligible
  patients get **both.**
- **Tenecteplase has arrived** (with a label caveat). The **2026 AHA/ASA guideline** makes **TNK
  0.25 mg/kg** and **alteplase 0.9 mg/kg** co-equal Class 1 within 4.5 h; TNK's single bolus is
  operationally simpler. Note the **FDA label approves TNK only to 3 h** — 3–4.5 h is guideline-based
  but off-label. **RAISE (2024)** found more excellent outcomes with reteplase than alteplase. Symptomatic ICH estimates were imprecise, and **any ICH was more frequent (7.7% vs 4.9%)**. It does not justify claiming equal bleeding safety or replacing the local approved lysis pathway. [Primary trial](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2400314).
- **Late thrombolysis depends on the population.** TRACE-III enrolled LVO with limited access to EVT. OPTION enrolled non-LVO with salvageable tissue and found more excellent outcomes alongside more symptomatic hemorrhage. TNK-PLUS and ATTENTION LATE found no functional benefit from routine late bridging at EVT-capable centers. Use the applicable pathway and involve stroke neurology.

- **Large-core thrombectomy:** SELECT2, ANGEL-ASPECT and TENSION support treatment in selected large-core populations. **TESLA missed its primary efficacy threshold**, and should not be listed as independently positive. Use the 2026 selection framework and involve the thrombectomy team promptly. [TESLA primary report](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.13933).

- **Short DAPT, not long.** For minor stroke / high-risk TIA, **21 days** of aspirin+clopidogrel
  (CHANCE/POINT) beats aspirin alone; beyond 21 days you only add bleeding. **THALES** offers
  ticagrelor+ASA.
- **Anticoagulate AF early — it reduces recurrence.** **CATALYST** (Lancet 2025; pooling ELAN/OPTIMAS/
  TIMING) shows **early DOAC ≤4 days lowers** recurrent ischemic stroke/sICH at 30 days **with no
  excess sICH** — early is now preferred, not just tolerated. The 1-3-6-12 wait is out.
- **Post-EVT, don't over-lower BP.** The **2025 ACC/AHA hypertension guideline** flags **SBP <140 in
  the first 24–72 h after successful reperfusion as Class 3: Harm.**
- **Chase LDL <70** with a high-intensity statin (± ezetimibe/PCSK9); treat the **mechanism**, not
  just "a stroke."
