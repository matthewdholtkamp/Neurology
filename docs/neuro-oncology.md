# Neuro-oncology emergencies

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: November 2026</span>

**One-liner:** In a patient with cancer and a new neurologic deficit, decide which clock is running: stroke, seizure, pressure, spinal cord compression, infection, or treatment toxicity.

!!! danger "Early calls that change the outcome"
    | Presentation | Route |
    |---|---|
    | Sudden deficit or abrupt severe headache | Activate [stroke or hemorrhage evaluation](stroke/index.md). Check anticoagulants, platelets, procedures and recent treatment in parallel. |
    | Declining consciousness, new pupil asymmetry or signs of obstructive hydrocephalus | Emergency CT, critical care and neurosurgery. Use the [raised ICP pathway](neurocritical-care/increased-icp.md). |
    | Cancer plus back pain and new weakness, sensory level, gait decline or bladder/bowel dysfunction | Treat as suspected metastatic spinal cord compression. Contact spine/neurosurgery and oncology immediately. Arrange urgent whole-spine MRI. |
    | New weakness, dysphagia, ptosis, dyspnea or chest symptoms after immune checkpoint inhibition | Consider myasthenia, myositis and myocarditis overlap. Withhold further checkpoint inhibitor pending urgent oncology/neurology assessment. Check airway protection, vital capacity and inspiratory force serially as feasible. Obtain ECG, troponin and CK and evaluate for all three toxicities. Bulbar or respiratory involvement needs hospital and possible ICU care. |

## What to do in order

!!! orderset "Step 1 — Stabilize and identify the lesion"
    - Check onset, neurologic baseline, glucose, vitals and airway. Review cancer type and distribution, recent radiation/operation, systemic drugs, immune status, antithrombotics and platelet count. Assess fever or neutropenia through the local emergency pathway.
    - Use emergent CT for acute deterioration, suspected blood, hydrocephalus or when speed is critical. Obtain contrast-enhanced brain MRI when safe and indicated to characterize a suspected tumor or treatment effect. Discuss contrast and protocol with radiology.
    - Treat active seizure through the [status pathway](seizure/index.md). Consider EEG when recovery is delayed. For ongoing treatment after a tumor-related seizure, review drug interactions with oncology/pharmacy. Routine prophylactic antiseizure medication is generally unnecessary in seizure-free adults with brain metastases.
    - For new focal findings or suspected raised ICP, defer LP until safe and appropriate. If leptomeningeal disease is suspected, coordinate imaging and CSF studies with the treating team.
    - **Suspected checkpoint inhibitor neuromuscular toxicity:** seek an immediate specialist treatment plan. ICI-related MG may require IVIG or plasma exchange with corticosteroids and other syndrome-specific treatment. ICI-related GBS can also require corticosteroids alongside IVIG or exchange. The [classic GBS recommendation](presentation/acute-weakness.md) against corticosteroids applies to that separate clinical setting. Do not wait for antibody results to escalate a deteriorating patient.

!!! orderset "Step 2 — Symptomatic brain metastasis with edema"
    - For adults with symptoms due to metastatic mass effect, **dexamethasone 4–8 mg/day** is a guideline-supported starting range for mild symptoms. Severe symptoms consistent with increased ICP may require **16 mg/day or higher** with specialist guidance. Choose route and regimen by acuity and ability to take oral medication.
    - Impending herniation or obstructive hydrocephalus requires emergency management and neurosurgical assessment. Steroid therapy alone cannot relieve a mechanical obstruction quickly enough.
    - Use the lowest effective dose, monitor glucose and other toxicities, and agree on a taper with oncology or neurosurgery. If primary CNS lymphoma is suspected and the patient is stable, discuss biopsy timing before steroids because they can compromise diagnostic yield. Life-threatening mass effect takes priority.

!!! orderset "Step 3 — Suspected metastatic spinal cord compression"
    - Call the spine and oncology services urgently. Minimize movement when spinal instability is suspected and follow the transfer protocol. Arrange whole-spine MRI **as soon as possible and within 24 hours** for suspected compression with neurologic signs/symptoms. Transfer if timely imaging or definitive care is unavailable.
    - With neurologic symptoms or signs of metastatic spinal cord compression, NICE recommends **dexamethasone 16 mg PO or equivalent parenteral dose promptly**, then **16 mg daily** while awaiting surgery or radiotherapy. Coordinate taper after definitive treatment. Stop if compression is excluded and no other indication exists, with specialist advice if exposure has been prolonged. Monitor blood glucose and address gastric protection per protocol.
    - If lymphoma or myeloma is suspected radiologically and there are no neurologic symptoms or signs, seek hematology advice before steroids. The urgent steroid pathway above is for neurologic compromise.

## Disposition

Admit or transfer with progressive deficits, significant edema, hydrocephalus, suspected compression, persistent altered consciousness or serious suspected treatment toxicity. Document diagnostic alternatives, steroid dose and timing, prior imaging, ability to walk, and bladder function. Coordinate definitive treatment and goals of care early.

!!! tip "TRICARE — urgent care, treatment site and continuity"
    Stabilize suspected emergencies before elective authorization. For ongoing treatment check exact drug in the [Formulary Search](https://www.express-scripts.com/frontend/open-enrollment/tricare/fst), and confirm imaging, infusion or procedure routing in the [TRICARE guide](military/tricare.md).

!!! military "Military medicine"
    Document tumor diagnosis, treatment, recurrence risk, function, and duty context for EPTS/LOD. Use the [profile, retention and MEB/IDES guide](military/deployability.md) and [deployment medication screen](military/deployment-medications.md) with oncology. Continue specialty treatment while fitness and availability are assessed.

## Discover

The ASCO/SNO endorsement treats steroids as short-term relief of symptomatic metastatic edema. Its steroid dose ranges differ from the NICE metastatic spinal cord compression regimen. Early consultation and imaging determine the next intervention.
