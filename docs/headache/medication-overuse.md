# Medication overuse headache

<span class="reviewed-stamp">Last reviewed: July 2026</span>

**One-liner:** in a patient with a **pre-existing primary headache**, frequent use of acute
headache medication drives headaches to **≥15 days/month** — both a complication and a
self-perpetuating engine. Recognizing it is half the treatment; the other half is **starting an
effective preventive while unwinding the overused drug.**

!!! danger "🚩 Don't diagnose MOH until you've excluded a new secondary headache"
    A patient whose headaches suddenly escalated may have a new process (mass, CVST, intracranial
    hypertension). If the **pattern changed** or there are any [red flags](index.md), work it up
    before attributing everything to medication overuse.

## Recognize the thresholds (ICHD-3: use over >3 months)

| Acute medication | Overuse threshold |
|---|---|
| Simple analgesics (NSAIDs, acetaminophen) | **≥15 days/month** |
| Triptans | **≥10 days/month** |
| Ergots | **≥10 days/month** |
| Opioids | **≥10 days/month** |
| Butalbital-containing combinations | **≥10 days/month** (worst offender) |
| Combination analgesics | **≥10 days/month** |
| **Ditans (lasmiditan)** | **≥10 days/month** — **NOT MOH-safe** (FDA label warning + preclinical data); count it like a triptan |

**Gepants have not demonstrated the established MOH pattern seen with triptans/opioids, but
they are not unlimited-use drugs.** FDA-label exposure limits still matter: safety beyond 8
ubrogepant/zavegepant-treated attacks or 18 rimegepant doses in 30 days is not established.
Atogepant is a daily **preventive**, not an acute rescue. Frequent attacks warrant prevention
review and an individualized rescue plan. Lasmiditan carries an MOH warning.

## Rapid approach

1. **Count acute-med days** at every headache visit — the screening question people forget.
2. **Exclude a new secondary headache** if the pattern changed.
3. **Start an effective preventive** — *don't wait for withdrawal to finish.*
4. **Address the overused agent** — stop or cap it, with a plan matched to the drug class.
5. **Bridge** the withdrawal window and **educate** — MOH recurs without a durable behavior change
   and a capped acute plan.
6. **Follow up** at a defined interval to confirm improvement.

## The prescriptive flow (what I want done, in order)

1. **Confirm MOH** (threshold table) and **rule out a new secondary headache** if the pattern changed.
2. **Start a preventive now** — pick by comorbidity, or go straight to a **CGRP therapy**, which
   works *even with ongoing overuse* (Step 1).
3. **Convert the acute plan:** stop the abrupt-stoppable culprits, **taper** the ones that need it,
   and hand the patient a **capped** rescue plan — **prefer a gepant** (Step 2).
4. **Bridge** the first few weeks (Step 3).
5. **Educate + set a follow-up** (diary, written acute-day cap, return visit).
6. **Severe (opioid/barbiturate) or refractory:** structured withdrawal / infusion program.

## Management

!!! note "Why the combined approach works — MOH is a nociplastic pain state"
    MOH is increasingly understood as a **nociplastic pain condition**: **central sensitization,
    impaired descending pain modulation, and reward/cognitive-control-circuit dysfunction** maintain
    the cycle — and these changes are **largely reversible** with a preventive + reduced overuse.
    **Behavioral features (craving, catastrophizing, cephalalgiophobia) predict poor outcomes**, which
    is exactly why education and follow-up sit alongside the drugs.

!!! orderset "Step 1 — start an effective preventive (do this first, don't wait)"
    - **Pick a migraine preventive by comorbidity** — topiramate, amitriptyline/nortriptyline,
      propranolol/metoprolol, candesartan, valproate (see the
      [migraine preventive ladder](migraine.md#preventive-treatment-the-ladder) for doses/titration).
    - **CGRP-targeted therapies (mAbs *and* gepants) work even while the patient is still overusing**
      and have transformed MOH care — reasonable **early**, and often the cleanest path. As migraine
      frequency falls, patients **naturally reduce** acute-med days, so **forced abrupt withdrawal is
      frequently unnecessary** when a CGRP mAb is on board. The dedicated phase-4 MOH trials now prove
      this:
        - **Erenumab 140 mg** (JAMA Neurol 2024, the first phase-4 RCT designed for CM-MOH): **69% MOH
          remission at 6 months vs 53% placebo** (OR 2.01), **without mandatory withdrawal.**
        - **Eptinezumab 100 mg IV + brief education** (RESOLUTION, Neurology 2026, n=608): **~3.2 more
          monthly migraine days reduced** than placebo (p<0.0001), sustained through 24 weeks.
    - **You don't have to withdraw first (MOTS):** in the largest strategy RCT (n=720), a preventive
      **without** switching/restricting the overused drug was **non-inferior** to switching for
      headache frequency — though **switching produced more MOH-free patients.** So: **start the
      preventive; withdrawal helps but isn't mandatory** (VA/DoD 2023 concurs).
    - **Combination beats any single lever (2025 network meta-analysis, 16 RCTs):** preventive +
      withdrawal ± nerve block was most effective; all preventive classes (oral, CGRP, Botox) were
      similarly effective; **abrupt withdrawal *alone* was not.**
    - **OnabotulinumtoxinA** reduces headache days in CM-MOH (~−1.9 vs placebo) but **adds no benefit
      over withdrawal alone** (Pijpers 2019), and **CGRP mAbs show larger effect sizes** in this
      population — so reach for a **CGRP first** where you can.
    - On TRICARE, the frictionless CGRP is **galcanezumab (Emgality) — preferred, no PA** (see box).

!!! orderset "Step 2 — convert the acute plan (stop or cap the culprit)"
    | Overused class | How to stop | Why |
    |---|---|---|
    | **NSAIDs, triptans, ergots** | **Can stop abruptly** | No dangerous withdrawal syndrome |
    | **Opioids** | **Taper** | Withdrawal symptoms; relapse risk |
    | **Butalbital / barbiturates** | **Taper (careful)** | Abrupt stop risks **seizures** |
    | **Benzodiazepine co-use** | **Taper** | Withdrawal seizures |

    - Replace with a **capped rescue plan: a non-overused acute agent limited to ≤2 days/week.**
    - **Prefer a gepant** (ubrogepant, rimegepant, or **zavegepant nasal** — useful with nausea/
      vomiting during withdrawal) for rescue — lower observed MOH concern, **with label dose/exposure limits**. Rimegepant also doubles as a
      preventive. **Do not swap in lasmiditan as a "safe" alternative — ditans carry MOH risk.**

!!! orderset "Step 3 — bridge the withdrawal window (2–4 weeks)"
    Symptoms flare transiently as the overused drug comes off. Any of:

    - **Scheduled NSAID:** e.g., **naproxen 500 mg BID × 2–4 weeks** (if not the overused agent),
      with an **antiemetic** (metoclopramide or prochlorperazine) as needed.
    - **Greater occipital nerve block** — fast, opioid-sparing, pregnancy-safe.
    - **Prednisone taper** (e.g., **60 mg tapering over ~6 days**) — **evidence is mixed** (some
      negative RCTs); reasonable in selected patients, not routine.
    - **DHE** (per the [ER/status protocol](migraine.md#order-set-status-migrainosus-er)) for a
      severe withdrawal flare.

!!! warning "Avoid — and taper existing use"
    **Opioids and butalbital** are the **highest-risk culprits and the least effective** acute
    drugs. Don't start them; **prioritize tapering** patients already on them. They are also the
    classes most likely to require a structured withdrawal program.

Ubrelvy/Qulipta have PA or claims-history rules; Nurtec is nonformulary with separate PA/MN requirements; Zavzpret is nonformulary with PA. ## Disposition

Outpatient for most, with **clear written instructions, a capped acute plan, a diary, and a defined
follow-up.** A minority with severe overuse (**opioid/barbiturate**) or refractory disease benefit
from a **structured withdrawal / infusion program** (inpatient or day-hospital).

!!! warning "Relapse is common (~30% at 1 year) — plan follow-up around the predictors"
    MOH relapses in **~30% at 1 year** (up to ~40% in some cohorts). **Highest-risk:** **opioid or
    combination-analgesic overuse** (worst prognosis), **resuming the causative drug** after
    withdrawal, high baseline headache frequency, poor sleep, and an **underlying tension-type
    headache** (vs migraine). Counter it with **structured follow-up, a written acute-day cap, and
    behavioral support** — and be most vigilant in the opioid/butalbital group.

!!! military "Military medicine — duty, retention and deployment"
    - **Profile / function:** Record incapacitating headache days, abortive success, preventive trials and drug effects. Grounding or hazardous-duty restrictions depend on attacks and treatment effects.
    - **Retention / MEB–MAR2:** Headache: DoDI V2 **5.26.g** and Army **3-31g**; trigeminal neuralgia and other diagnoses need their applicable standard, rather than automatically borrowing the migraine threshold. Use the [retention framework](../military/deployability.md); permanent P3/4 does not by itself select DES versus MAR2.
    - **Deployment / medications:** CENTCOM disruptive migraine may require waiver; injectable CGRP therapy/Botox is separately relevant. Valproate, carbamazepine and lamotrigine are named medication exceptions even when used for pain/headache. See [the disease AND drug screen](../military/deployment-medications.md) for the current CENTCOM rules and waiver packet.
    - **Special duty:** flight, diving and other special assignments have separate standards and waiver authorities; a general duty profile does not clear them.
    - **LOD / EPTS:** document onset, duty status, exposures and aggravation objectively. The designated authority determines LOD/EPTS; neither in-service diagnosis nor a genetic finding settles it automatically.

## 📚 Discover — evidence brief

- **Both levers work, and combination is best.** A **2025 network meta-analysis** (16 RCTs) found
  **preventive + withdrawal ± nerve block** most effective; all preventive classes were similar; and
  **abrupt withdrawal *alone* was insufficient.**
- **You don't have to withdraw first.** **MOTS** (n=720): a preventive without switching the overused
  drug was **non-inferior** for headache frequency (switching yielded more MOH-free patients). The
  dedicated CGRP phase-4 trials seal it: **erenumab** (JAMA Neurol 2024) → **69% MOH remission at 6 mo
  without withdrawal**; **eptinezumab + education** (RESOLUTION, 2026) → sustained benefit through
  24 weeks. These trials do **not** make opioid/barbiturate withdrawal optional: dependence,
  withdrawal seizures, and safety require a separate supervised plan.
- **Gepants have lower observed MOH concern, but product-specific exposure limits still apply** — the preferred rescue. **Ditans (lasmiditan) are
  NOT MOH-safe** (FDA warning) — don't treat them like gepants.
- **OnabotulinumtoxinA** helps modestly but adds nothing over withdrawal alone; **CGRP mAbs are the
  stronger choice** in CM-MOH.
- **Opioids and butalbital are the highest-risk culprits** (and the worst for relapse) — avoid/taper.
- **MOH is a nociplastic, largely reversible pain state** — which is why **education and follow-up**
  (relapse ~30%/year) are core, not optional.
