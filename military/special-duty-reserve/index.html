    Aeromedical, Guard, and Reserve pathways¶

    Policy reviewed: November 2026

    A Soldier may need an immediate functional restriction, a separate aviation determination and a formal retention decision. Write down the decision being requested and send it to the authority that owns it.

    Aeromedical and other special duty¶

    1. Identify the role and standard. Record Service, flight class or special-duty category, whether this is initial qualification or continued duty, and the current waiver authority. For Army aviation, start with AR 40-501 chapters 4 and 6 and the current Army aeromedical policy letters. Other Services and dive programs have their own standards.
    2. Notify flight medicine promptly when a new neurologic condition or medicine may impair safe performance. Keep the member out of safety-sensitive tasks per local process pending the appropriate flight medicine disposition. Ask about sedating drugs, dose changes and last episodes of sudden incapacity.
    3. Send a useful clinical packet. Include the diagnosis and level of certainty, event dates, disorder-specific testing, objective residual findings, recurrence risk, previous therapies, current dose and side effects, recent medication changes, restrictions and follow-up plan.
    4. Use the official flight disposition. The flight surgeon coordinates Army review with the U.S. Army Aeromedical Activity through AERO as applicable. Verify condition-specific intervals and waiver eligibility in the current policy letter. Use the designated authority for return to flight, other special duty or waiver renewal.
    Presentation Add to the packet
    Seizure or blackout Provoked versus unprovoked, witness narrative, recurrence, ECG/EEG/imaging as relevant, and antiseizure regimen and adverse effects
    Migraine or vertigo Frequency, time to functional recovery, aura or vestibular features, medication use during duty, and impairment between attacks
    Diplopia, visual loss or TBI Functional acuity and fields, ocular alignment, symptom trajectory, balance and cognitive findings, and objective recovery

    Army School of Army Aviation Medicine · Army aeromedical standards reference · AERO

    Guard and Reserve¶

    1. Establish duty facts. Record the dates and type of orders or drill, onset or aggravation timeline, the contemporaneous clinical record, and any duty restrictions already in place. Ask the component to verify the actual status and whether LOD processing is needed. Treat or transfer urgent illness while administrative processing proceeds.
    2. Route the profile through the component. Army Reserve members should work through the unit medical readiness channel and AR-MMC. Army National Guard members should work through their unit/state medical readiness and State Surgeon channels. Confirm local submission requirements, routing and review responsibility before submitting. Civilian records should describe function, objective findings, expected recovery and what reassessment is needed.
    3. Ask which determination is pending. Use the applicable retention standard. A member who meets retention standards but cannot perform the MOS may need the applicable administrative review. For potentially unfitting conditions, ask the component disability office whether duty-related DES processing or a non-duty-related fitness determination applies. An LOD decision and a fitness decision serve different purposes.
    4. Confirm the care benefit separately. For potentially duty-related care, coordinate with the component and DHA military medical support office as appropriate. Coverage can depend on orders, eligibility and LOD authorization. Confirm the approved treatment episode before elective care.

    Do not promise a disability rating, compensation, care entitlement, return date, or civilian appointment authorization from a note or temporary profile. The component and designated authorities decide eligibility and disposition.
