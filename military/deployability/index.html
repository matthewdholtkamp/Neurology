    Military medicine — profiles, retention & deployability¶

    Policy review: 21 November 2026

    One-liner: write the functional restrictions today; separately decide retention, deployment, and special-duty clearance. A normal clinic examination does not answer all three questions, and a pharmacy approval answers none of them.

    The first five minutes¶

    1. Protect the member and unit. Restrict driving, weapons, heights, water operations, flight, or remote duty according to the actual deficit, risk of sudden incapacity, and medication effects. Document a review date and who will reassess.
    2. Use retention standards for someone already serving. DoDI 6130.03 Volume 2 and the member’s Service regulation apply. Volume 1 is for accession; do not import its exclusions into an established Soldier’s retention decision.
    3. Identify an immediate retention trigger. Under current DoDI V2 5.26.k, diagnosed epilepsy prompts medical/personnel action on diagnosis; 5.26.d likewise addresses permanent/progressive dementia. Do not defer these actions solely to finish an arbitrary treatment clock. Provoked seizures are addressed separately in 5.26.j.
    4. For other conditions, assess the actual governing standard. The general DoD neurologic functional/treatment framework in 5.26.a does not cancel a more specific or stricter Army standard. Record diagnosis, adequate treatment, trajectory, and duty effects. Seek the profiling/MEB physician’s help when standards intersect.
    5. Check the destination and every medication. Use the deployment and medication guide. A condition may meet retention standards while still requiring a theater waiver.

    Army: choose the correct pathway¶

    Finding Practical next step
    Temporary impairment with reasonable recovery expected Temporary profile with specific restrictions, linked extensions, treatment and reassessment plan
    Meets retention standards but permanent restrictions may prevent PMOS performance Consider MAR2, the administrative MOS-retention/reclassification process
    Fails retention standards at the medical retention determination point (MRDP), or another governing referral trigger applies Coordinate DES referral, using the appropriate permanent profile and approval-authority process
    Permanent PULHES 3/4 Requires the proper approval/review pathway; the number alone does not establish whether the member belongs in MAR2 or DES
    Guard/Reserve member Determine duty status, component-specific processing and any required LOD determination before promising the active-duty pathway or benefits

    MRDP is a decision point, not a requirement to wait a year. It may be reached earlier when further functional improvement is not expected. Twelve months on temporary profile for the same condition is an administrative threshold requiring disposition review under Army procedures. Do not keep restarting an unlinked profile to hide the clock, and do not wait for every coexisting condition to stabilize once a qualifying referral is due.

    The profiling provider describes the condition and restrictions; the designated approval authority reviews the permanent profile and referral. The MEB assesses whether retention standards are met; the PEB makes the formal fitness/disability determination. A treating clinician’s prediction is not a PEB finding. Use the installation’s current e-Profile/MEB process rather than telling a member that a particular signature guarantees separation.

    Common neurologic retention references¶

    These are starting references, not a substitute for reading the whole applicable standard. Check current Army publication status and any intervening policy before signing.

    Condition DoDI 6130.03 V2 Army reference / bedside implication
    Epilepsy 5.26.k AR 40-501 3-31i; coordinate action on diagnosis under current DoD policy. Do not cite 5.26.c for epilepsy
    Provoked seizures 5.26.j Distinguish an acute symptomatic event from epilepsy; document cause, recurrence, treatment and safety risk
    Migraine / other incapacitating headache 5.26.g 3-31g: ≥3 incapacitating days/month prompts neurologist evaluation; document preventive drug/dose/duration and response. Army provisions address a prophylaxis trial and direct referral when response is unlikely
    Stroke / recurrent TIA / vascular lesion 5.26.b 3-31f; residual function, mechanism, recurrence and treatment matter
    MS / chronic or recurrent demyelination 5.26.f 3-31e; use the specific Army standard rather than assuming good control cancels it
    TBI with residual impairment 5.26.h 3-31j; document cognition, balance, vision, headache and task performance after appropriate treatment
    Generalized myasthenia 5.26.l 3-31c; ocular-only disease is a specific exception in the standard, not blanket deployment clearance
    Permanent/progressive dementia 5.26.d 3-31k; initiate appropriate action on diagnosis. A potentially reversible encephalopathy requires diagnosis and functional reassessment
    Neuromuscular disease / neuropathy / paralysis 5.26.e / i, depending on diagnosis Apply the relevant disease and functional standard; do not force LEMS into a single exhaustive code
    Sleep disorders 5.27 3-32 and relevant 3-31h narcolepsy provisions; treatment response and daytime function matter
    Tremor / dystonia / syncope 5.26.m / n Frequency, function and sudden-incapacity risk drive restrictions; aviation has separate standards

    What to put in the note¶

    • Diagnosis and diagnostic confidence; date of onset and objective findings.
    • Treatments tried: drug, dose, dates, adherence, benefit, adverse effects, and why stopped.
    • Function: actual lost duty days, observed task limits, attacks, recovery time, and safety risks. “Doing well” is not an operational assessment.
    • Current restrictions, expected duration, recovery trajectory, follow-up and referral plan.
    • For deployment: destination, mission, medical support, supply/monitoring needs, and the specialist’s assessment for the waiver authority.
    • For LOD/EPTS: record facts and records supporting onset/aggravation. Diagnosis during service does not itself establish LOD; a genetic diagnosis does not itself establish EPTS. The designated authority makes that determination.

    Sources and boundaries¶

    • DoDI 6130.03 Volume 2, Change 2, 6 February 2026.
    • DoDI 6025.19 — Individual Medical Readiness, current 2026 change.
    • Army Publishing Directorate: AR 40-501 (27 June 2019), AR 40-502 (27 June 2019), DA Pam 40-502 (18 December 2023), and AR 635-40. Check the current controlled versions and local implementation; this review did not authenticate every internal MAR2 procedural step.
